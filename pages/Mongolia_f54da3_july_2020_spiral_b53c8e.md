## Introduction

In late July 2020, people across several western and northern Mongolian provinces recorded an unusual luminous spiral suspended in the night sky. Because the phenomenon was captured from multiple locations, it represents one of [Mongolia]({{ 'what-do-mongolias-ufo-reports-really/' | relative_url }})'s strongest documented unexplained-sky events in the sense that a genuine atmospheric or space-based display was widely observed rather than being a single ambiguous photograph. However, the available evidence does **not** support claims of an extraordinary craft. Instead, the balance of evidence points towards a high-altitude rocket exhaust plume illuminated by sunlight—a phenomenon that has produced remarkably similar spiral displays around the world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zmianynaziemi.pl/wiadomosc/jasny-spiralny-obiekt-zaobserwowany-w-mongolii" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zmianynaziemi.pl">[zmianynaziemi.pl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zmianynaziemi.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Jasny spiralny obiekt zaobserwowany w Mongolii &#124; zmianynaziemi.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 24, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 24, 2020</span></span></span>

## What the surviving videos can establish

The surviving footage provides several pieces of useful evidence even though it falls well short of a scientific dataset.

First, multiple recordings show the same distinctive structure: a bright central region surrounded by an expanding spiral or corkscrew pattern. The agreement between independent videos makes fabrication highly unlikely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.seniorennet.be/peter2011/archief.php?ID=2336830" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.seniorennet.be">[SeniorenNet Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.seniorennet.be</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SeniorenNet BlogUFO&#x27;S of UAP&#x27;S, ASTRONOMIE, RUIMTEVAART, ARCHEOLOGIE, OUDHEIDKUNDE, SF-SNUFJES EN ANDERE ESOTERISCHE WETENSCHAPPEN - DE A...</span></span></span>

Second, the feature appears to expand gradually rather than manoeuvre. There is no convincing evidence of abrupt acceleration, directional changes or controlled flight. Instead, the visible structure slowly grows larger while fading, behaviour consistent with an expanding cloud rather than a solid vehicle.

Third, the recordings lack the information needed to reconstruct the event precisely. Publicly available copies generally do not include:

## Why a high-altitude rocket plume fits the evidence

Among conventional explanations, a rocket upper-stage exhaust plume provides the closest match to what witnesses recorded.

After completing part of a mission, an upper stage may vent unused propellant or perform a controlled engine burn while slowly rotating for stability. In the near-vacuum of the upper atmosphere or space, the escaping gases expand rapidly into a huge cloud. If this cloud is illuminated by sunlight while observers on the ground are already in darkness, it can appear as a bright white or bluish spiral suspended against the night sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/d0f19d48010a2ccc26ccba31387d47d5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The images, taken by a camera near the Subaru telescope, showed a white orb evolving into a spiral before fading away. Several characteristics of the Mongolian event closely match this mechanism:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A symmetrical spiral centred on a bright core.
* Gradual outward expansion.
* No visible propulsion independent of the expanding cloud.
* Visibility across a very large region.
* Event duration measured in minutes rather than seconds.

</div>

These are all recognised characteristics of illuminated rocket exhaust rather than atmospheric plasma or an aircraft.

## Why the spiral shape appears

The spiral itself often causes confusion because it resembles something actively rotating through the atmosphere.

In reality, the visible spiral is usually produced by two separate processes acting together.

The rocket stage is already rotating slowly to maintain stability or prepare for disposal. When propellant is vented, the escaping gas leaves the vehicle in a continuous stream. Because the stage is spinning, the gas traces out a corkscrew pattern. As the cloud expands into the surrounding vacuum, the spiral becomes much larger and easier to see from the ground. Sunlight reflecting from frozen exhaust particles or condensed vapour makes the entire structure glow dramatically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/d0f19d48010a2ccc26ccba31387d47d5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The images, taken by a camera near the Subaru telescope, showed a white orb evolving into a spiral before fading away. This combination creates a striking visual illusion of a giant rotating object even though observers are actually seeing sunlight scattered from an expanding cloud many hundreds of kilometres above Earth.

## What remains uncertain

Although the rocket-plume explanation is the strongest available, some uncertainties remain because of limitations in the surviving evidence.

Publicly available reporting has not produced a complete reconstruction linking every witness video to a specific rocket operation using orbital calculations and precise timing. Nor have official Mongolian aviation or scientific agencies published a formal investigation of the event. ## What the incident tells us about Mongolia's UFO record

The July 2020 spiral is valuable because it illustrates the difference between an unexplained observation and an unexplained object.

The observation itself is well supported: numerous people across widely separated parts of Mongolia genuinely witnessed an unusual luminous display. That makes it one of the country's best-attested modern sky events.

The interpretation is different. The available imagery shows a high-altitude phenomenon whose appearance closely matches documented rocket exhaust spirals observed elsewhere in the world. No publicly available evidence demonstrates structured flight, intelligent manoeuvring, radar-confirmed anomalies or physical traces associated with the event.
