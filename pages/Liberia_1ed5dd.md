## Why Liberia’s record is so sparse

Several overlapping conditions can explain the absence of a detailed national case archive without invoking either a complete absence of sightings or deliberate suppression.

**No dedicated public investigation programme.** Liberia does not appear among countries known to have operated a formal UFO investigation body. Its Ministry of National Defense publishes general institutional information, while the Liberia Civil Aviation Authority concentrates on aviation regulation, accidents and incidents rather than maintaining a separate public UAP catalogue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lcaa.gov.lr/publications/document-type/accident-incident-data" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lcaa.gov.lr">[lcaa.gov.lr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lcaa.gov.lr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lcaa.gov.lr.</span></span></span>

**Fragmented archival survival.** Liberia experienced prolonged civil conflict between 1989 and 2003, during which public institutions, communications systems and military capabilities were severely disrupted. The former Liberian air arm became effectively inoperative and was formally dissolved during post-war demobilisation. Records from aviation, defence, police or provincial administrations may therefore be incomplete, undigitised or inaccessible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Liberia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Armed Forces of Liberia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Armed Forces of Liberia</span></span></span>

**Reporting infrastructure is concentrated around aviation centres.** Roberts International Airport, near Harbel in Margibi County, has historically been Liberia’s main international aviation facility. Any report made by a trained pilot or controller around these locations would potentially be more useful than an isolated rural recollection, because flight plans, weather information and traffic records could help identify conventional aircraft. ## Where reports would most plausibly cluster

Because there is no dependable dataset, any map of Liberian UFO activity would be speculative. Nevertheless, the country’s geography and infrastructure show where reports would be most likely to originate or be verifiable.

**Monrovia and Montserrado County** contain the largest population concentration, more artificial lighting, greater media access and Spriggs Payne Airport. This increases the number of potential observers but also the number of ordinary aerial objects: aircraft, helicopters, drones, illuminated clouds and bright celestial bodies seen through urban haze.

**Margibi County and the Robertsfield corridor** offer the strongest possibility of aviation-quality observations. Roberts International Airport has long handled international traffic, making landing lights, holding aircraft, high-altitude flights and airport activity routine sources of unfamiliar lights. It is also the region where air-traffic-control records would be most likely to exist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lcaa.gov.lr/publications/document-type/accident-incident-data" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lcaa.gov.lr">[Liberia Civil Aviation Authority]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lcaa.gov.lr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lcaa.gov.lr.</span></span></span>

**The Atlantic coast**, including areas around Buchanan, Greenville, Harper and Robertsport, provides broad horizons and comparatively dark views over the ocean. Distant ship lights, offshore weather, aircraft approaching from the sea, planets close to the horizon and atmospheric refraction can appear unusual. Without reliable range cues, observers may interpret a stationary or slowly moving light as being much closer and faster than it is.

**Interior counties** have darker skies but fewer cameras, radar sources and easily accessible contemporary archives. Reports from Lofa, Nimba, Grand Gedeh or Sinoe could therefore be visually impressive yet difficult to reconstruct after the event. A precise location, compass bearing and duration would be especially important in these areas.

These are reporting-environment observations, not identified Liberian UFO hotspots. There is currently no case count robust enough to demonstrate regional variation.

## Ordinary explanations that deserve priority

A UFO is simply an object or light that the observer cannot identify at the time. It is not, by definition, an extraterrestrial craft. In Liberia, several conventional categories should be checked before a case is described as anomalous.

**Aircraft and helicopters** are the first candidates near Monrovia, Robertsfield and established air routes. Landing lights can appear motionless when an aircraft is approaching directly towards an observer, then seem to move abruptly when it turns.

**Satellites and satellite trains** are increasingly important. Research has demonstrated that newly launched Starlink satellites and unusual sunlight reflections can produce convincing UAP reports, including reports from experienced commercial pilots. Reconstructing satellite positions from orbital data can resolve cases that appear highly unusual in video.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08155" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

**Balloons, birds and drones** routinely generate official UAP reports. The US All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office has published cases resolved through analysis as balloons or migratory birds, including an infrared report from Africa that was assessed with high confidence as a bird formation following known migration behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AAROM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on aaro.mil.</span></span></span>

**Meteors and fireballs** can cross a large part of the sky in seconds, fragment, change brightness or appear to descend nearby even when they are many kilometres away. A single observation without triangulation cannot establish altitude or landing location.

**Planets and atmospheric effects** are especially persuasive near a flat ocean horizon. Venus, Jupiter or a bright star viewed through moving cloud layers may seem to change colour or position. Humidity, haze and refraction can enlarge or distort distant lights.

Current official UAP analysis strongly supports beginning with these categories. ## What would make a Liberian case credible?

A valuable future report would need much more than a description of a “strange light”. The strongest evidence would combine independent witnesses with records that can be tested.

A serious report should include:

1. the exact date and time in Greenwich Mean Time, which Liberia uses;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

2. precise coordinates or an identifiable observation point;
3. compass direction, elevation above the horizon and duration;
4. the object’s apparent movement relative to stars, buildings or terrain;
5. original photographs or video files with metadata preserved;
6. statements recorded separately from each witness;
7. aircraft, helicopter and drone activity in the area;
8. satellite-pass, meteorological and astronomical checks;
9. any radar or air-traffic-control record;
10. a documented chain of custody for physical evidence.

</div>

Images alone are rarely decisive. Zoom, camera shake, compression and autofocus can turn a point of light into an apparent disc, triangle or pulsating object. A stronger investigation uses several sensor types, such as wide-field cameras, narrow-field imaging, radar, radio receivers, microphones and environmental instruments. This is the multimodal approach proposed by scientific UAP research projects because one sensor can reveal artefacts produced by another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

For Liberia, the most productive institutional route would be integration with ordinary aviation and safety reporting rather than the creation of a sensational “alien desk”. The Liberia Civil Aviation Authority could preserve reports from pilots, controllers and airport staff in a standardised form, while astronomical and meteorological checks could be performed before a case is labelled unresolved.

## The most defensible conclusion

Liberia’s UFO history is best described as undocumented rather than mysterious. No accessible case presently combines the elements required for a credible national incident: contemporaneous records, named witnesses, corroborating sensors and a transparent investigation. Publicly visible “Liberia UFO” pages are either empty, generic or concerned with international stories rather than events inside the country.

The absence of a strong archive leaves two possibilities open. Genuine observations may have occurred but gone unreported, been lost during institutional disruption or remained in undigitised aviation and newspaper collections. It is equally possible that Liberia has simply not generated any case unusual enough to survive careful scrutiny and attract sustained investigation.

Until stronger material appears, claims should be graded conservatively. A report with a known witness and date is evidence that somebody observed something. It is not evidence of extraordinary technology. An unexplained case means that available information is insufficient; it does not mean that every conventional explanation has been eliminated. For Liberia, that distinction is the central fact of the subject.
