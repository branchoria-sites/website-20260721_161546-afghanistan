## Introduction

[North Macedonia]({{ 'north-macedonia/' | relative_url }}) has a small but persistent catalogue of reported UFO sightings, yet none has reached the evidential standard of a scientifically verified unidentified aerial phenomenon. Unlike countries with military investigation programmes or extensive declassified archives, the country's most frequently cited cases survive mainly through witness reports, local media coverage and international UFO databases. This means that the key question is not whether unusual objects have been reported—they clearly have—but whether the available evidence is strong enough to exclude ordinary explanations.

Across roughly two decades of reports, a consistent pattern emerges. The best-known incidents include a 2006 triangular-light sighting near Skopje, several reports of multiple lights over the capital during the 2010s, and a well-publicised red-light incident over Bardovci in 2018. Each contains intriguing elements, but each also suffers from significant evidential limitations such as missing original recordings, lack of independent witnesses, absent radar data or plausible conventional explanations.

## The 2006 triangular-light report

The most frequently referenced North Macedonian UFO case is a report submitted to the National UFO Reporting Center (NUFORC) describing an event on 13 August 2006 near Skopje. According to the witness, a triangular orange-red object remained visible for approximately two to three minutes and was reportedly observed from locations about 18 kilometres apart. The report also claimed that television stations possessed photographs and video recordings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=52006" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 52006October 30, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 30, 2006</span></span></span>

Several aspects make this case noteworthy:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It is one of the earliest internationally archived reports from North Macedonia.
* The witness claimed multiple observers, with the database listing around one hundred people.
* The object was described as having a distinct triangular appearance rather than simply unusual lights.

</div>

However, the evidence remains weak despite these claims.

The television footage mentioned in the report has not entered any accessible archive, no named investigators published a technical analysis, and no publicly available radar records or official aviation data corroborate the event. Without the original imagery, it is impossible to determine whether observers saw a solid triangular craft, three separate lights interpreted as one object, or an optical illusion created by atmospheric conditions or camera limitations. The report therefore remains unresolved because of missing evidence rather than because it demonstrates extraordinary behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=52006" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 52006October 30, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 30, 2006</span></span></span>

## Why the Skopje wave attracted attention

Beginning in the early 2010s, reports from the Skopje region became the dominant feature of North Macedonia's UFO record. Rather than describing close encounters, witnesses generally reported distant luminous objects, often appearing as groups of lights moving together.

### Five lights over Skopje in 2016

One of the most widely reported incidents occurred in September 2016, when local television stations and online media circulated videos showing five bright lights travelling across the sky over Skopje. Witnesses described the objects as moving rapidly in a straight line while emitting a reddish glow. Some reports claimed they appeared larger than conventional aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telegraf.rs/english/2345892-ufo-visited-macedonia-five-mysterious-flying-objects-seen-last-night-over-skopje-video" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telegraf.rs">[Telegraf.rs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telegraf.rs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO VISITED MACEDONIA? Despite the publicity, several factors reduce the evidential value of the incident.

Most published articles repeated essentially the same eyewitness account rather than documenting multiple independently investigated observations. No authenticated original video with metadata became publicly available, and no aviation, astronomical or meteorological reconstruction appears to have been completed.

Several ordinary explanations remain plausible:

* aircraft flying similar routes;
* illuminated lanterns or balloons;
* coordinated drone flights;
* visual misperception caused by weather and distance.

Because the event occurred in 2016, the now-familiar explanation of Starlink satellite trains cannot apply, as the Starlink constellation had not yet been deployed. Nevertheless, the surviving evidence is insufficient to distinguish confidently between competing conventional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telegraf.rs/english/2345892-ufo-visited-macedonia-five-mysterious-flying-objects-seen-last-night-over-skopje-video" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telegraf.rs">[Telegraf.rs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telegraf.rs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO VISITED MACEDONIA? ### Earlier reports from the Skopje area

Media coverage surrounding the 2016 event also referred to previous sightings, including reports from 2012 and 2013 involving red lights or multiple luminous objects above the capital. These accounts are frequently repeated in regional UFO articles, but they rely almost entirely on secondary media summaries rather than preserved primary evidence. No publicly available photographs, original witness interviews or official investigations have survived that would allow detailed evaluation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telegraf.rs/english/2345892-ufo-visited-macedonia-five-mysterious-flying-objects-seen-last-night-over-skopje-video" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telegraf.rs">[Telegraf.rs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telegraf.rs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO VISITED MACEDONIA? As a result, these reports are historically interesting because they suggest recurring public interest in unusual aerial lights around Skopje, but they contribute little reliable evidence for extraordinary aerial phenomena.

## The Bardovci red-light sighting of 2018

Among modern North Macedonian reports, the December 2018 Bardovci incident is one of the better documented because it includes mobile-phone video and a more detailed narrative.

Witnesses reported a bright red object rising rapidly above the suburb of Bardovci while heavy snow was falling. According to the report, the light paused briefly before accelerating upward and disappearing. The recording was made from nearby Trno Dol. Witnesses also claimed that a similar object had appeared roughly two months earlier in the same area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telegraf.rs/amp/english/3017297-ufo-spotted-on-the-sky-above-skopje-macedonians-took-a-photo-of-the-mysterious-red-ball" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telegraf.rs">[Telegraf.rs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telegraf.rs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO spotted on the sky above Skopje: Macedonians took a photo of the mysterious red ball - Telegraf.rsDecember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 20, 2018</span></span></span>

Unlike many sensational UFO stories, the reporting itself contained a significant note of scepticism.

The originating publication suggested that the object was probably a drone, possibly one operating near the Kosovo border, rather than an extraterrestrial craft. Other commentators proposed possibilities including a meteor or another conventional aerial object. None of these explanations could be confirmed conclusively because the available footage lacks sufficient detail for trajectory, altitude or speed analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telegraf.rs/amp/english/3017297-ufo-spotted-on-the-sky-above-skopje-macedonians-took-a-photo-of-the-mysterious-red-ball" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telegraf.rs">[Telegraf.rs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telegraf.rs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO spotted on the sky above Skopje: Macedonians took a photo of the mysterious red ball - Telegraf.rsDecember 20, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 20, 2018</span></span></span>

The case illustrates an important distinction between "unidentified" and "unexplainable". Although the object was never positively identified, the available evidence is compatible with several ordinary explanations and does not require an extraordinary one.

## Reports beyond central Skopje

While Skopje dominates the country's UFO record, occasional reports have emerged from elsewhere.

Some regional media have mentioned sightings near Strumica and Ohrid, while UFO enthusiasts frequently cite historical traditions involving Štip and mountainous regions. However, these references are usually anecdotal, lacking preserved witness statements or verifiable documentation. Much of the material traces back to repeated media claims rather than independent investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telegraf.rs/english/2345892-ufo-visited-macedonia-five-mysterious-flying-objects-seen-last-night-over-skopje-video" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telegraf.rs">[Telegraf.rs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telegraf.rs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO VISITED MACEDONIA? A later NUFORC submission described a boomerang-shaped object observed over Butel Municipality during the August 2018 Perseid meteor shower. ## What later investigations suggest

Subsequent experience has shown how easily unfamiliar aerial objects can be misidentified. During 2020, observers across North Macedonia reported long chains of bright lights that initially generated UFO speculation before astronomers identified them as newly launched SpaceX Starlink satellites. That episode demonstrated the importance of astronomical verification before treating unusual lights as unexplained phenomena.

This lesson is relevant when reassessing earlier reports. Some historical cases pre-date Starlink and therefore cannot be explained that way, but the broader principle remains: without precise observational data, distinguishing between aircraft, satellites, drones, balloons, atmospheric effects and genuinely unidentified objects is extremely difficult.

For that reason, North Macedonia's most famous UFO incidents are best understood as examples of varying evidential quality rather than as a sequence of confirmed unexplained phenomena. The 2006 triangular sighting remains the country's best-known historical report, the 2016 Skopje lights represent its most widely publicised cluster, and the 2018 Bardovci incident offers its clearest surviving visual record.
