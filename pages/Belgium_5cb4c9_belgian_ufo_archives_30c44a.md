## Introduction

[Belgium]({{ 'what-really-happened-during-belgiums-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s reputation in UFO history rests not only on the famous 1989–1991 wave of sightings but also on an unusually durable civilian investigation network. Long after the most publicised incidents faded, volunteer researchers continued to collect witness accounts, preserve documents and publish case analyses. Their archives are among the most extensive national UFO collections in Europe, yet they are frequently misunderstood. A catalogue of reports is not the same as proof that the reported objects existed as described. Instead, these archives preserve testimony, investigation records and historical evidence that allows later researchers to reassess claims as new information emerges.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/en/home.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cobeps.org.</span></span></span>

Belgium therefore offers an instructive example of how a civilian research community can create a long-term historical record while remaining distinct from official government investigations. Understanding who built these archives—and how they should be interpreted—is essential to understanding the Belgian UFO story.

## How SOBEPS became Belgium's principal civilian investigator

The organisation that shaped Belgian UFO research more than any other was the **Société belge d'étude des phénomènes spatiaux (SOBEPS)**, founded in 1971 by Patrick Ferryn and Lucien Clerebaut. Unlike informal enthusiast clubs, SOBEPS presented itself as a research association attempting to investigate unidentified aerial reports using structured interviews, documentation and technical analysis rather than assuming extraterrestrial explanations from the outset.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/en/home.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cobeps.org.</span></span></span>

Its work expanded dramatically during the Belgian UFO wave beginning in November 1989. Hundreds of witnesses contacted the organisation directly, while police, journalists and members of the public increasingly regarded SOBEPS as the country's central clearing house for unexplained sightings. This created a remarkable archive containing:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* witness statements;
* investigator interview notes;
* sketches and photographs;
* correspondence with authorities;
* media reports;
* statistical summaries;
* follow-up investigations.

</div>

Because many witnesses reported similar-looking lights or triangular formations over different locations, SOBEPS attempted to compare reports chronologically and geographically instead of treating each case in isolation. This produced one of Europe's largest organised civilian UFO databases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/fr/publications-sobeps.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Publications Sobeps</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Publications Sobeps...</span></span></span>

### Cooperation with official institutions

One reason SOBEPS became influential was its willingness to cooperate with official bodies rather than position itself in opposition to them.

During the Belgian wave, the organisation exchanged information with the Belgian Air Force and incorporated publicly available military information into its analyses. This cooperation helped distinguish Belgium from countries where civilian researchers and official investigators rarely communicated. It also contributed to the widespread public perception that Belgian authorities treated the sightings more openly than many other governments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-be-sobeps-vague-ovni-volume-1-sobeps-vague-ovni-belge-volume-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl be sobeps vague ovni volume 1 sobeps vague ovni belge volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencySOBEPS Vague d&#x27;OVNI sur la Belgique, Volume 1 (1989–1991), SOBEPS (Société Belge d&#x27;Étude des Phénomènes Spatiaux) · 1991...</span></span></span>

That cooperation should not be overstated, however. The Air Force investigated matters relevant to aviation safety and air defence, while SOBEPS pursued broader civilian documentation. Their objectives overlapped but were not identical.

## The publications that preserved the Belgian wave

SOBEPS did more than collect reports. It transformed much of its archive into substantial reference works.

The two-volume **Vague d'OVNI sur la Belgique** remains the principal published source for the Belgian wave. Together the books compile witness testimony, timelines, maps, technical discussions, radar episodes, statistical summaries and competing interpretations assembled during and shortly after the events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/fr/publications-sobeps.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Publications Sobeps</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Publications Sobeps...</span></span></span>

These publications became influential because they preserved original material that otherwise might have disappeared as newspapers, police files and personal recollections were lost over time. Even researchers who disagree with SOBEPS' interpretations frequently rely on its catalogues because they remain the most comprehensive compilation of Belgian reports.

Importantly, the books distinguish between raw reports and subsequent interpretation. Readers therefore have access to many original witness descriptions even where later conclusions remain disputed.

## Why the archives survived after SOBEPS closed

After more than three decades of activity, SOBEPS voted to dissolve in 2007. According to its final president, Michel Bougard, organisational and practical considerations—not a resolution of the UFO question—led to the association's closure after thirty-six years of work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/en/dissolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cobeps.org.</span></span></span>

Rather than allowing decades of documentation to disappear, former members established the **Belgian Committee for the Study of Space Phenomena (COBEPS)**.

COBEPS deliberately describes itself as continuing part of SOBEPS' mission while adopting a simpler organisational structure. It no longer operates as a large membership association with a printed magazine and library. Instead, it concentrates on:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* collecting new Belgian sightings;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.ufouap.net/en/cases/belgian-ufo-wave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufouap.net">[ufouap.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufouap.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">belgian ufo wave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">belgian ufo wave</span></span></span>
* conducting field investigations;
* preserving earlier archives;
* publishing research articles;
* maintaining access to important historical material.

</div>

The organisation has also made many historical SOBEPS publications available digitally, significantly improving access for researchers and historians.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/en/home.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cobeps.org.</span></span></span>

### Continuity rather than replacement

Although COBEPS inherited much of SOBEPS' investigative tradition, it is not simply the same organisation under a different name.

The newer committee functions as a smaller volunteer network without the institutional scale that SOBEPS achieved during the Belgian wave. Nevertheless, it preserves continuity through shared investigators, shared historical records and the ongoing acceptance of new Belgian reports. Patrick Ferryn, one of SOBEPS' founders, remained involved in establishing this successor structure alongside physicist Léon Brenig and other long-time investigators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/en/home.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cobeps.org.</span></span></span>

## Reporting networks beyond Wallonia

Belgium's linguistic diversity also shaped its reporting landscape.

While SOBEPS and later COBEPS historically operated primarily in French-speaking Belgium, Dutch-language reporting networks developed to serve witnesses in Flanders. The Belgian UFO Reporting Centre (often referred to as UFO-Meldpunt) accepts contemporary reports and maintains its own catalogue of observations, ensuring that reports continue to be collected across both major language communities. This decentralised model reflects Belgium's broader institutional structure rather than disagreement over investigative methods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thetimes.co.uk/article/belgian-ufo-sighting-memorial-removed-at-familys-request-7mmv2bbqh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thetimes.co.uk">[The Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thetimes.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Times Belgian UFO sighting memorial removed at family&#x27;s request</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The marble stone, which had been restored earlier in 2025 to mark the 70th anniversary of the event, honored Vandercruyssen’s claim that...</span></span></span>

The existence of multiple reporting channels also reduces the risk that the historical record depends on a single organisation. Researchers can compare catalogues and assess where reports overlap or differ.

## How to read Belgian UFO catalogues without mistaking reports for proof

The greatest value of Belgium's civilian archives lies in preservation rather than verification.

A report demonstrates that someone claimed to observe something unusual at a particular place and time. It does **not** by itself establish:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* that the object existed as described;
* that witnesses interpreted it correctly;
* that independent reports necessarily referred to the same object;
* that an extraterrestrial explanation is justified.

</div>

This distinction is especially important because later developments altered the evidential value of some famous Belgian cases. The well-known Petit-Rechain triangular photograph, for example, was widely regarded for years as one of the strongest visual pieces of evidence from the Belgian wave before its creator admitted it had been fabricated. The report remains historically significant because it documents the evolution of public belief, but it no longer serves as reliable photographic evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/fr/publications-sobeps.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Publications Sobeps</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Publications Sobeps...</span></span></span>

Similarly, witness catalogues often preserve sincere observations that may later receive conventional explanations or remain unresolved because available information is insufficient.

## Why Belgium's archives remain valuable

Regardless of one's view of UFO claims, Belgium's civilian investigation network created an unusually rich historical record.

For historians, it documents how a national wave of sightings unfolded in real time. For psychologists and sociologists, it provides material for studying eyewitness testimony, media influence and public reporting behaviour. For investigators interested in unresolved aerial phenomena, it preserves original accounts that can be revisited using methods unavailable when they were first collected.

The archives therefore occupy an important middle ground. They neither prove extraordinary explanations nor reduce every report to error. Instead, they preserve the documentary record necessary for evidence-based re-examination—a role that has allowed Belgium's UFO investigations to remain relevant long after the original wave ended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cobeps.org/en/home.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cobeps.org">[cobeps.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cobeps.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cobeps.org.</span></span></span>
