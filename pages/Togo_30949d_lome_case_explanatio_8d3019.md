## Introduction

The alleged March 1974 encounter near Lomé remains one of Togo's best-known UFO reports, but it is also one of its weakest in terms of independent [evidence]({{ 'evidence-1074c8/' | relative_url }}). No contemporary police records, photographs, radar data, medical documentation or publicly verifiable testimony from the second witness have emerged. As a result, the case cannot be conclusively explained—but neither can it be treated as evidence for an extraordinary phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinsight.com/ufos/sightings/togo-beach-ufo-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinsight.com">[UFO Insight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinsight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO InsightThe Togo Beach UFO Incident – Paralysis, Blinding Lights, And Further Suggestions Of Underwater Bases - UFO InsightJune 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 2021</span></span></span>

A conventional assessment therefore asks a different question: not whether every reported detail has a single mundane cause, but whether ordinary physical, psychological and environmental mechanisms could account for important parts of the experience. Several well-established mechanisms fit significant elements of the account, particularly those involving perception over dark water, unusual coastal conditions, physiological stress and the reconstruction of memories reported years after the event. None of these explanations proves what happened, but together they offer a plausible framework that requires fewer assumptions than an unidentified advanced craft.

## Could lights over dark water have been misjudged?

Night-time observations across open water are notoriously difficult to interpret because the observer lacks reliable visual reference points. Without known distances or object sizes, even experienced witnesses can dramatically misjudge how far away a light is, how large it is and whether it is moving at all. Investigators of unidentified aerial phenomena have repeatedly noted that distance, speed and size become inseparable unknowns under these conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[skepticalinquirer.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UF O Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

In the Lomé account, the object was estimated to be roughly 200–300 metres offshore. That estimate, however, depended entirely on visual judgement in darkness. If the distance estimate was inaccurate, then every derived estimate—including the object's size, altitude and apparent behaviour—would also become uncertain.

Several ordinary sources could potentially resemble an unusual hovering object over the sea:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Fishing or commercial vessels displaying bright navigation or working lights.
* Shore-based lights reflected or distorted across the water.
* Atmospheric effects that cause lights near the horizon to shimmer, change colour or appear to move.
* Visual illusions produced by prolonged staring into darkness.

</div>

Importantly, proposing these possibilities does not identify the exact source. Rather, it illustrates that coastal night-time lighting frequently creates ambiguous observations without requiring an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[skepticalinquirer.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UF O Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

## Waves, vessels and changing coastal conditions

One striking feature of the report is the sudden rush of seawater that allegedly reached the witnesses while the object hovered offshore.

Although later UFO accounts often interpret this as evidence that the object disturbed the sea, ordinary coastal processes provide alternative possibilities. Beaches experience rapidly changing wave sets, particularly at night when visual cues are limited. Groups of larger waves can arrive after relatively calm intervals, giving the impression of a sudden surge. A witness concentrating on an unexpected light offshore may notice the incoming water only when it reaches them.

If an offshore vessel happened to be present, wake waves could also contribute to unusual surf conditions, although there is no surviving evidence confirming that any vessel occupied the reported location at the relevant time. Likewise, the witness's description of a "depression" in the sea surface cannot now be independently examined because no physical observations or photographs exist. The feature was estimated visually under poor lighting, making its shape and scale inherently uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinsight.com/ufos/sightings/togo-beach-ufo-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinsight.com">[UFO Insight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinsight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO InsightThe Togo Beach UFO Incident – Paralysis, Blinding Lights, And Further Suggestions Of Underwater Bases - UFO InsightJune 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 2021</span></span></span>

These explanations cannot recreate every detail of the narrative, but they demonstrate that unusual wave behaviour does not automatically imply an unknown physical force.

## Stress, paralysis sensations and intense heat

The reported inability to move has often been presented as one of the most mysterious elements of the encounter. Yet temporary immobility does not necessarily require an external force.

Psychologists recognise that sudden fear can produce a "freeze" response alongside the more familiar fight-or-flight reaction. During an unexpected threatening event, people may experience:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Difficulty initiating movement.
* Narrowed attention.
* Altered perception of time.
* Reduced awareness of surrounding details.
* Strong physiological reactions such as sweating and rapid heartbeat.

</div>

If the witnesses genuinely believed they were facing immediate danger from the sea or from an unidentified object, these reactions would not be unusual.

The reported sensation of intense heat is similarly difficult to interpret decades later. Physical exertion after swimming, emotional arousal, tropical nighttime humidity and stress-induced sweating could all contribute to a subjective feeling of overwhelming warmth without requiring an external heat source. Because no medical examination was documented immediately afterwards, there is no objective measurement against which these sensations can be tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinsight.com/ufos/sightings/togo-beach-ufo-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinsight.com">[UFO Insight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinsight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO InsightThe Togo Beach UFO Incident – Paralysis, Blinding Lights, And Further Suggestions Of Underwater Bases - UFO InsightJune 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 2021</span></span></span>

## How memory may have shaped the final account

One of the strongest conventional considerations concerns timing rather than physics.

According to the published history of the case, the witness did not provide a public account until roughly two years after the incident, when interviewed by French UFO researcher Joël Mesnard. That delay matters because human memory is reconstructive rather than photographic. Over months or years, people often retain the emotional core of an experience while unintentionally altering precise sequences, durations and visual details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinsight.com/ufos/sightings/togo-beach-ufo-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinsight.com">[UFO Insight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinsight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO InsightThe Togo Beach UFO Incident – Paralysis, Blinding Lights, And Further Suggestions Of Underwater Bases - UFO InsightJune 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 2021</span></span></span>

Research into eyewitness testimony and investigations of unusual aerial reports consistently shows that later recollections may incorporate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Improved narrative structure.
* Sharper apparent certainty.
* Estimated rather than remembered timings.
* Details inferred from later reflection.
* Accidental blending of genuine memories with later discussion or expectation.

</div>

This does not imply dishonesty. A witness may sincerely report events exactly as remembered while still recalling some elements inaccurately. The absence of a contemporary written statement or independent corroboration makes it impossible to separate original observation from later reconstruction in the Lomé case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[skepticalinquirer.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UF O Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

## Why no single conventional explanation solves every detail

Conventional explanations should not be understood as a claim that one ordinary event perfectly reproduces every reported feature.

Instead, sceptical investigators often favour a combination of mechanisms:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Ambiguous lights viewed across dark water.
* Difficulty estimating distance and movement.
* Natural wave activity or vessel wakes.
* Acute stress responses affecting perception.
* Memory changes during delayed reporting.

</div>

This layered approach is common in the study of unusual sighting reports because complex experiences often combine genuine observations with ordinary perceptual and psychological processes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[skepticalinquirer.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UF O Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

At the same time, the evidence is too limited to identify one definitive explanation. No surviving data allow investigators to determine weather conditions with sufficient precision, verify maritime traffic at the reported location or compare the anonymous witness's account against that of the unnamed companion.

## What the evidence supports today

The Lomé sighting remains genuinely unidentified in the narrow sense that there is insufficient evidence to determine exactly what the witnesses experienced. However, "unidentified" is not equivalent to "inexplicable."

The available record supports a cautious conclusion. Ordinary mechanisms—including misperception over dark water, changing coastal conditions, physiological stress and the normal reconstruction of memory over a two-year reporting delay—provide credible explanations for many of the reported features without requiring unknown technology or paranormal causes.
