## Introduction

[Albania]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-albanias-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s UFO literature is unusually dependent on memory, repetition and secondary reporting rather than surviving primary documentation. Unlike countries that produced extensive official UFO files, Albania has not released a comparable archive of military investigations, police reports or scientific assessments. As a result, many of the country's best-known cases have been reconstructed decades after the alleged events through newspaper features, television programmes, memoirs and internet compilations. The central question is therefore not whether unusual aerial phenomena were reported—they clearly were—but whether the stories can still be verified using contemporary evidence. In most cases, the answer is only partially, if at all.

This makes Albania an instructive case in how narratives evolve. Individual witness accounts may be genuine, yet repeated retellings frequently add dramatic details that cannot be traced back to original records. Separating documented facts from later embellishment is therefore more important than deciding whether any particular sighting involved extraordinary technology.

## Why Albania's documentary record is unusually thin

The weakness of Albania's UFO archive reflects both historical circumstances and archival practice rather than necessarily the absence of unusual observations.

During the communist period (1944–1991), military information, aviation incidents and security matters were tightly controlled by the state. Many records remained classified, while others may never have been preserved in publicly accessible collections. Even after the creation of the Albanian Authority responsible for opening former State Security (Sigurimi) files, historians have emphasised that questions of authenticity, completeness and missing documentation remain significant challenges when interpreting surviving files. Unlike Britain, where thousands of Ministry of Defence UFO reports are now catalogued and available for researchers, Albania has never released a comparable national collection of UFO investigations. That difference matters because countries with systematic reporting allow researchers to compare witness statements, official correspondence and later interpretations. Albania rarely offers that evidential chain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[The National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Memoirs, newspapers and intelligence references

Most Albanian UFO narratives survive through three principal channels rather than original investigative files.

### Memoirs and personal recollections

Some of the earliest accounts, including the frequently cited Mihal Grameno story from the early twentieth century, survive because they appeared in memoirs rather than official reports. Such material is historically valuable because it demonstrates that unusual aerial observations entered personal memory before the modern UFO era.

However, memoirs also introduce unavoidable limitations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* they are often written years after the events;
* descriptions may be influenced by later cultural ideas;
* technical observations are usually absent;
* independent corroboration is often impossible.

</div>

A memoir can establish that someone remembered an unusual light, but it rarely allows investigators to reconstruct altitude, direction, weather conditions or alternative explanations.

### Newspaper interviews

Many communist-era incidents entered public discussion only after the collapse of the regime. Journalists interviewed former soldiers, pilots or local residents decades after the alleged sightings.

This created an important historical record, but newspapers generally sought compelling narratives rather than forensic reconstruction. Basic investigative information—including original dates, exact locations, weather data, aircraft logs and maintenance records—was often missing.

Consequently, later writers frequently relied upon those same newspaper articles instead of locating earlier documentation, producing an expanding chain of citation without new evidence.

### Foreign intelligence references

Occasionally Albania appears within foreign intelligence or governmental collections concerning the worldwide "flying saucer" wave.

Such references are often misunderstood.

For example, Albania's appearance within collections of international UFO reporting demonstrates that reports circulated internationally, but not that foreign intelligence agencies confirmed the sightings. The FBI itself has repeatedly explained that documents within its files frequently consist of unverified reports, rumours or information received from outside sources rather than authenticated investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/news/stories/ufos-and-the-guy-hottel-memo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and the guy hottel memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs and the Guy Hottel Memo — FBIMarch 25, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2013</span></span></span>

## How repeated retellings change the stories

One of the clearest patterns in Albanian UFO literature is narrative expansion.

As stories pass from witness testimony to newspaper articles, then to television documentaries, websites and social media, additional details frequently appear without identifiable documentary origins.

Common examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* precise military responses that earlier versions never mention;
* technical failures allegedly affecting aircraft or vehicles;
* dramatic dialogue attributed to unnamed witnesses;
* claims that secret files "proved" extraterrestrial involvement;
* assertions that entire military units participated despite no identifiable operational records.

</div>

These additions often become accepted simply because they are repeated across multiple publications. Yet repetition alone does not create independent confirmation. If ten websites ultimately rely on the same newspaper interview from decades earlier, they still represent only one underlying source.

The Mount Tomorr and Kuçova narratives illustrate this process particularly well. Different versions disagree over dates, aircraft losses, pilot identities and the sequence of events. Those inconsistencies suggest that later retellings incorporated assumptions, speculation or memory reconstruction rather than newly discovered archival evidence.

## The problem of missing archival chains

Reliable historical investigation normally depends upon an evidential chain linking an event from occurrence to archive.

For a military UFO incident, researchers would ideally expect at least some combination of:

* operational logs;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* radar records;
* flight plans;
* maintenance documentation;
* accident reports;
* weather observations;
* communications records;
* photographs or film;
* multiple independent witness statements.

</div>

For Albania's most famous cases, this chain is usually incomplete.

Instead, researchers often encounter a gap between the alleged event and its first published description, sometimes spanning decades. During that interval it is difficult to determine whether details have been forgotten, altered or unintentionally combined with other events.

The absence of records does not prove an event never occurred. Equally, however, it prevents historians from treating later narratives as verified historical fact.

## Can Sigurimi archives resolve the debate?

Claims regularly appear that Albania's former secret police extensively investigated UFOs.

Publicly available evidence does not presently support such sweeping conclusions.

The opening of Sigurimi records has transformed research into political repression and state surveillance, but no comprehensive, publicly catalogued collection of UFO investigation files has emerged comparable to the extensive archives available in some other countries. Researchers working with these archives also caution that surviving files must be evaluated critically because questions of completeness, authenticity and institutional context remain important.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rime.cnr.it/index.php/rime/article/view/886" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rime.cnr.it">[RiMe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rime.cnr.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The authenticity of files facing their believability &#124; RiMe. Rivista dell&#x27;Istituto di Storia dell&#x27;Europa Mediterranea (ISSN 2035-794X)Jun...</span></span></span>

If additional aviation, defence or intelligence files become accessible in future, they may clarify particular incidents. At present, however, references to secret investigations usually derive from secondary reporting rather than directly accessible archival documents.

## What evidence would significantly improve reliability?

Several categories of documentation could substantially strengthen—or weaken—the credibility of Albania's most discussed UFO cases.

High-value archival discoveries would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* original military flight logs from alleged interception missions;
* aircraft maintenance records showing unexplained damage;
* radar plots preserved with dates and locations;
* contemporaneous police or military reports;
* authenticated photographs with known provenance;
* independently recorded witness statements created immediately after events;
* official correspondence between defence institutions discussing specific incidents.

</div>

Conversely, archival discoveries could also provide ordinary explanations, such as military exercises, foreign aircraft, meteorological events or equipment malfunctions.

Either outcome would represent genuine historical progress because it would replace retrospective storytelling with primary documentation.

## Assessing Albania's UFO record today

A practical way to evaluate Albanian UFO claims is to distinguish between three levels of reliability.

      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });

    });

        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);

        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
