## Introduction

The Rodrigues balloon incident of April 2021 is the best-documented case in [Mauritius]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-mauritiuss-ufo/' | relative_url }}) in which an initially unidentified aerial object directly affected civil aviation operations. Unlike many UFO reports that rely primarily on witness testimony, this incident involved a scheduled Air Mauritius flight, airport authorities, the Department of Civil Aviation, and subsequent official statements explaining what had occurred. Although the object was initially treated as an unidentified hazard, it was later identified as a high-altitude Google Loon communications balloon operating well above normal aircraft cruising altitudes. The episode is significant not because it supports extraordinary claims, but because it demonstrates how aviation authorities respond when an object cannot immediately be identified and safety margins must take precedence over operational convenience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/index.php/download/34/2021/13495/debate-no-07-of-2021-tuesday-11-may-2021.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mauritiusassembly.govmu.org">[Mauritius National Assembly]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mauritiusassembly.govmu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mauritius National Assembly1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No. 07 of 2021 SEVENTH NATIONAL ASSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

## The airport sighting and flight diversion

On 18 April 2021, Air Mauritius flight MK130 departed Mauritius carrying around sixty Rodrigues residents who had completed COVID-19 quarantine and were returning home. As the aircraft approached Sir Gaëtan Duval Airport at Plaine Corail, reports emerged that an unidentified object had been observed near the approach path and runway area. Because the object's nature and altitude could not immediately be confirmed, the crew did not attempt to land. Instead, the aircraft returned to Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport in Mauritius.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lemauricien.com/actualites/rodrigues-un-ballon-sonde-a-laeroport-force-un-avion-a-rebrousser-chemin-vers-maurice/417152/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lemauricien.com">[lemauricien.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lemauricien.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lemauricien.com.</span></span></span>

## Official identification of the balloon

The incident received parliamentary attention in Mauritius, allowing the government to place an official explanation on the public record. During proceedings in the National Assembly, the responsible minister stated that the Director of Civil Aviation had confirmed the object to be a Google Loon balloon operating at approximately 53,000 feet (about 16 kilometres above sea level).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/index.php/download/34/2021/13495/debate-no-07-of-2021-tuesday-11-may-2021.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mauritiusassembly.govmu.org">[Mauritius National Assembly]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mauritiusassembly.govmu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mauritius National Assembly1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No. 07 of 2021 SEVENTH NATIONAL ASSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Google's Loon project, launched in 2011, used fleets of unmanned stratospheric balloons to provide internet connectivity to remote regions. These balloons typically operated between 50,000 and 70,000 feet—well above the cruising altitude of most commercial turboprop aircraft serving Rodrigues and even above the maximum operating altitude of most commercial jetliners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/index.php/download/34/2021/13495/debate-no-07-of-2021-tuesday-11-may-2021.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mauritiusassembly.govmu.org">[Mauritius National Assembly]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mauritiusassembly.govmu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mauritius National Assembly1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No. 07 of 2021 SEVENTH NATIONAL ASSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

The parliamentary statement also clarified that Mauritius had already signed a Letter of Agreement with Loon LLC in March 2019. The agreement established procedures for coordinating balloon operations within the Mauritius Flight Information Region. According to the Department of Civil Aviation, Notices to Airmen (NOTAMs) were normally issued when balloons entered airspace at or below 50,000 feet. Because this balloon remained above that threshold, no NOTAM had been required under the existing procedures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/index.php/download/34/2021/13495/debate-no-07-of-2021-tuesday-11-may-2021.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mauritiusassembly.govmu.org">[Mauritius National Assembly]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mauritiusassembly.govmu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mauritius National Assembly1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No. 07 of 2021 SEVENTH NATIONAL ASSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Once officials confirmed the balloon's altitude and trajectory, Air Mauritius was informed that it would not interfere with aircraft operations, allowing the flight to resume safely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/index.php/download/34/2021/13495/debate-no-07-of-2021-tuesday-11-may-2021.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mauritiusassembly.govmu.org">[Mauritius National Assembly]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mauritiusassembly.govmu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mauritius National Assembly1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No. 07 of 2021 SEVENTH NATIONAL ASSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why the flight still turned back

A natural question is why a balloon flying above 50,000 feet could lead to a flight diversion.

The answer lies in standard aviation risk management rather than the balloon itself. During an approach, pilots and air traffic authorities must make decisions using the information available at the time—not with the benefit of later investigation. If an airborne object near an airport cannot immediately be identified or its altitude accurately established, the safest course is to avoid the potential conflict until its characteristics are verified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/index.php/download/34/2021/13495/debate-no-07-of-2021-tuesday-11-may-2021.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mauritiusassembly.govmu.org">[Mauritius National Assembly]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mauritiusassembly.govmu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mauritius National Assembly1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No. 07 of 2021 SEVENTH NATIONAL ASSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Several factors likely influenced the decision:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The object had initially been reported in proximity to the runway environment.

## What the incident shows about aviation safety

This episode provides an unusually transparent example of how aviation systems handle uncertainty.

Rather than attempting to identify the object visually during the approach or assuming it was harmless, the aircraft diverted until reliable information became available. The subsequent investigation demonstrated several strengths of the aviation safety system:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Precaution first.** Decisions were based on uncertainty rather than assumptions.
* **Institutional coordination.** Airport authorities, the Department of Civil Aviation and Air Mauritius exchanged information before authorising renewed operations.
* **Formal investigation.** Officials publicly documented the object's identity and explained existing coordination procedures with the balloon operator.
* **Operational review.** The government confirmed that an inquiry into the incident had been initiated following the diversion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/index.php/download/34/2021/13495/debate-no-07-of-2021-tuesday-11-may-2021.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mauritiusassembly.govmu.org">[Mauritius National Assembly]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mauritiusassembly.govmu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mauritius National Assembly1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No. 07 of 2021 SEVENTH NATIONAL ASSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Within Mauritius's broader history of unusual aerial reports, the Rodrigues incident stands apart because it generated official documentation rather than relying solely on eyewitness testimony.
