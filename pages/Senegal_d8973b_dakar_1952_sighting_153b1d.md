## Introduction

The 3 July 1952 Dakar sighting is the earliest Senegalese UFO case with a surviving documentary record, but it is also an example of how a report can become famous while the underlying evidence almost entirely disappears. A single eyewitness reportedly observed a fast-moving, flame-trailing object over Dakar shortly after dawn. The account was reproduced in a French-language newspaper and later entered a United States intelligence summary that is now publicly available through the CIA's declassified archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cia-52-2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CUFON 1952 CIA UFO-Related Documents Sampler, Part 2 of 2May 17, 1996</span></span></span>

What makes the case historically significant is not that it proves an extraordinary event, but that almost every piece of evidence needed to identify the object was never preserved. There are no known photographs, no surviving witness interview, no official Senegalese investigation, no weather analysis from the time, and no independent technical data. As a result, modern researchers are left assessing a brief secondary report rather than the original incident itself.

## What the witness reportedly saw

According to the surviving report, the observation occurred at **06:08 on 3 July 1952** over Dakar, then part of French West Africa. The witness described an object travelling south at very high speed at an estimated altitude of roughly **1,500 metres**. It was reportedly:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* flat and tapered in shape;
* trailing long bluish and reddish flames;
* moving rapidly across the sky;
* seen when no aircraft were believed to be operating over Dakar.

</div>

Some later reproductions also state that the stars were no longer visible at the time of the observation, although it is unclear whether this referred to normal daylight conditions around sunrise or was intended as part of the witness description. The surviving versions differ slightly because they derive from translated intelligence summaries rather than the original witness statement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufon.org/cufon/cia-52-2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CUFON 1952 CIA UFO-Related Documents Sampler, Part 2 of 2May 17, 1996</span></span></span>

Even this basic description contains uncertainties. The estimated altitude, speed and shape appear to be witness impressions rather than measured values. Without knowing the object's true distance, none of those estimates can be independently verified.

## How the report reached a CIA archive

The documentary chain is much more limited.
