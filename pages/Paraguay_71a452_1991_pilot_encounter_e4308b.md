## Introduction

The reported UFO encounter of **8 June 1991** is widely regarded as [Paraguay]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-paraguays-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s most significant aviation-related unidentified aerial phenomenon case because it allegedly involved **multiple trained witnesses, two aircraft, air traffic controllers and radar observations during the same event**. Unlike many civilian UFO reports, the case centres on experienced aviation personnel operating within controlled airspace rather than isolated eyewitnesses. That combination has made it the strongest candidate in Paraguay for a genuinely evidential UFO incident.

At the same time, the case remains unresolved not because the available evidence conclusively points to an extraordinary object, but because the most important records—original radar data, air traffic recordings, operational logs and official investigative files—have never been released in a form that allows independent scrutiny. As a result, the incident occupies an unusual position: it is stronger than an ordinary anecdotal sighting, yet weaker than a fully documented aviation investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unidentifiedphenomena.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Unidentified-Flying-Objects-Briefing-Document-The-Best-Available-Evidence.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unidentifiedphenomena.com">[Unidentified Phenomena]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unidentifiedphenomena.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentified Flying Objects Briefing Document The Best Available Evidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified PhenomenaUNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why this case attracts more attention than most Paraguayan sightings

Most UFO reports depend on one witness describing an unexpected light. This incident is considered unusually significant because several independent categories of evidence are claimed to overlap.

### Multiple trained observers

Commercial airline crews, private pilots and air traffic controllers receive professional training in identifying aircraft, navigation lights and airspace hazards. While expertise does not eliminate observational error, it generally makes such testimony more valuable than reports from casual observers.

Published interviews indicate that both flight crews independently reported unusual traffic while controllers were simultaneously monitoring the situation from the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.scribd.com/document/941264951/Entrevista-Ronald-Maidana-en-UFo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: es.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">es.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Entrevista sobre Ufología en Paraguay &#124; PDF &#124; Objeto volador no identificado &#124; Ufología...</span></span></span>

### Alleged radar detection

Perhaps the strongest claimed element is that controllers reportedly observed an unidentified radar return corresponding to the visual reports.

If accurate, this would distinguish the event from purely optical sightings. A radar return suggests either:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a physical object producing a detectable reflection;
* an atmospheric or technical radar anomaly;
* a processing or interpretation error.

</div>

However, the original radar plots have not entered the public domain, preventing specialists from determining which explanation is most plausible. Modern investigators therefore cannot independently verify whether the radar observations matched the visual reports in position, timing or movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unidentifiedphenomena.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Unidentified-Flying-Objects-Briefing-Document-The-Best-Available-Evidence.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unidentifiedphenomena.com">[Unidentified Phenomena]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unidentifiedphenomena.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentified Flying Objects Briefing Document The Best Available Evidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified PhenomenaUNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

### An alleged official report

Several Paraguayan researchers state that an internal report was prepared by air traffic controller **Aníbal Gabigan** and that documentation existed within the Ministry of Defence or civil aviation system.

Published interviews describe the document but do not reproduce the complete original file or supporting operational records. ## Which records would actually test the account

The continuing uncertainty surrounding the 1991 encounter stems less from disputed witness testimony than from the absence of documentary evidence needed to evaluate it objectively.

The most valuable records would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* primary radar recordings or preserved radar plots;
* air traffic control voice recordings;
* cockpit voice recordings, if retained;
* aircraft flight logs;
* controller logbooks;
* meteorological observations for the period;
* military airspace activity records;
* maintenance records showing whether radar equipment experienced faults.

</div>

Together these records could answer questions that eyewitness testimony alone cannot.

For example, radar data could establish whether an unknown target maintained a consistent track or instead appeared intermittently in a manner suggestive of interference. Radio recordings could determine precisely what each crew reported before later memories evolved. Weather data might identify atmospheric conditions capable of producing unusual optical or radar effects.

Unfortunately, no publicly accessible archive presently contains this complete evidential package. The absence of these materials prevents the case from reaching the evidential standard expected in modern aviation investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unidentifiedphenomena.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Unidentified-Flying-Objects-Briefing-Document-The-Best-Available-Evidence.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unidentifiedphenomena.com">[Unidentified Phenomena]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unidentifiedphenomena.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentified Flying Objects Briefing Document The Best Available Evidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified PhenomenaUNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

## Ordinary explanations and the limits of current evidence

Despite its reputation, the encounter has never received a definitive official explanation.

Several conventional possibilities remain open.

**Misidentified aircraft.** Multiple aircraft operating at different altitudes and headings can create confusing visual impressions, particularly at night. Without radar data, this possibility cannot be excluded.

**Astronomical objects.** Bright planets can produce striking visual effects under some atmospheric conditions, but they generally do not account for reported coordinated radar observations or apparent manoeuvring around aircraft.

**Atmospheric phenomena.** Temperature inversions, reflections and unusual weather conditions can occasionally affect both visual perception and radar performance. Evaluating this possibility requires the detailed meteorological records that are currently unavailable.

**Radar anomalies.** Primary radar occasionally produces false targets through interference, anomalous propagation or equipment faults. Independent radar plots would be needed to determine whether the reported track behaved like a genuine object or a technical artefact.

None of these explanations can presently be confirmed or eliminated because the original technical evidence has not been released. ## Why the encounter still matters

Within Paraguay's UFO history, the 1991 encounter occupies a unique position because it combines several forms of testimony that rarely occur together:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* commercial and private aviation witnesses;
* reported visual observations from both air and ground;
* claimed radar confirmation;
* references to official documentation;
* continuing discussion by Paraguayan investigators over more than three decades.

</div>

Those features explain why researchers frequently describe it as Paraguay's strongest UFO case. Yet the same case also illustrates the limits of historical UFO research. Without preservation and public release of original operational records, even an incident involving experienced pilots and air traffic personnel cannot move beyond informed debate.
