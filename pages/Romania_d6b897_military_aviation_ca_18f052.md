## Introduction

[Romania]({{ 'what-really-supports-romanias-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s best-known military UFO stories are not claims of recovered spacecraft or official confirmation of extraterrestrial visitors. Instead, they centre on two unusual aviation incidents that remain incompletely explained because the underlying military evidence has never been fully released. The first is the reported 1984 alert at Alexeni Air Base, where radar operators and ground personnel described an unidentified object above the base. The second is the 2007 MiG-21 LanceR accident near Gherla, in which an unidentified object apparently struck a fighter aircraft, injuring the pilot and damaging the canopy. Both cases occupy an important place in Romanian UFO history because they involve military personnel rather than civilian witnesses. At the same time, both demonstrate how an incident can remain officially unresolved without providing evidence for extraordinary technology or extraterrestrial origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/romanian-cases?start=9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Romanian cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romanian cases</span></span></span>

## The 1984 Alexeni Air Base alert

The Alexeni incident is widely regarded as Romania's most significant reported military UFO encounter during the communist era. According to accounts preserved by the Romanian Association for the Study of Unidentified Aerospace Phenomena (ASFAN), the event occurred on 23 August 1984 after aircraft participating in the national military parade had returned to Alexeni Air Base in Ialomia County. Radar personnel at Otopeni reportedly contacted Alexeni after detecting an unidentified target directly above the airfield, even though local staff confirmed that all authorised aircraft had already landed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/istorie/109-alexeni-23-august-1984" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">23 august 1984February 13, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2014</span></span></span>

The case is primarily known through the testimony of Emil Strainu, then serving within the radar command structure and later a retired general. According to his account, the unidentified target was not only detected by radar but was also reportedly observed visually using optical instruments including telescopes and theodolites. Witnesses described an ovoid, metallic-looking object estimated at roughly two to three metres in length, apparently hovering over the base before departing rapidly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/istorie/109-alexeni-23-august-1984" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">23 august 1984February 13, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2014</span></span></span>

What distinguishes Alexeni from many civilian reports is the claimed combination of:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* radar detection by military stations;
* confirmation that authorised aircraft were on the ground;
* multiple military witnesses;
* visual observation through optical equipment.

</div>

However, these strengths are balanced by significant evidential weaknesses. No authenticated radar recordings, air-defence logs, flight-operation documents, photographs, or command reports have entered the public domain. Most modern descriptions ultimately derive from retrospective witness testimony collected years later rather than from contemporary military documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/istorie/109-alexeni-23-august-1984" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">23 august 1984February 13, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2014</span></span></span>

For historians, Alexeni therefore represents an unresolved military observation rather than a verified anomalous aerospace event. The absence of original operational records makes it impossible to determine whether the radar target reflected an unusual atmospheric phenomenon, equipment behaviour, an unidentified conventional object, or something genuinely unexplained.

## The 2007 MiG-21 canopy strike near Gherla

The strongest Romanian military aviation case is not a sighting but a physical accident. On 30 October 2007, a Romanian Air Force MiG-21 LanceR from the 71st Air Base near Cmpia Turzii was conducting a training mission near Gherla in Cluj County when the aircraft suffered a sudden high-speed impact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/romanian-cases/188-romanian-fighter-hit-by-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span></span></span>

Pilot Marin Mitric reported that a hard object struck the right side of the cockpit, shattering the canopy. Flying at approximately 6,500 metres and about 850 km/h, he was suddenly exposed to freezing airflow. Fragments from the canopy penetrated his helmet and caused facial injuries, while the rapid decompression created an immediate flight emergency. Despite these conditions, he reduced speed, descended to denser air to avoid hypoxia and successfully landed the damaged aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/romanian-cases/188-romanian-fighter-hit-by-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span></span></span>

Unlike many UFO cases, there was indisputable physical damage:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the canopy was destroyed;
* the pilot sustained documented injuries;
* the aircraft returned with impact damage requiring investigation.

</div>

The military established a commission to examine the incident. Reports from investigators indicated that the aircraft's onboard camera had captured several rapidly moving objects approaching from the right-hand side before the collision. General Ion Avram later stated publicly that the recording appeared to show four triangular objects approaching the aircraft, after which the imagery was reportedly forwarded to the Romanian Intelligence Service (SRI) for technical analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/romanian-cases/188-romanian-fighter-hit-by-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span></span></span>

## Why the MiG-21 incident remains unresolved

The Gherla case is frequently presented in sensational terms, but the available evidence supports a more cautious assessment.

Investigators reportedly considered several conventional explanations. Bird strikes were regarded as unlikely because of the aircraft's altitude and seasonal conditions. Fragments from another aircraft were also considered improbable because no nearby traffic matched the timing. Large meteorites or orbital debris would have been expected to produce substantially greater damage than was observed. None of these possibilities produced a definitive explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/romanian-cases/188-romanian-fighter-hit-by-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span></span></span>

Crucially, "unidentified" does not mean "extraordinary". Public reporting has never included:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the complete cockpit video;
* engineering analysis of the damaged canopy;
* laboratory reports on recovered material;
* radar tracks of the objects;
* a final technical investigation released by the Romanian Ministry of Defence.

</div>

Without these materials, independent investigators cannot verify either the characteristics of the objects or the process by which alternative explanations were excluded.

The available evidence therefore supports only a limited conclusion: an unidentified object or objects apparently collided with the aircraft, causing genuine damage, while the precise nature of those objects remains publicly unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/romanian-cases/188-romanian-fighter-hit-by-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Romanian fighter hit by &quot;UFOs</span></span></span>

## What military evidence would be needed

Both Alexeni and Gherla illustrate the same evidential problem. Military incidents often sound stronger than civilian reports because trained observers, radar systems and operational procedures are involved. Yet those advantages matter only if the underlying records become available for independent examination.

For either Romanian case to move beyond historical interest, researchers would ideally need:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* authenticated primary radar recordings;
* original air-defence command logs;
* complete maintenance and damage reports;
* full cockpit video at original resolution;
* chain-of-custody documentation for any recovered fragments;
* meteorological and air-traffic records for the relevant time;
* official conclusions from the investigating authorities rather than later recollections.

</div>

## How these cases fit within Romania's broader UFO record

Romania's military UFO reports differ from famous civilian incidents such as the 1968 [Hoia-Baciu]({{ 'hoia-baciu/' | relative_url }}) photographs because they involve operational military environments rather than isolated eyewitnesses. That gives them greater historical interest but not necessarily stronger evidential value.

The Alexeni alert rests largely on retrospective testimony describing radar and visual observations for which the original operational records remain unavailable. The Gherla collision offers stronger physical evidence because an aircraft was genuinely damaged and the pilot injured, yet the decisive technical documentation has likewise not been released publicly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asfanufo.ro/index.php/istorie/109-alexeni-23-august-1984" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asfanufo.ro">[asfanufo.ro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asfanufo.ro</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">23 august 1984February 13, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2014</span></span></span>

Taken together, these incidents illustrate an important theme across Romania's UFO history: the country's most intriguing military cases are unresolved primarily because the evidential record is incomplete, not because existing evidence demonstrates technology beyond known human capabilities. Their significance lies in showing how military encounters can remain unexplained for decades when radar data, engineering reports and official investigative files are inaccessible, leaving both extraordinary claims and conventional explanations impossible to confirm conclusively.
