## Introduction

The September 2011 Boumdeid fireball is the strongest documented example in [Mauritania]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-mauritanias/' | relative_url }}) of an object that was initially unidentified but was subsequently explained through physical evidence. Unlike many reported UFO incidents, this event did not remain dependent on eyewitness testimony alone. A recovered meteorite, laboratory analysis, and publication in the official Meteoritical Bulletin transformed what many observers first experienced as a mysterious object into a well-documented meteorite fall.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meteor/metbull.cfm?code=57167" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lpi.usra.edu">[LPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lpi.usra.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meteoritical Bulletin: Entry for Boumdeid (2011)September 14, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

For researchers examining alleged UFO incidents in Mauritania, the Boumdeid case is valuable precisely because it demonstrates how an unexplained aerial event can move from speculation to scientific certainty. Rather than supporting claims of unidentified technology, the case illustrates the evidential process by which a spectacular atmospheric phenomenon became conclusively identified.

## The Night of the Fireball

On the evening of **14 September 2011**, at approximately **21:00 GMT**, residents across parts of [southern Mauritania]({{ 'southern-cluster/' | relative_url }}) witnessed an exceptionally bright fireball travelling across the sky. The object was seen by hundreds of people, particularly in the regions of **Assaba** and **Gorgol**, north and west of Kiffa.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-231b333e-b99e-48d4-a5d8-baf5606ee63e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yadda.icm.edu.pl">[Yadda]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yadda.icm.edu.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Yadda The meteorite fall near Boumdeid, Mauritania, from</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The meteorite fall near Boumdeid, Mauritania, from September 14, 2011 - Meteorites - Tom Vol. 3, No. 1-2 (2014) - BazTech - Yadda...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

The explosions caused understandable alarm among local residents. Before any scientific investigation had begun, many witnesses simply knew that an extraordinarily bright object had crossed the sky and appeared to explode overhead.

## From Witness Reports to a Reconstructed Trajectory

One reason the Boumdeid event became scientifically valuable is that investigators collected eyewitness information rapidly rather than relying on memories years later.

Researchers recorded numerous witness accounts and mapped observation locations using GPS. Because Mauritania lacked an all-sky fireball camera network, these human observations became the primary data source for reconstructing the object's atmospheric path. Weather information, particularly upper-atmosphere wind data, was then incorporated to estimate the likely flight path and impact area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-231b333e-b99e-48d4-a5d8-baf5606ee63e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yadda.icm.edu.pl">[Yadda]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yadda.icm.edu.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Yadda The meteorite fall near Boumdeid, Mauritania, from</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The meteorite fall near Boumdeid, Mauritania, from September 14, 2011 - Meteorites - Tom Vol. 3, No. 1-2 (2014) - BazTech - Yadda...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

This approach demonstrated that useful trajectory reconstruction remains possible even in regions without sophisticated monitoring infrastructure. The Boumdeid investigation has since been cited as an example of post-event recovery methods in remote areas where instrument coverage is limited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-231b333e-b99e-48d4-a5d8-baf5606ee63e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yadda.icm.edu.pl">[Yadda]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yadda.icm.edu.pl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Yadda The meteorite fall near Boumdeid, Mauritania, from</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The meteorite fall near Boumdeid, Mauritania, from September 14, 2011 - Meteorites - Tom Vol. 3, No. 1-2 (2014) - BazTech - Yadda...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

Although the reconstructed trajectory could not achieve the precision available from dedicated fireball camera networks, the independent witness observations showed remarkable consistency regarding the direction of travel, fragmentation, and approximate fall location.

## The Recovery That Changed the Investigation

The decisive turning point came the following morning.

Witnesses had reported seeing a stone strike the ground, and a meteorite was recovered near Boumdeid shortly afterwards. Rather than relying solely on stories or photographs, investigators now possessed physical evidence that could be analysed in laboratories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meteor/metbull.cfm?code=57167" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lpi.usra.edu">[LPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lpi.usra.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meteoritical Bulletin: Entry for Boumdeid (2011)September 14, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

The recovered specimen weighed approximately **3.6 kilograms**. It retained a fresh black fusion crust formed during atmospheric entry, while broken surfaces revealed visible chondrules—small spherical structures characteristic of many stony meteorites. Approximately **1.6 kg** of the main mass was reportedly transferred to local authorities, while type specimens were preserved for scientific study.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meteor/metbull.cfm?code=57167" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lpi.usra.edu">[LPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lpi.usra.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meteoritical Bulletin: Entry for Boumdeid (2011)September 14, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

The existence of a recovered stone fundamentally separates the Boumdeid incident from most UFO reports. Instead of asking what witnesses thought they had seen, investigators could examine the object itself.

## Laboratory Proof That It Was a Meteorite

Laboratory examination produced several independent lines of evidence confirming that the recovered object was a freshly fallen meteorite.

Petrographic analysis revealed a strongly recrystallised **ordinary chondrite of type L6**, showing a brecciated internal texture. Mineralogical examination identified typical meteoritic minerals including:

* olivine;
* pyroxene;
* kamacite;
* taenite;
* troilite;
* chromite;
* ilmenite.

The meteorite was classified as **L6, shock stage S2, weathering grade W0**. The **W0** designation is especially important because it indicates essentially no terrestrial weathering, exactly what would be expected from a newly fallen meteorite rather than an older desert find.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meteor/metbull.cfm?code=57167" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lpi.usra.edu">[LPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lpi.usra.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meteoritical Bulletin: Entry for Boumdeid (2011)September 14, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

Researchers also performed gamma-ray spectroscopy only **84 days after the fall**. They detected short-lived cosmogenic radionuclides, including **beryllium-7, cobalt-56, cobalt-58, and scandium-46**, isotopes that decay relatively quickly after exposure to cosmic radiation ends. Their presence confirmed that the meteorite had entered Earth's atmosphere only recently. Additional radionuclide measurements suggested a pre-atmospheric meteoroid radius of roughly **10–20 cm**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meteor/metbull.cfm?code=57167" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lpi.usra.edu">[LPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lpi.usra.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meteoritical Bulletin: Entry for Boumdeid (2011)September 14, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

These laboratory results provided independent confirmation of the eyewitness accounts. Even if no witnesses had existed, the recovered stone itself demonstrated that a recent meteorite fall had occurred.

## What the Boumdeid Fall Means for Mauritania's UFO Record

Within Mauritania's limited catalogue of reported unusual aerial phenomena, the Boumdeid fall serves as an important benchmark for evaluating evidence.

It demonstrates several principles relevant to later reports:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* eyewitness testimony becomes much stronger when supported by physical evidence;
* recovered material allows laboratory testing independent of witness interpretation;
* multiple independent lines of evidence produce much greater certainty than visual reports alone;
* spectacular atmospheric events can initially resemble unidentified objects but ultimately receive ordinary scientific explanations.

</div>

This is why the Boumdeid incident occupies a distinctive place within Mauritania's record of unusual aerial observations. It began as a dramatic unidentified fireball witnessed across southern Mauritania but ended as one of the country's most thoroughly documented meteorite falls, supported by eyewitness observations, trajectory reconstruction, physical recovery, mineralogical analysis, cosmogenic radionuclide measurements, and formal international classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meteor/metbull.cfm?code=57167" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lpi.usra.edu">[usra.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lpi.usra.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Meteoritical Bulletin: Entry for Boumdeid (2011)September 14, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2011</span></span></span>

    }
    return '';
  }

      var saved = window.localStorage && localStorage.getItem(storageKey);
      if (saved && available.indexOf(saved) >= 0) return saved;
    } catch (err) {}
    var timezoneMarket = inferFromTimezone();
    if (timezoneMarket && available.indexOf(timezoneMarket) >= 0) return timezoneMarket;
    var localeMarkets = localeMatches();
    if (localeMarkets.exact && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.exact) >= 0) return localeMarkets.exact;
    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
    if (options.defaultMarket && available.indexOf(options.defaultMarket) >= 0) return options.defaultMarket;
    return available[0] || '';
  }

    inferMarket: inferMarket,
