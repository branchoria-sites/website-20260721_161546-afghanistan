## Introduction

[Grenada]({{ 'grenadas-ufo-mystery-beyond-the-myths/' | relative_url }})’s most significant connection to UFO history comes not from a verified sighting or recovered object, but from Prime Minister Sir Eric Gairy’s attempt to make UFO research an issue of international governance. During the late 1970s, Gairy argued that unidentified flying objects and related phenomena deserved coordinated scientific study, and he used Grenada’s position at the United Nations to push for an international research mechanism. His campaign resulted in formal UN discussions in 1977 and 1978, making Grenada one of the few countries to place UFO investigation on the agenda of a major international organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/679889" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemEstablishment of an agency or a department of the United Nations for undertaking, co-ordinating and...</span></span></span>

The initiative remains controversial because it sat between scientific inquiry, political advocacy, and Gairy’s personal beliefs about unexplained phenomena. Supporters viewed it as an early call for international cooperation on an unresolved subject, while critics considered it an unusual diplomatic priority for a small Caribbean state facing serious domestic political tensions. The lasting historical importance of Grenada’s UFO story lies mainly in this diplomatic campaign rather than in evidence for extraterrestrial activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1977/11/06/some-people-think-grenada-is-a-ufo/47e0490b-b764-48ce-8cef-8c426abf13b9/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Washington Post Some People Think Grenada Is a UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Washington PostSome People Think Grenada Is a UFO - The Washington PostNovember 6, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 6, 1977</span></span></span>

## Gairy’s interest in unexplained phenomena

### From personal curiosity to government policy

Sir Eric Gairy, Grenada’s prime minister from 1974 until his government was overthrown in 1979, developed a strong personal interest in UFO reports and other unexplained phenomena. Contemporary accounts described him as fascinated by questions that he believed remained unanswered by conventional science, including reports of unidentified objects and possible extraterrestrial life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1977/11/06/some-people-think-grenada-is-a-ufo/47e0490b-b764-48ce-8cef-8c426abf13b9/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Washington Post Some People Think Grenada Is a UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Washington PostSome People Think Grenada Is a UFO - The Washington PostNovember 6, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 6, 1977</span></span></span>

Gairy did not present UFO research simply as a hobby. As head of government, he argued that unidentified phenomena were a matter worthy of systematic investigation and international cooperation. His position was that individual countries lacked the resources and information-sharing systems needed to examine reports from around the world. This transformed the subject from a private interest into a diplomatic proposal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/676944/files/A_SPC_32_SR-35-EN.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemSPECIAL POLITICAL COMtHTTEEJanuary 21, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 21, 2026</span></span></span>

The available record does not establish that Gairy’s views were based on independently verified UFO evidence from Grenada. Some accounts refer to his belief that unusual events had occurred in or around the island, and later retellings have repeated claims that he personally witnessed unexplained objects. However, the strongest documented evidence concerns his advocacy activities, speeches, and UN submissions rather than any confirmed encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/676944/files/A_SPC_32_SR-35-EN.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemSPECIAL POLITICAL COMtHTTEEJanuary 21, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 21, 2026</span></span></span>

## The push for international UFO research

### Taking UFOs to the United Nations

Gairy’s campaign reached the United Nations in the 1970s through Grenada’s diplomatic delegation. In 1977, Grenada submitted a draft resolution calling for the establishment of a UN agency or department to undertake, coordinate, and distribute research into unidentified flying objects and related phenomena. The proposal was formally recorded as a UN General Assembly document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/679889" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemEstablishment of an agency or a department of the United Nations for undertaking, co-ordinating and...</span></span></span>

The proposal was notable because it did not ask the UN to accept extraterrestrial explanations as fact. Instead, it framed UFOs as an area requiring organised research and the exchange of information between governments. The argument was that unexplained reports existed internationally and should be examined through a coordinated process rather than through isolated national efforts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/679889" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemEstablishment of an agency or a department of the United Nations for undertaking, co-ordinating and...</span></span></span>

[UN records]({{ 'un-records/' | relative_url }}) show that Gairy personally addressed the issue and argued that modern scientific progress had not eliminated all unexplained questions. During discussions of the proposal, Grenada presented the creation of an international research body as a way to collect data and encourage cooperation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/676944/files/A_SPC_32_SR-35-EN.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemSPECIAL POLITICAL COMtHTTEEJanuary 21, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 21, 2026</span></span></span>

### The 1978 continuation of the campaign

Grenada renewed its effort in 1978 by again placing the subject before the United Nations. A Grenadian request sought inclusion of an agenda item concerning the creation of an agency or department for researching and coordinating information on UFOs and related phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/655114" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemRequest for the inclusion of an item in the provisional agenda of the 33rd session:July 12, 1978...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 12, 1978</span></span></span>

The resulting 1978 draft resolution repeated the central idea of the campaign: that the UN could provide a neutral forum for gathering and sharing research. The proposal became part of discussions in the General Assembly’s Special Political Committee, where the subject received formal attention but did not lead to the creation of a permanent UN body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/678474?ln=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System Unidentified flying objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemUnidentified flying objects...</span></span></span>

The campaign also brought together figures from the wider UFO research community. Accounts of the 1978 UN discussions refer to presentations involving researchers and public figures interested in UFO investigation, helping make the event one of the most visible moments in the history of official UFO advocacy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theufodatabase.com/people/eric-gairy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The UFO Database Eric Gairy &#124; The UFO Database</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Database Eric Gairy &#124; The UFO Database</span></span></span>

## Political impact and lasting legacy

### A diplomatic achievement with limited institutional results

Gairy’s campaign achieved something unusual: it moved UFOs from a subject usually discussed by private researchers and enthusiasts into formal international diplomacy.
