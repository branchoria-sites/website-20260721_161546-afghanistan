## Introduction

Some of the most striking aerial sightings reported from [Bosnia and Herzegovina]({{ 'bosnia-and-herzegovina/' | relative_url }}) have ultimately turned out to be among the easiest to explain. Rather than weakening the country's UFO record, these cases illustrate an important principle: when accurate timing, multiple observations and astronomical data are available, apparently mysterious lights can often be identified with considerable confidence. Two examples stand out. The first is the exceptionally bright fireball recorded above Sarajevo in 2013, which was scientifically analysed by Bosnia's own meteor-monitoring network. The second is the repeated appearance of SpaceX's Starlink satellite trains, whose orderly formations prompted numerous UFO reports across Bosnia during 2023 before being matched to known satellite passes.

These cases are valuable because they show how evidence quality changes when observations can be checked against independent data. They provide a useful contrast with Bosnian UFO reports that remain genuinely unidentified only because too little information survives to reach a reliable conclusion.

## The 2013 Sarajevo Fireball

In the early hours of 2 November 2013, residents across parts of Bosnia and Herzegovina witnessed an exceptionally bright object crossing the sky. Unlike many dramatic aerial reports, this event was not dependent solely on eyewitness testimony. It was captured by cameras belonging to the Bosnian meteor network operated by the Astronomical Society Orion together with the Federal Hydrometeorological Institute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radiosarajevo.ba/metromahala/kultura/iznad-sarajeva-eksplodirao-meteor/130454" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radiosarajevo.ba">[Radiosarajevo.ba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radiosarajevo.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Iznad Sarajeva eksplodirao meteor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 2, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2013</span></span></span>

Astronomers identified the object as a **bolide**—an unusually bright meteor that fragments violently while passing through Earth's atmosphere. Their preliminary measurements indicated:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an entry speed of roughly **20 km/s**;
* a peak brightness exceeding magnitude **−9**, making it comparable to the brightness of the quarter Moon;
* fragmentation at an altitude of about **30 km**;
* a possible meteorite fall area within the triangle between **Zenica, Bugojno and Travnik** if any fragments survived atmospheric entry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radiosarajevo.ba/metromahala/kultura/iznad-sarajeva-eksplodirao-meteor/130454" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radiosarajevo.ba">[Radiosarajevo.ba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radiosarajevo.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Iznad Sarajeva eksplodirao meteor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 2, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2013</span></span></span>

</div>

Because the event was observed simultaneously from several monitoring stations, researchers could reconstruct its trajectory far more accurately than would be possible from eyewitness accounts alone. This transformed what might otherwise have become a long-lived UFO story into a documented astronomical event.

### Why the case matters

The Sarajevo bolide demonstrates how scientific monitoring changes the quality of evidence.

Instead of relying on descriptions such as "a glowing object" or "an exploding light", investigators could compare images from multiple cameras, estimate the object's velocity, calculate its flight path and assess whether surviving meteorites were plausible. These are the same techniques used internationally to analyse significant meteor events. The International Meteor Organization and associated fireball reporting programmes similarly combine numerous eyewitness reports and instrumental observations to reconstruct fireball trajectories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.imo.net/observations/fireballs/fireball-report-program/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: imo.net">[International Meteor Organization]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">imo.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Meteor Organization Fireball Program &#124; IMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Meteor Organization Fireball Program &#124; IMO</span></span></span>

Not every bright meteor leaves recoverable fragments, and there is no widely documented confirmation that meteorites from this event were ultimately recovered. Nevertheless, the incident remains one of Bosnia and Herzegovina's best-documented spectacular sky events precisely because it moved rapidly from eyewitness reports to astronomical analysis.

## The 2023 Starlink Formation

A decade later, another series of dramatic sightings spread across Bosnia and Herzegovina—but this time the objects were moving far more slowly.

During February 2023, numerous observers in Herzegovina, Prijedor and other parts of the country reported seeing dozens of bright lights travelling in a perfectly ordered line across the evening sky. Videos circulated rapidly online, with many viewers wondering whether they were witnessing an unusual aircraft formation or something more mysterious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radiosarajevo.ba/multimedia/video/video-na-nebu-iznad-bosne-i-hercegovine-ponovo-snimljen-niz-neobicnih-svjetala/483847" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radiosarajevo.ba">[Radiosarajevo.ba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radiosarajevo.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Video: Na nebu iznad Bosne i Hercegovine ponovo snimljen niz neobičnih svjetala</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 13, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2023</span></span></span>

The explanation was considerably less exotic.

The lights closely matched recently launched **Starlink** communications satellites from SpaceX. Shortly after launch, Starlink satellites temporarily travel in closely spaced "trains" before gradually dispersing into higher operational orbits. During this phase they can appear as a striking procession of evenly spaced lights moving silently across the sky.

Local reporting noted that the formation remained visible for several minutes and matched the appearance of Starlink trains already documented elsewhere around the world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radiosarajevo.ba/multimedia/video/video-na-nebu-iznad-bosne-i-hercegovine-ponovo-snimljen-niz-neobicnih-svjetala/483847" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radiosarajevo.ba">[Radiosarajevo.ba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radiosarajevo.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Video: Na nebu iznad Bosne i Hercegovine ponovo snimljen niz neobičnih svjetala</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 13, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 13, 2023</span></span></span>

### Why Starlink produces UFO reports

Starlink formations possess several characteristics that naturally encourage UFO interpretations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* dozens of identical lights;
* perfectly regular spacing;
* silent movement;
* steady speed with no visible flashing navigation lights;
* appearance in regions where observers have never previously seen such formations.

</div>

Unlike aircraft, the satellites produce no audible engine noise and usually maintain a constant trajectory. Unlike meteors, they move slowly enough to remain visible for several minutes. To someone unfamiliar with recent satellite launches, the effect can appear highly unusual.

The same pattern has occurred internationally, to the point that astronomical organisations and meteor-reporting networks now routinely identify Starlink trains when members of the public report apparently extraordinary linear formations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fireball.amsmeteors.org/imo_view/event/2023/6878" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fireball.amsmeteors.org">[fireball.amsmeteors.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fireball.amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fireball event</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 10, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 10, 2023</span></span></span>

## How Astronomers Confirm What Witnesses Saw

The Sarajevo bolide and the Starlink observations illustrate two different identification methods.

For bright meteors, astronomers rely on:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* synchronised camera recordings;
* observations from multiple locations;
* trajectory reconstruction;
* brightness estimates;
* atmospheric entry modelling.

</div>

These allow researchers to estimate speed, altitude, fragmentation and potential meteorite fall zones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://radiosarajevo.ba/metromahala/kultura/iznad-sarajeva-eksplodirao-meteor/130454" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: radiosarajevo.ba">[radiosarajevo.ba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">radiosarajevo.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Iznad Sarajeva eksplodirao meteor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 2, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2013</span></span></span>

For Starlink sightings, investigators instead compare:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the exact observation time;
* the observer's location;
* predicted satellite passes;
* launch schedules;
* the distinctive geometry of newly deployed satellite trains.

</div>

Because satellite orbits are predictable, a reported formation can often be matched to a known Starlink pass with high confidence.

The important point is that both explanations depend on **independent, testable data**, not simply on opinion or scepticism.
