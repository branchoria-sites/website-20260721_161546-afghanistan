## Introduction

The strongest UFO reports associated with [Panama]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-panamas-ufo/' | relative_url }}) City and the Pacific entrance to the Panama Canal are visually striking but evidentially modest. They have attracted attention because they occur over one of the world's busiest combinations of urban skyline, international airport traffic, commercial shipping lanes and strategically important waterways. That environment creates many opportunities for unusual-looking aerial observations while simultaneously making conventional explanations difficult to rule out without detailed technical evidence.

Within the wider history of Panamanian UFO reports, the Panama City and canal cases are therefore best understood as **unresolved visual incidents rather than confirmed anomalies**. The best-known examples include a widely shared series of photographs taken over Panama City in 2015 and an orange spherical object reportedly seen near ships waiting to enter the canal in 2013. Neither case is supported by radar records, calibrated imagery, multiple independent observation points or official investigative findings demonstrating extraordinary flight characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=101377" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 101377August 30, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 30, 2013</span></span></span>

## The 2015 Panama City photographs

One of Panama's best-known modern UFO episodes occurred in July 2015, when photographs showing an apparently unusual object above Panama City circulated widely through local media and social networks.

The photographs generated considerable public discussion because the object appeared disc-like in some enlarged images. However, the available public record contains significant limitations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the original image files have not been released for forensic analysis;
* no verified sequence demonstrating unusual motion has been published;
* no triangulation from multiple photographers exists;
* no accompanying radar or air traffic evidence has entered the public domain; and
* no official investigation concluded that the object represented unknown technology.

</div>

Without those elements, the photographs cannot establish whether the object was distant or nearby, large or small, stationary or moving rapidly. Objects such as balloons, birds captured out of focus, airborne debris or conventional aircraft photographed under unusual perspective remain plausible explanations because the available evidence cannot reliably eliminate them.

For these reasons, the case is most accurately classified as **unresolved because of insufficient information**, rather than as either a confirmed extraordinary event or a solved misidentification.

## The 2013 orange sphere near canal shipping

Another frequently cited report concerns an observation made on 21 August 2013. According to the witness report, two observers saw an orange sphere over the bay near vessels waiting at the Pacific entrance to the Panama Canal. The object reportedly moved steadily for approximately two minutes, and three zoomed photographs were taken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=101377" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 101377August 30, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 30, 2013</span></span></span>

The report is notable because it places the object over a familiar maritime reference point rather than an empty sky. Nevertheless, the available evidence remains limited.

Several characteristics reduce its evidential strength:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* only one witness account entered the public reporting system;
* the photographs are zoomed rather than wide-field contextual images;
* no precise distance to the object can be established;
* no independent witnesses have publicly corroborated the observation; and
* no supporting aviation, radar or maritime data have been published.

</div>

The reported steady linear motion is compatible with numerous ordinary possibilities, including a distant aircraft viewed from an unusual angle, an illuminated balloon drifting with upper-level winds, or another conventional aerial object whose apparent position was influenced by perspective over the shipping anchorage. Because no geometric reconstruction is possible, the report remains unidentified rather than demonstrably anomalous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=101377" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 101377August 30, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 30, 2013</span></span></span>

## Why photographs over the canal are especially difficult to interpret

Photographs taken from Panama City's waterfront often compress enormous distances into a single frame. The canal entrance, anchored ships and the skyline can appear visually close together even though they are separated by several kilometres.

This geometry creates several recurring problems.

### Distance illusions

Without knowing exactly where an object lies relative to ships or buildings, observers frequently overestimate its size. A small balloon a few hundred metres away may appear comparable in scale to a vessel several kilometres distant.

### Digital zoom and focus

Many reported UFO images from the 2010s were captured with mobile phones using substantial digital zoom. Compression, sharpening and autofocus can transform ordinary objects into apparently structured discs or spheres, particularly against bright skies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufouap.net/en/explainers/common-ufo-misidentifications" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufouap.net">[ufouap.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufouap.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 21, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2026</span></span></span>

### Maritime reference errors

Observers often assume an object is directly above visible ships. In reality, it may be much closer or much farther away than the vessels beneath it in the image. Without multiple observation points, apparent altitude and speed cannot be measured reliably.

## Aircraft, ships, drones and ordinary explanations

The canal approaches demand a higher evidential threshold than quieter locations because conventional traffic is exceptionally dense.

Potential ordinary explanations commonly considered include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Commercial aircraft.** Aircraft viewed head-on or at shallow angles can appear nearly stationary before suddenly changing apparent direction as geometry changes.
* **Helicopters.** Hovering or slow-moving helicopters may appear unusually stable, particularly in hazy conditions.
* **Ships and mast lighting.** Bright maritime lights viewed across humid air can produce misleading impressions of hovering luminous objects.
* **Balloons.** Sunlit or illuminated balloons often appear as metallic or orange spheres, especially near sunset.
* **Consumer drones.** Since the mid-2010s, drones have become an increasingly plausible explanation for low-altitude urban observations.
* **Atmospheric effects.** Tropical haze and strong temperature gradients over water reduce contrast and distort distance estimates.

</div>

None of these explanations automatically solves every report. Rather, they demonstrate why extraordinary interpretations require stronger supporting evidence than photographs or eyewitness testimony alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufouap.net/en/explainers/common-ufo-misidentifications" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufouap.net">[ufouap.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufouap.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 21, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2026</span></span></span>

## How these cases compare with stronger UFO evidence

The Panama City and canal reports differ substantially from internationally recognised high-evidence UFO cases.

The strongest modern cases typically combine several independent forms of evidence, such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* multiple witnesses separated by distance;
* radar observations;
* precisely timed imagery;
* pilot testimony;
* flight records; and
* preserved original data suitable for later forensic examination.

</div>

The Panama City cases lack this combination. Their principal evidence consists of eyewitness accounts and photographs whose geometry cannot be reconstructed. As a result, they remain interesting examples of unexplained observations but do not demonstrate extraordinary performance or technology.

## What these cases contribute to Panama's UFO record

Within Panama's broader UFO history, the Panama City and canal reports occupy an important but limited position. They illustrate how prominent locations generate highly visible sightings that rapidly circulate through news outlets and social media, yet still fail to produce evidence capable of distinguishing between unusual perception and genuinely unknown phenomena.

The 2015 photographs remain the country's most widely discussed urban image case, while the 2013 orange sphere illustrates how the canal's unique maritime setting can encourage interpretations that exceed what the available evidence can support.
