## Introduction

Many reports of unexplained lights over western [Albania]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-albanias-ufo/' | relative_url }}), particularly around Tirana and the Adriatic coast, are intriguing but not necessarily mysterious. The combination of busy air corridors, maritime traffic, broad sea horizons, changing atmospheric conditions and increasing light pollution creates an environment where ordinary objects can appear highly unusual. While some Albanian sightings remain unidentified because too little information was recorded, the available evidence does not establish the presence of extraordinary craft. Instead, most modern reports lack the photographs, precise timings, radar [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}), astronomical checks and witness measurements needed to distinguish between aircraft, celestial bodies, atmospheric optics and genuinely unexplained events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_sightings_in_Albania" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO sightings in Albania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO sightings in Albania</span></span></span>

## The Seman coastguard sighting and why it remains unresolved

One of Albania's best-known coastal UFO reports occurred on **6 March 2007** near Seman in Fier County. According to media interviews, coastguard officer Admir Jemishaj and another officer reported observing a bright object over the sea at approximately 7:15 pm. They described it as remaining apparently stationary for around twenty minutes while changing colour between green, blue and red. The witnesses also reported viewing it through binoculars and estimated that it appeared roughly above the horizon over the Adriatic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_sightings_in_Albania" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO sightings in Albania</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO sightings in Albania</span></span></span>

## Why Albania's western coastline produces unusual-looking lights

The Adriatic coast presents several conditions that regularly produce reports of unfamiliar lights without requiring an extraordinary explanation.

### Long horizons compress distance

Standing on Albania's western beaches, observers often view lights tens of kilometres away over open water. Human depth perception is poor at such distances, making bright sources appear much closer or larger than they really are.

A distant aircraft flying toward the observer may appear motionless for several minutes because its angular position changes very slowly despite travelling hundreds of kilometres per hour. Only when it changes heading does obvious motion become visible.

## Urban lights over Tirana

Reports from Tirana commonly involve bright stationary or slowly moving lights seen shortly after sunset or during clear evenings. The city's expanding urban lighting creates several observational challenges.

Artificial illumination reduces dark adaptation, making bright planets and stars appear unusually prominent while obscuring surrounding reference stars. Without those reference points, observers often overestimate brightness and underestimate distance.

Aircraft approaching Tirana International Airport can also produce prolonged head-on views. Landing lights remain intensely bright over many kilometres and may seem fixed against the sky until the aircraft changes direction. This effect becomes particularly convincing when viewed from elevated neighbourhoods or balconies overlooking the airport approach paths.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.albcontrol.al/al/aip/16-APR-2026-A/2026-04-16-AIRAC/html/eAIP/LA-AD-2.LATI-en-GB.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: albcontrol.al">[Albcontrol]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">albcontrol.al</span><span class="citation-popover-title">LA AD 2.LAT I en GB</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AIP for ALBANIA(section AD-2.LATI)valid from 16 APR 2026...</span></span></span>

## Why colour-changing lights are often atmospheric

Many Albanian reports describe lights shifting between red, green and blue. Although striking, this behaviour is not unusual for bright objects viewed near the horizon.

Several mechanisms can produce this appearance:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Atmospheric scintillation**, where turbulent air rapidly bends different wavelengths of light.
* **Temperature inversions**, which distort the apparent position and brightness of distant lights.
* **Humidity and sea haze**, which scatter light unevenly.
* **Thin cloud layers**, which intermittently dim and brighten objects.

</div>

These effects become strongest shortly after sunset and over long paths above the sea—the same circumstances described in several coastal Albanian reports.

Importantly, apparent colour changes alone provide little evidence regarding an object's physical nature.

## Missing observations limit stronger conclusions

Many Albanian urban and coastal sightings remain officially unexplained not because they demonstrate extraordinary behaviour but because essential observational information was never collected.

The most useful information would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* exact observation time to the minute;
* compass bearing and elevation above the horizon;
* continuous photographs or video with original metadata;
* independent witnesses from separate locations;
* weather observations;
* astronomical sky positions;
* aircraft and marine traffic records.

</div>

Without these, investigators cannot reliably test competing explanations.

This lack of documentation explains why numerous reports remain classified simply as **unidentified** rather than **unexplainable**.

## Using bearings, weather and sky charts to test reports

Modern investigators can often evaluate reports far more effectively than was possible when many Albanian sightings occurred.

A structured assessment typically asks:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Where exactly was the observer looking?** A compass bearing immediately narrows possible aircraft routes, celestial objects and marine traffic.
2. **What was the weather?** Visibility, humidity, cloud layers and atmospheric stability strongly influence apparent brightness and colour.
3. **What astronomical objects were present?** Bright planets such as Venus and stars such as Sirius frequently generate reports of hovering lights, especially near the western horizon after sunset.
4. **Were aircraft or ships present?** Flight tracking, navigation records and known shipping routes can often identify otherwise puzzling observations.
5. **Did multiple independent observers report the same object from different locations?** Triangulation allows investigators to estimate actual altitude and distance rather than relying on subjective impressions.

</div>

When these tests can be performed, many apparently extraordinary lights prove consistent with ordinary aircraft, celestial bodies or atmospheric optics. When they cannot, the case generally remains unresolved because of insufficient evidence rather than because an extraordinary explanation has been demonstrated.
