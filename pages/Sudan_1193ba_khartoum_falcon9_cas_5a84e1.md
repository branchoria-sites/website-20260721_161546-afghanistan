## Introduction

The luminous object seen over Khartoum in the early hours of 8 January 2018 is by far [Sudan]({{ 'what-really-explains-sudans-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s best-known modern UFO incident. Witnesses described a brilliant object surrounded by a green-blue glow, photographs spread rapidly across social media, and early news reports noted that even Sudanese military personnel had not immediately identified what they were seeing. Within days, however, independent satellite trackers reconstructed the event using launch schedules, orbital geometry, witness timing and photographs. Their conclusion was that the display was almost certainly produced by the Falcon 9 upper stage associated with the classified Zuma mission, most likely during a de-orbit burn and propellant venting sequence rather than by an unknown aircraft or extraterrestrial object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sattrackcam.blogspot.com/2018/01/fuel-dump-of-zumas-falcon-9-upper-stage.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sattrackcam.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sattrackcam.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SatTrackCam Leiden (b)log: Fuel dump of Zuma&#x27;s Falcon 9 Upper Stage observed by a Dutch pilot over east Africa (and rumours th...</span></span></span>

The Khartoum case is therefore significant not because it remains unexplained, but because it demonstrates how an apparently extraordinary aerial phenomenon can be investigated using publicly available orbital data and independent technical analysis.

## What witnesses saw over Khartoum

Shortly after 3:00 am local time on 8 January 2018, residents of Khartoum and surrounding areas reported an unusual luminous object crossing the sky. Photographs showed a bright central point enveloped by an expanding greenish or bluish spiral or cloud. The object appeared much larger than a normal satellite and remained visible long enough for numerous observers to photograph it.

Early media reports reflected genuine uncertainty. Sudanese military officials acknowledged reports of an unidentified aerial object over the Wadi Sayidna military area and initially suggested it might have been a satellite while stopping short of making a definitive identification. This cautious response helped fuel public speculation because no detailed explanation accompanied the announcement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://satobs.org/seesat_ref/misc/zuma_vs_falcon9-stage2_clouds_plumes_overview.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: satobs.org">[SATOBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">satobs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">zuma vs falcon9 stage2 clouds plumes overview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GROUND OBSERVATIONS OFOctober 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 18, 2025</span></span></span>

## How satellite trackers reconstructed the event

The strongest evidence emerged not from speculation but from orbital analysis.

Dutch satellite tracker Dr Marco Langbroek received photographs from Peter Horstink, a Boeing 747 pilot flying near Khartoum during the event. Because the pilot could provide both an accurate location and a reliable observation time, the images became especially valuable for reconstructing what had happened. Langbroek compared the observation with the trajectory of the Falcon 9 upper stage launched only hours earlier carrying the classified Zuma payload.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sattrackcam.blogspot.com/2018/01/fuel-dump-of-zumas-falcon-9-upper-stage.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sattrackcam.blogspot.com">[SatTrackCam]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sattrackcam.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SatTrackCam Leiden (b)log: Fuel dump of Zuma&#x27;s Falcon 9 Upper Stage observed by a Dutch pilot over east Africa (and rumours th...</span></span></span>

His reconstruction found several independent lines of agreement:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the aircraft was positioned north of Khartoum at the reported observation time;
* the luminous object moved generally southwards, matching the expected orbital path;
* the sighting occurred shortly before the predicted upper-stage re-entry window;
* the object's appearance matched previously documented rocket-stage fuel venting events.

</div>

Rather than relying on appearance alone, the explanation combined geometry, timing and known spacecraft operations. This convergence is considerably stronger than a simple visual comparison.

Langbroek subsequently refined orbital estimates using the Khartoum observations themselves, showing how the sighting contributed additional information about the probable trajectory of the mission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.satobs.org/seesat/Jan-2018/0068.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: satobs.org">[SATOBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">satobs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zuma orbit estimates from Marco Langbroek via Seesat-l on 2018-01-09 (SeeSat-L Jan-18)January 9, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 9, 2018</span></span></span>

## Why the Falcon 9 explanation fits so well

The leading explanation is not that witnesses observed the secret Zuma satellite itself but that they saw activity from the Falcon 9 second stage after completing its primary mission.

Rocket upper stages commonly perform manoeuvres after payload deployment. These may include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* engine burns to lower the orbit;
* venting unused propellant;
* depressurisation before atmospheric re-entry.

</div>

When expelled into the near-vacuum of space, propellant expands into an enormous cloud. At high altitude this cloud can still be illuminated by sunlight even while observers below remain in darkness. The result can be a spectacular glowing spiral or expanding halo that appears enormous from the ground.

The Khartoum photographs display several characteristics expected from such events:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a bright central source surrounded by a diffuse expanding cloud;
* symmetrical spiral structure produced by vehicle rotation during venting;
* gradual expansion rather than abrupt disappearance;
* colour consistent with illuminated exhaust products.

</div>

Similar "space spirals" have been documented after other Falcon 9 missions and launches by other space agencies, making the Sudan observation part of a recognised class of atmospheric and orbital phenomena rather than a unique anomaly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://satobs.org/seesat_ref/misc/180206_fh_s2_burn3_d2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: satobs.org">[SATOBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">satobs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GROUND OBSERVATIONSNovember 28, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 28, 2025</span></span></span>

## The Zuma mission and why it caused confusion

The classified nature of Zuma contributed significantly to the mystery.

Because the mission involved a secret United States government payload, little official information was released regarding its orbit or operational sequence. Soon after launch, reports emerged suggesting that the satellite may have failed to separate properly from the Falcon 9 upper stage. SpaceX maintained that its launch vehicle had performed normally, while later reporting indicated the payload itself may have been lost because of separation-related problems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arstechnica.com/science/2018/01/the-zuma-satellite-launched-by-spacex-may-be-lost-sources-tell-ars/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arstechnica.com">[arstechnica.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arstechnica.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ars TechnicaIt’s not official, but sources say the secretive Zuma satellite was lost - Ars TechnicaJanuary 8, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 8, 2018</span></span></span>

This distinction is important.

The Khartoum sighting does not depend on knowing the ultimate fate of Zuma. Whether the payload successfully separated or remained attached, the Falcon 9 upper stage was expected to execute post-mission procedures consistent with the observed timing and location. Independent trackers noted that the sighting demonstrated the rocket stage had reached the expected orbital region before its final disposal manoeuvres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/world/the-americas/did-us-government-s-secret-zuma-mission-fail-or-not-1.694462" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Did US government&#x27;s secret Zuma mission fail or not? &#124; The National</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The NationalDid US government&#x27;s secret Zuma mission fail or not? &#124; The NationalJanuary 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 12, 2018</span></span></span>

## What evidence remains uncertain?

Although the overall explanation is strong, some details remain uncertain because of the classified mission.

Open questions include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the precise operational status of the Zuma payload after launch;
* exactly which upper-stage manoeuvre produced the visible cloud;
* whether the venting occurred immediately after a de-orbit burn or during depressurisation before re-entry.

</div>

These uncertainties concern the spacecraft's operations rather than the identity of the luminous object itself. They do not substantially weaken the conclusion that the observed phenomenon was associated with the Falcon 9 upper stage.

## What the Khartoum case shows about UFO investigations

The Khartoum incident illustrates the value of combining witness testimony with independent technical evidence.
