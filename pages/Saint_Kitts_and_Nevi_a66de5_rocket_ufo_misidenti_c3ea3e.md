## What residents saw in November 2024

On the evening of 19 November 2024, numerous people in Saint Kitts and Nevis observed an unusually bright object moving across the sky. Witnesses described a glowing light accompanied by a diffuse halo or expanding cloud. Images and videos circulated rapidly online, with many viewers suggesting the phenomenon might be a UFO.

The timing closely matched SpaceX's sixth Starship flight from Boca Chica, Texas. Although the launch occurred far from the federation, the vehicle climbed into the upper atmosphere where its exhaust plume remained illuminated by the Sun after sunset for observers on the ground. Local reporting subsequently identified the display as the Starship launch rather than an unexplained aerial phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://associatestimes.com/saint-kitts-nevis/spacex-launch-misinterpreted-as-ufo-by-locals-in-st-kitts-and-nevis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: associatestimes.com">[associatestimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">associatestimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Space X launch misinterpreted as UFO by locals in St Kitts and Nevis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SpaceX launch misinterpreted as UFO by locals in St Kitts and Nevis - Associates TimesNovember 20, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 20, 2024</span></span></span>

Unlike many historical UFO reports, this event benefits from a precise launch time, publicly available flight data, extensive imagery from multiple countries and official mission documentation. Those independent lines of [evidence]({{ 'evidence-78f77c/' | relative_url }}) strongly support the rocket explanation.

## How illuminated exhaust creates strange shapes

Rocket launches can produce some of the most convincing "UFO" appearances seen from the ground, particularly during twilight.

The effect depends on simple geometry rather than anything unusual about the vehicle itself. While observers on the ground may already be in darkness, the rocket and its exhaust are often tens or even hundreds of kilometres above Earth, where they remain in direct sunlight. The exhaust particles scatter that sunlight, producing an intensely bright cloud against a dark sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacex.com">[SpaceX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacex.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Space XSpace X</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SpaceX - Starship&#x27;s Sixth Flight Test...</span></span></span>

Several characteristics commonly lead to misidentification:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A brilliant white or blue glow** that appears much brighter than ordinary aircraft.
* **A rapidly expanding circular or jellyfish-shaped cloud** as exhaust disperses in the thin upper atmosphere.
* **Apparent changes in direction or shape**, caused by changing viewing angles and atmospheric winds rather than sudden manoeuvres.
* **A sudden disappearance**, which occurs when the plume enters Earth's shadow or becomes too diffuse to reflect enough sunlight.

</div>

These effects are well documented during launches viewed from large distances and have generated mistaken UFO reports in many parts of the world. The November 2024 observations from Saint Kitts and Nevis fit this pattern closely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacex.com">[SpaceX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacex.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Space XSpace X</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SpaceX - Starship&#x27;s Sixth Flight Test...</span></span></span>

## Comparing the Starship event with the 2023 harbour case

The November 2024 mass sighting also helps explain an earlier report from Basseterre harbour.

### February 2023 harbour observation

A NUFORC report describes four witnesses aboard a yacht seeing a bright disc-like object rising above the harbour with a large luminous plume before the light disappeared abruptly after about two minutes. Rather than leaving the case unexplained, NUFORC classified it as **"Rocket – Probable."** The recorded features include a surrounding haze, a trail and an instantaneous disappearance—features that are characteristic of distant rocket launches viewed at high altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=174790" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[National UFO Reporting Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National UFO Reporting CenterNUFORC UFO Sighting 174790March 6, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 6, 2023</span></span></span>

Although the precise launch responsible has not been formally identified in the public record, the observational characteristics closely resemble known twilight rocket-plume events.

### November 2024 provides a real-world comparison

The Starship sighting effectively serves as a natural demonstration of the same visual mechanism. Residents across the country observed a dramatic luminous display that initially prompted UFO speculation but was later traced to a documented launch with publicly available telemetry and mission data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://associatestimes.com/saint-kitts-nevis/spacex-launch-misinterpreted-as-ufo-by-locals-in-st-kitts-and-nevis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: associatestimes.com">[associatestimes.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">associatestimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Space X launch misinterpreted as UFO by locals in St Kitts and Nevis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SpaceX launch misinterpreted as UFO by locals in St Kitts and Nevis - Associates TimesNovember 20, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 20, 2024</span></span></span>

The comparison strengthens the interpretation of the 2023 harbour report because both events share several notable features:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* a bright concentrated light;
* an extensive glowing plume or halo;
* apparent upward movement;
* disappearance without explosion or debris over Saint Kitts;
* visibility over a relatively short period.

</div>

This does not prove both observations involved the same type of launch vehicle, but it demonstrates that ordinary spaceflight can reproduce exactly the kinds of visual effects that many witnesses initially interpret as extraordinary.

## What these cases mean for the UFO record

The rocket-related incidents are significant because they improve, rather than weaken, understanding of Saint Kitts and Nevis' UFO history.

Instead of dismissing witnesses, they show that sincere observers can accurately describe an unusual visual event while understandably misidentifying its cause. The observations themselves were real; the interpretation changed after independent evidence became available.

For researchers examining the federation's small catalogue of UFO reports, these cases also provide a useful benchmark.
