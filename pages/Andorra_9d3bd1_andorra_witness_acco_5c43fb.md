## Introduction

Among [Andorra]({{ 'what-really-flew-over-andorra/' | relative_url }})'s small body of reported UFO incidents, three retrospective witness accounts associated with Alt de la Capa near Arinsal, Engolasters and the ridge of La Solana occupy an unusual position. They are neither well-investigated cases nor demonstrable hoaxes. Instead, they represent sincere testimony that entered the public record years after the alleged events, without the supporting documentation normally needed to evaluate unusual aerial observations. As a result, they remain unverified rather than confirmed or disproved.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Andorra_9d3bd1_andorra_witness_acco_5c43fb-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Witness Accounts illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Andorra_9d3bd1_andorra_witness_acco_5c43fb-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Andorra_9d3bd1_andorra_witness_acco_5c43fb-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

These reports are important precisely because they illustrate the limits of eyewitness evidence. They offer identifiable locations, an identifiable witness and approximate dates, but lack contemporaneous records, independent corroboration and physical evidence. Within Andorra's broader catalogue of reported aerial phenomena, they therefore serve as examples of how genuine personal experiences can remain permanently inconclusive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.altaveu.com/reportatges/no-es-ovni-es-broma_33950_102.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: altaveu.com">[Altaveu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">altaveu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No és un ovni, és una broma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No és un ovni, és una broma - Altaveu el diari digital d&#x27;AndorraNovember 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2021</span></span></span>

## What Is Missing from the Public Record?

The main obstacle to evaluating these sightings is not contradictory evidence but the absence of evidence that investigators would normally require.

No publicly available record includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* contemporaneous written reports made immediately after the sightings;
* original photographs or video files with verifiable metadata;
* radar observations;
* aviation records linked to the specific events;
* astronomical reconstruction for the precise dates and times;
* detailed weather analysis;
* independent witness statements describing the same objects from different locations.

</div>

Without these materials, it is impossible to test whether the observations corresponded to aircraft, satellites, atmospheric effects, drones, astronomical objects or genuinely unidentified phenomena.

Equally significant is the retrospective nature of the testimony. Because the reports entered the public domain years after the alleged events, investigators cannot easily reconstruct environmental conditions or recover transient data that might once have been available. Memory also becomes more vulnerable to ordinary human processes such as reconstruction, omission and changing interpretation over time.

## Why the Accounts Remain Unverified

Being unverified does not mean the witness fabricated the events. Instead, it reflects a straightforward evidential assessment.

Investigations of unusual aerial reports typically become more reliable when several independent forms of evidence converge. For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* multiple unrelated witnesses report the same event;
* observations are made from different viewpoints;
* photographs can be authenticated;
* aviation or meteorological records match or exclude possible explanations;
* timings can be independently confirmed.

</div>

The Arinsal, Engolasters and La Solana reports currently satisfy none of these criteria in publicly accessible sources. Consequently, they cannot be elevated beyond personal testimony, regardless of the witness's sincerity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.altaveu.com/reportatges/no-es-ovni-es-broma_33950_102.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: altaveu.com">[Altaveu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">altaveu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No és un ovni, és una broma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No és un ovni, és una broma - Altaveu el diari digital d&#x27;AndorraNovember 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2021</span></span></span>

This distinction is particularly important because Andorra's documented UFO history already contains examples where apparently mysterious observations later received conventional explanations. Local reporting recalls the 1967 episode in which supposed UFOs were ultimately identified as Spanish meteorological balloons, demonstrating that unusual appearances alone cannot establish extraordinary causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.altaveu.com/reportatges/no-es-ovni-es-broma_33950_102.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: altaveu.com">[Altaveu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">altaveu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No és un ovni, és una broma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No és un ovni, és una broma - Altaveu el diari digital d&#x27;AndorraNovember 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why Sincere Testimony Can Still Be Inconclusive

Eyewitness accounts remain valuable because they record what observers genuinely believed they experienced. They often preserve details that instruments never captured. Nevertheless, testimony alone has recognised limitations.

Mountain environments such as those around Arinsal and Engolasters can complicate perception in several ways:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* darkness removes depth cues;
* uneven terrain obscures an object's true horizon;
* atmospheric conditions can distort apparent brightness;
* isolated points of light provide little information about size or distance;
* unfamiliar viewing angles make ordinary aircraft or satellites appear unusual.

</div>

These factors do not imply that witnesses are unreliable. Rather, they explain why honest observers can reach different conclusions about the same visual stimulus and why independent corroboration is essential before drawing technical conclusions.

The timing of these reports also limits further investigation. Without precise timestamps and observational data, many potentially useful checks—including satellite passes, aircraft movements and weather reconstruction—cannot be performed with confidence years later.

## What These Cases Contribute to Andorra's UFO Record

The Arinsal, Engolasters and La Solana accounts occupy a middle ground within Andorra's broader catalogue of reported aerial phenomena.

Unlike the 2021 rectangular-light incident, they have never been exposed as an intentional hoax. Unlike the 1967 balloon episode, they have not received a documented conventional explanation. At the same time, they lack the evidence necessary to support claims of anomalous craft or extraordinary technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.altaveu.com/reportatges/no-es-ovni-es-broma_33950_102.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: altaveu.com">[Altaveu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">altaveu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">No és un ovni, és una broma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No és un ovni, és una broma - Altaveu el diari digital d&#x27;AndorraNovember 13, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2021</span></span></span>

For that reason, they are best understood as examples of unresolved witness testimony rather than established UFO incidents. They illustrate how a report can remain genuinely unidentified in the personal sense—meaning the witness could not identify what was seen—without becoming evidence for an objectively unexplained aerial phenomenon.

Within Andorra's small and fragmented body of UFO reporting, these three cases therefore serve less as proof of unusual activity than as reminders of the distinction between a compelling story and a well-documented investigation.
