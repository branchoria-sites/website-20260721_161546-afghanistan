## Introduction

Anyone searching for historical UFO records from [Kiribati]({{ 'what-do-kiribatis-ufo-claims-really-show/' | relative_url }}) quickly encounters an unusual problem: there are almost no official files labelled "UFO". That absence does not necessarily mean unusual aerial observations were never recorded. Rather, Kiribati's administrative history means that any surviving reports are far more likely to be buried inside aviation, meteorological, colonial or maritime records than in a dedicated UFO archive.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Kiribati_3f57ad_kiribati_hidden_ufo_193b20-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Hidden Records illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Kiribati_3f57ad_kiribati_hidden_ufo_193b20-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Kiribati_3f57ad_kiribati_hidden_ufo_193b20-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

For researchers, the challenge is therefore archival rather than conspiratorial. Before independence in 1979, the Gilbert Islands (now the main part of Kiribati) were administered as the Gilbert and Ellice Islands Colony under British rule. Records were dispersed across colonial departments, regional aviation offices and overseas repositories. Finding a genuine aerial observation often depends on searching the right institutions and using the terminology of the period rather than modern UFO language.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C15114" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[Discovery]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commonwealth Office and Foreign and Commonwealth Office: Pacific and Indian Ocean Department and Pacific Dependent Territories D...</span></span></span>

## Why Kiribati's records are unusually fragmented

Unlike countries such as the United Kingdom, Australia or the United States, Kiribati never operated a publicly known national programme for investigating unidentified flying objects. There is no evidence of an equivalent to Britain's Ministry of Defence UFO Desk or the United States' Project Blue Book.

Instead, reports that today might be described as unidentified aerial phenomena would probably have entered government paperwork only if they affected:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

Complicating matters further, Kiribati spans millions of square kilometres of ocean while its population is spread across dozens of distant islands. ## Civil aviation records are one of the strongest places to search

If an unusual aerial object was considered significant, aviation authorities would have had practical reasons to document it regardless of whether anyone believed it represented something extraordinary.

Potential record types include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* pilot reports of unidentified lights;
* air traffic incident logs;
* flight safety investigations;
* notices affecting navigation;
* airport occurrence books;
* communications between airfields.

</div>

One particularly valuable historical source is the 1969 *Aeronautical Information Document for the Gilbert and Ellice Islands Colony*, preserved through the British Library's Endangered Archives Programme. Although it is not a UFO file, it demonstrates that organised aviation documentation existed for the colony and helps identify the administrative structures that handled air navigation. Researchers tracing unusual aerial reports from the late colonial period should therefore investigate aviation documentation before looking for explicit "UFO" files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002841005" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: searcharchives.bl.uk">[British Library Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">searcharchives.bl.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Library Archives EAP110/1/13/2/18</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Library ArchivesEAP110/1/13/2/18 - British Library Archives and Manuscripts Catalogue...</span></span></span>

Post-independence material may also exist within Kiribati's civil aviation administration or regional Pacific aviation bodies if pilots reported unexplained lights or hazards.

## Colonial archives may contain overlooked observations

British colonial administration generated extensive correspondence covering security, policing, communications, shipping and local administration.

The UK National Archives contains numerous record series relating to the Gilbert and Ellice Islands across the Colonial Office, Commonwealth Office and later Foreign and Commonwealth Office collections. Most are catalogued by territory rather than by subject, meaning unusual aerial observations—if reported—would most likely appear as incidental references inside wider administrative files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C15114" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[Discovery]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commonwealth Office and Foreign and Commonwealth Office: Pacific and Indian Ocean Department and Pacific Dependent Territories D...</span></span></span>

Researchers should pay particular attention to files involving:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* district commissioners;
* police reports;
* emergency communications;
* island administration;
* aviation development;
* defence or security correspondence;
* radio communications.

</div>

The important point is that searches using only "UFO" are unlikely to retrieve these records.

## Meteorological and radio records could preserve unexplained events

Weather observers routinely recorded unusual atmospheric phenomena because they affected forecasting and aviation.

For Kiribati, potentially relevant material includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* weather station logs;
* cloud observations;
* lightning reports;
* atmospheric optics;
* visibility records;
* radio interference reports.

</div>

Many historical UFO cases worldwide were later explained through atmospheric phenomena such as bright planets, meteors, unusual cloud illumination or temperature inversions. Consequently, meteorological records are valuable not because they prove extraordinary events but because they can corroborate—or contradict—witness descriptions.

Likewise, radio logs from aviation and maritime services may contain reports of unexplained lights affecting navigation or communications.

## Older place names matter as much as modern ones

One of the most common research mistakes is searching only for "Kiribati".

Before independence, the country appeared under several different administrative names, including:

* Gilbert Islands<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C11151433" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Discovery Kiribati (formerly Gilbert Islands) &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery Kiribati (formerly Gilbert Islands) &#124; The National Archives</span></span></span>
* Gilbert and Ellice Islands * Gilbert and Ellice Islands Colony<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://natlib.govt.nz/records/22308624" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: natlib.govt.nz">[natlib.govt.nz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">natlib.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span> * Western Pacific<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://natlib.govt.nz/records/22355661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: natlib.govt.nz">[natlib.govt.nz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">natlib.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span> * British Western Pacific territories<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C15114" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commonwealth Office and Foreign and Commonwealth Office: Pacific and Indian Ocean Department and Pacific Dependent Territories D...</span></span></span>

Searching these historical names substantially increases the likelihood of finding relevant archival references. UK catalogue entries, for example, continue to organise many colonial records under "Gilbert and Ellice Islands" rather than "Kiribati".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C15114" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[Discovery]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commonwealth Office and Foreign and Commonwealth Office: Pacific and Indian Ocean Department and Pacific Dependent Territories D...</span></span></span>

Similarly, individual islands such as Tarawa, Tabiteuea, Kiritimati (Christmas Island), Banaba (Ocean Island) and Starbuck Island may appear independently in archival catalogues.

## Practical search terms that often work better than "UFO"

Historical researchers generally obtain better results by searching for operational language used at the time.

Useful combinations include:

* "unidentified aircraft"
* "unidentified light"
* "strange light"
* "meteor"
* "fireball"

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* "aircraft incident"
* "pilot report"
* "navigation hazard"
* "radio interference"
* "atmospheric phenomenon"
* "Gilbert Islands aviation"
* "Gilbert and Ellice Islands police"
* "Western Pacific High Commission"<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C4498334" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

</div>

Many genuine sightings were originally recorded as navigational problems or unexplained lights rather than as flying saucers.

## Archives outside Kiribati may be the richest sources

Because of colonial administration and regional record keeping, some of the most useful surviving material may be held outside Kiribati itself.

Likely repositories include:

* **The UK National Archives** for Colonial Office, Commonwealth Office and Foreign Office records relating to the Gilbert and Ellice Islands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C15114" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[Discovery]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commonwealth Office and Foreign and Commonwealth Office: Pacific and Indian Ocean Department and Pacific Dependent Territories D...</span></span></span>
* **The British Library**, particularly the Endangered Archives Programme, which preserves historical administrative and aviation documents from the former colony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002841005" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: searcharchives.bl.uk">[British Library Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">searcharchives.bl.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Library Archives EAP110/1/13/2/18</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Library ArchivesEAP110/1/13/2/18 - British Library Archives and Manuscripts Catalogue...</span></span></span>
* **Pacific Manuscripts Bureau collections**, distributed through partner libraries, which preserve copies of colonial administrative records that may no longer exist locally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://natlib.govt.nz/records/48625711" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: natlib.govt.nz">[National Library of New Zealand]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">natlib.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on govt.nz.</span></span></span>
* Regional aviation archives and weather services, which may retain operational records relevant to unexplained aerial observations.

These repositories are valuable not because they are known to contain UFO investigations, but because they preserve the routine government paperwork in which any genuine reports would most plausibly have been recorded.

## What the missing files probably do—and do not—indicate

The scarcity of accessible Kiribati UFO records is better explained by historical administration than by evidence of concealed investigations.

There is currently no credible evidence that Kiribati maintained a secret national UFO archive that has since disappeared. Instead, the documentary trail appears to reflect three ordinary historical realities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the country's limited administrative capacity before and after independence;
* the dispersal of colonial records across British and regional repositories; and
* the likelihood that unusual aerial observations, if officially noted at all, were filed under aviation, weather, police or administrative subjects rather than under any UFO designation.

</div>

For anyone investigating Kiribati's reported aerial anomalies—including isolated modern cases such as the Tabiteuea sighting—the most productive approach is therefore to reconstruct the historical record through these operational archives rather than expecting to discover a dedicated collection of "UFO files."
