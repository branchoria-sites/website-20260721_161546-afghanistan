## Introduction

The strongest Belgian UFO evidence is not a photograph, a piece of recovered material or a radar recording proving extraordinary flight. Instead, what has survived serious scrutiny is a narrower and more defensible set of facts: a genuine nationwide wave of reports, unusually well-documented police observations, a transparent military response, and a small number of incidents that remain unidentified despite investigation. At the same time, several of the most dramatic claims associated with the Belgian UFO wave have weakened substantially under later analysis. The result is a case that is more interesting—and more cautious—than either believers or sceptics sometimes portray.

The Belgian record therefore offers an important distinction between *confirmed events* (people genuinely reported unusual objects and the authorities genuinely investigated them) and *confirmed explanations* (which are often absent). An unexplained report is not proof of an extraordinary craft, but neither is it automatically evidence of error or fabrication.

## Which Parts of the Belgian Record Are Secure?

Several elements are established beyond reasonable dispute.

**The 1989–1990 UFO wave was real as a social and investigative event.** Thousands of reports were submitted over several months, with concentrations in eastern and central [Belgium]({{ 'what-really-happened-during-belgiums-ufo/' | relative_url }}). The reports came from civilians, police officers and military personnel. Regardless of what witnesses actually observed, the reporting wave itself is historically documented through police records, military correspondence and the extensive investigations conducted by the civilian research organisation SOBEPS.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://decur.org/documents/sobeps-belgian-ufo-wave-1991" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decur.org">[DECUR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SOBEPS Belgian UFO Wave Investigation Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SOBEPS Belgian UFO Wave Investigation Report - DECURJanuary 1, 1991...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1991</span></span></span>

**Belgian authorities responded unusually openly.** Rather than dismissing reports outright, the Belgian Air Force acknowledged receiving credible reports, coordinated with police and launched F-16 intercept missions during the night of 30–31 March 1990. Public briefings by Colonel Wilfried De Brouwer made Belgium one of the few countries where military officials discussed an ongoing UFO investigation in considerable detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufoevidence.com/case/the-belgian-ufo-wave/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoevidence.com">[UFO Evidence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoevidence.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Evidence The Belgian UFO Wave – UFOevidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Evidence The Belgian UFO Wave – UFOevidence</span></span></span>

## Official Actions That Still Matter

One reason the Belgian wave remains prominent is that official actions themselves are well documented.

### Police observations

The initial incidents near Eupen on 29 November 1989 involved experienced gendarmes who filed detailed reports after prolonged observations. Their testimony was recorded through normal reporting channels rather than emerging years later as folklore.

This does not prove that the officers saw an exotic craft. It does demonstrate that trained observers considered the event sufficiently unusual to justify formal reporting and continued investigation.

## Why the Radar Evidence Is Much Weaker Than Often Claimed

The radar episode has frequently been presented as proof that unknown craft performed impossible manoeuvres. That interpretation has not held up particularly well.

Early analyses suggested extraordinary accelerations and rapid altitude changes. These conclusions became widely repeated in books and documentaries.

Later technical reviews, including work commissioned within the Belgian defence establishment, identified significant problems with interpreting the radar data. Analysts concluded that intermittent tracking, radar artefacts and even tracking of the second F-16 could account for some of the apparent anomalies. One influential civilian investigator who had originally regarded the radar traces as highly significant later moderated his conclusions after reviewing the official engineering analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-be-salmon-radar-report-rapport-lt-col-salmon-f16-radar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl be salmon radar report rapport lt col salmon f16 radar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyLt. This does not mean every radar return has been fully explained. Rather, it means the radar evidence no longer supports confident claims that an object demonstrably executed impossible flight characteristics.

The strongest surviving conclusion is therefore modest: the radar data were unusual enough to merit investigation, but they do not constitute proof of extraordinary aerospace performance.

## The Petit-Rechain Photograph Did Not Survive

Perhaps the clearest example of evidence collapsing under scrutiny is the famous Petit-Rechain photograph.

For many years, the image was widely promoted as one of the world's best UFO photographs. It appeared in books, documentaries and scientific discussions because it seemed to depict a structured triangular object with remarkable clarity.

In 2011, the photographer publicly admitted that the image had been fabricated using a painted polystyrene model suspended by thin thread. Although a small minority of researchers continue to question aspects of that confession, the overwhelming historical assessment now treats the photograph as a hoax rather than reliable physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://conspirafy.com/conspiracies/belgian-ufo-wave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: conspirafy.com">[conspirafy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">conspirafy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">belgian ufo wave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belgian UFO wave (29 Nov 1989 - Apr 1990) &#124; Conspirafy...</span></span></span>

Its collapse significantly altered the evidential balance of the Belgian wave. Without the photograph, the case rests almost entirely on witness testimony and official investigation rather than physical documentation.

## Which Witness Patterns Still Deserve Attention?

Although individual sightings remain difficult to verify, several recurring characteristics continue to attract attention because they appeared repeatedly across independent reports.

These include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* large triangular or chevron-shaped arrangements of lights;
* exceptionally slow movement or prolonged hovering;
* very little reported engine noise;
* repeated observations by multiple witnesses separated in location.

</div>

None of these characteristics independently demonstrates an unknown craft. Human perception can converge on similar interpretations under comparable viewing conditions, particularly at night. Nevertheless, the consistency across many reports explains why investigators have continued to regard parts of the Belgian wave as worthy of study rather than dismissing the entire episode as isolated misidentifications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://decur.org/documents/sobeps-belgian-ufo-wave-1991" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decur.org">[DECUR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SOBEPS Belgian UFO Wave Investigation Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SOBEPS Belgian UFO Wave Investigation Report - DECURJanuary 1, 1991...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1991</span></span></span>

## Conventional Explanations That Weakened Many Cases

Serious investigation also identified several ordinary mechanisms capable of explaining substantial portions of the wave.

These include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* bright stars and planets observed under unusual atmospheric conditions;
* helicopters and military aircraft viewed at night;
* advertising lights and searchlights;
* optical perspective errors affecting estimates of speed and distance;
* media influence increasing reporting after widely publicised sightings.

</div>

No single conventional explanation accounts for every report. However, investigators increasingly recognised that different incidents probably had different causes rather than sharing one common origin. ## Why Belgium Still Matters

Belgium remains one of the most instructive national UFO case studies not because it produced definitive evidence of extraordinary vehicles, but because it combined extensive eyewitness testimony with documented official investigation. Few countries have such a well-preserved record of police reports, military involvement and public briefings occurring during an active sighting wave.

The surviving evidence therefore supports a restrained conclusion. Belgium provides strong evidence that a significant number of people—including trained observers—experienced unusual aerial events that prompted legitimate official concern. It does **not** provide equally strong evidence that those events involved craft displaying verified extraordinary capabilities or technology beyond known human engineering. The distinction between those two propositions is precisely what has survived the closest scrutiny.
