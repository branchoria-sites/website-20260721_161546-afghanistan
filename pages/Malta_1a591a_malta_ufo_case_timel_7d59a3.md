## Introduction

[Malta]({{ 'what-really-lies-behind-maltas-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history is surprisingly rich for a small island nation, but it is also unusually fragmented. Unlike countries with military reporting systems or official archives, Malta's record survives through nineteenth-century newspaper reports, local journalism, private researchers, photographs, eyewitness accounts and occasional commentary from astronomers. The result is a chronology that contains several genuinely interesting incidents but very little evidence capable of supporting extraordinary conclusions.

From the famous 1845 report of luminous objects emerging from the sea to modern smartphone videos, airport sightings and organised skywatching groups, Malta's cases illustrate a recurring pattern. Many reports initially appeared mysterious yet later received plausible explanations involving astronomy, meteors or atmospheric effects. Others remain technically "unidentified" only because the surviving evidence is too limited to reach a confident conclusion. Rather than demonstrating extraterrestrial visitation, Malta's UFO record provides a valuable case study in how unusual observations are documented, interpreted and debated over nearly two centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.independent.com.mt/articles/2024-01-07/blogs-opinions/What-are-they-6736257573" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: independent.com.mt">[com.mt]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">independent.com.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Malta Independent What are they?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Malta IndependentWhat are they? - The Malta IndependentJanuary 7, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 7, 2024</span></span></span>

## The earliest recorded case: the 1845 maritime lights

The earliest widely cited Maltese UFO-related event occurred on **18 June 1845** aboard a brig sailing near Malta during the evening. According to contemporary newspaper accounts later reproduced by researchers, witnesses described intense heat, a sulphurous smell and three glowing bodies rising from the sea roughly half a mile from the vessel. The luminous objects reportedly remained visible for around ten minutes before disappearing.

Because the report predates the modern concept of flying saucers by more than a century, later writers have interpreted it in very different ways. Some UFO enthusiasts regard it as evidence that unusual aerial phenomena have always existed. More cautious historians emphasise that nineteenth-century observers lacked modern recording methods and that the original documentation provides few details about weather conditions, the vessel's precise location or the appearance of the lights beyond broad descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.independent.com.mt/articles/2024-01-07/blogs-opinions/What-are-they-6736257573" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: independent.com.mt">[The Malta Independent]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">independent.com.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Malta Independent What are they?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Malta IndependentWhat are they? - The Malta IndependentJanuary 7, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 7, 2024</span></span></span>

One proposed natural explanation links the event to geological activity between Sicily and Malta. The region sits within a complex tectonic environment, and some researchers have suggested that submarine volcanic gases or other luminous geophysical phenomena could account for both the reported glow and the sulphurous odour. However, no direct evidence confirms volcanic activity at the reported location and time, so this remains a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated solution.

The historical importance of the case lies less in proving an unexplained craft than in showing that unusual maritime light phenomena were being documented in Maltese waters long before UFO culture existed.

## How Malta's record reappears in the late twentieth century

After scattered nineteenth-century references, Malta's surviving UFO chronology becomes sparse until the late twentieth century. This gap almost certainly reflects the loss or absence of systematic archives rather than an absence of unusual observations.

One of the best-known modern incidents occurred in **October 1989**, when *The Times* published a photograph showing a lozenge-shaped object above Xemxija Bay. According to the original report, the photographer noticed a flash through the camera viewfinder while taking the picture. Looking up immediately afterwards, only a tiny speck remained before apparently disappearing.

The image attracted attention because there was no immediate explanation. However, the surviving academic discussion treated it as an exercise in perception rather than evidence of an alien craft. The accompanying analysis stressed an important point that is often forgotten: the label **"unidentified flying object"** simply indicated that the object in the photograph had not been identified. It said nothing about its size, distance, speed or origin.

Possible explanations discussed over the years have included photographic artefacts, processing anomalies, misidentified airborne objects and deliberate manipulation. None has been proven conclusively, but neither has any evidence emerged demonstrating an extraordinary object. The photograph therefore remains historically significant while offering little scientific information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/25155/1/Various%20Ways%20of%20Seeing%20an%20UFO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: um.edu.mt">[L-Università ta&#x27; Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">um.edu.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">L-Università ta&#x27; Malta VARIOUS WAYS OF SEEING AN UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L-Università ta&#x27; Malta VARIOUS WAYS OF SEEING AN UFO</span></span></span>

## Photographs, fireballs and airport sightings

The twenty-first century produced more frequent reports as digital cameras and mobile phones became commonplace. While the quantity of material increased, the overall quality of evidence generally did not.

### The 2009 "mystery light"

During February and March 2009, many Maltese residents reported an exceptionally bright object visible soon after sunset. Witnesses described a brilliant light apparently hovering over familiar landmarks before slowly changing position.

Astronomers identified the object as **Venus**, which was then exceptionally bright in the evening sky. Atmospheric turbulence near the horizon can make the planet shimmer, change colour and appear to move erratically. Observer movement relative to nearby buildings further strengthens the illusion that the object itself is travelling. The timing, brightness and direction all matched the known position of Venus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://timesofmalta.com/article/whats-that-mysterious-light-in-the-night-sky.249960" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofmalta.com">[Times of Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofmalta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Times of Malta What&#x27;s that mysterious light in the night sky?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Times of Malta What&#x27;s that mysterious light in the night sky?</span></span></span>

This episode demonstrates one of the most common causes of UFO reports worldwide: familiar astronomical objects viewed under unusual atmospheric conditions.

### The 2011 Birżebbuġa fireball

Another widely discussed case occurred in **August 2011**, when residents filmed a burning object crossing the sky near Birżebbuġa. Witnesses described it as resembling a flaming balloon or fiery object moving rapidly towards the coast.

The Astronomical Society of Malta concluded that the most likely explanation was a **bright meteor (fireball)**, noting that the observation occurred during the period of the annual Perseid meteor shower. Fireballs frequently appear dramatically larger and closer than they really are because observers lack reliable distance references at night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://timesofmalta.com/article/Burning-UFO-thought-to-be-a-fireball.381693" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofmalta.com">[Times of Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofmalta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Times of Malta Burning UFO thought to be a fireball</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Times of MaltaBurning UFO thought to be a fireballAugust 25, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2011</span></span></span>

Unlike many UFO stories, this case illustrates the value of expert review. The available observations matched a well-understood natural phenomenon without requiring speculative explanations.

### Airport and coastal videos

From roughly 2010 onwards, online forums and local media began circulating numerous photographs and videos allegedly showing unusual objects over Malta International Airport, coastal areas and Gozo.

These reports vary greatly in quality. Some consist of distant lights filmed with mobile phones; others show objects whose size and distance cannot be estimated because the footage lacks reference points. Several have later been interpreted as aircraft, satellites, drones, Chinese lanterns or optical effects, while others remain simply too ambiguous to classify.

Importantly, none of the publicly available airport-related footage has been accompanied by supporting radar data, air traffic documentation or multiple calibrated observations that would allow independent verification. As a result, these cases remain unresolved primarily because the available evidence is insufficient, not because it demonstrates unknown technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.independent.com.mt/articles/2024-01-07/blogs-opinions/What-are-they-6736257573" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: independent.com.mt">[The Malta Independent]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">independent.com.mt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Malta Independent What are they?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Malta IndependentWhat are they? - The Malta IndependentJanuary 7, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 7, 2024</span></span></span>

## Modern gatherings and organised contact claims

Malta's contemporary UFO culture extends beyond isolated sightings into organised communities interested in extraterrestrial life.

In recent years, local groups have arranged **CE-5 (Close Encounters of the Fifth Kind)** gatherings, where participants meditate collectively in the hope of establishing peaceful contact with extraterrestrial intelligence. These events typically involve night-time skywatching combined with guided meditation rather than scientific observation.

Media coverage has documented these gatherings as social and cultural events rather than validated contact experiences. Organisers describe personal experiences and unusual lights, while also acknowledging that many observations turn out to be satellites, aircraft or astronomical objects. No independently verified evidence has emerged from these meetings demonstrating communication with non-human intelligence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://timesofmalta.com/article/in-malta-countryside-group-meditates-make-contact-aliens.1112641" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofmalta.com">[Times of Malta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofmalta.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Times of MaltaIn Malta&#x27;s countryside, a group meditates to make contact with aliensJuly 15, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2025</span></span></span>

The growth of these gatherings nevertheless reflects an important shift in Malta's UFO history. Earlier reports were largely accidental sightings, whereas modern interest increasingly includes organised communities actively seeking unusual experiences.

## Why many Maltese sightings remain unresolved

Several factors make Malta an unusually challenging environment for investigating unidentified aerial reports.

The islands experience:

* Heavy commercial and military air traffic crossing the central Mediterranean.
* Frequent visibility of satellites, including large Starlink groups.
* Bright planets and low-horizon astronomical objects over the sea.
* Regular meteor activity.
* Increasing drone use.
* Severe light pollution across much of the archipelago, reducing accurate night-sky observation while increasing glare and visual distortions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04435" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv A photometric mapping of the night sky brightness of the Maltese islands</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A photometric mapping of the night sky brightness of the Maltese islandsFebruary 11, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2020</span></span></span>

In addition, many historical cases survive only as newspaper summaries or recollections rather than complete investigative files. Essential information—camera metadata, precise witness locations, weather records, radar tracks or independent observations—is often unavailable.

Consequently, "unidentified" frequently means **there is not enough evidence to determine what was observed**, rather than evidence for an extraordinary craft.

## Which Malta cases still defy explanation?

When assessed using modern investigative standards, very few Maltese reports qualify as strong unexplained cases.

The strongest historical candidate remains the **1845 maritime lights**, not because they clearly indicate an unknown craft, but because the event combines multiple reported phenomena—luminous objects, apparent emergence from the sea and an unusual smell—for which no definitive explanation exists. Unfortunately, the historical record is too incomplete for modern analysis.

The **1989 Xemxija photograph** remains visually intriguing but scientifically weak because the single image cannot establish the object's physical characteristics.

Most twenty-first century reports are considerably better documented visually but are generally easier to explain through known causes such as Venus, meteors, aircraft, drones or imaging artefacts.

Taken together, Malta's chronology demonstrates that unexplained observations do occur, yet the surviving evidence rarely reaches the level needed to distinguish genuinely anomalous events from ordinary phenomena viewed under unusual circumstances. The country's UFO history therefore remains valuable primarily as a record of changing eyewitness experiences, photographic technology and public interpretation rather than as evidence confirming extraterrestrial visitation.
