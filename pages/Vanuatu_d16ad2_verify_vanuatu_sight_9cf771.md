## Introduction

Verifying a reported UFO sighting in [Vanuatu]({{ 'what-really-explains-vanuatus-ufo/' | relative_url }}) is usually less about proving an extraordinary explanation than determining whether enough reliable evidence exists to identify an ordinary one. Because the country has relatively few documented cases, limited public investigative records and many reports that emerge only through social media or retellings, the quality of the original observation is often the deciding factor. A sighting that includes an exact time, location, viewing direction, duration and original photographs can frequently be checked against aircraft, satellites, astronomical objects and weather conditions. By contrast, reports that lack these details often remain permanently unresolved—not because they are necessarily unusual, but because there is no reliable way to reconstruct what happened. The sparse public record associated with Vanuatu, including the widely repeated Bunlap report, illustrates how missing information prevents meaningful investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flightradar24.com/how-it-works/ads-b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flightradar24.com">[Flightradar24]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flightradar24.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Automatic Dependent Surveillance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Automatic Dependent Surveillance - Broadcast (ADS-B) &#124; Flightradar24...</span></span></span>

## Record the observation before discussing it

The first few minutes after a sighting are the most important. Memories change quickly, especially once witnesses compare accounts or read online speculation. A useful report should therefore preserve the original observation before interpretation.

Record:

## Check flights, satellites and weather first

Most unusual aerial observations can be investigated by comparing the reported time and location with known activity.

### Aircraft

Commercial aircraft, many private aircraft and some government flights broadcast Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast (ADS-B) signals that can be reconstructed through flight-tracking services. These systems record an aircraft's position, altitude, heading and speed, allowing investigators to determine whether a visible light or object matches a known flight. ADS-B coverage has expanded significantly, although remote oceanic regions may still contain gaps and some military or specially restricted aircraft may not appear in public databases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flightradar24.com/how-it-works/ads-b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flightradar24.com">[flightradar24.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flightradar24.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Automatic Dependent Surveillance</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Automatic Dependent Surveillance - Broadcast (ADS-B) &#124; Flightradar24...</span></span></span>

When checking a Vanuatu sighting, compare:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The exact observation time.
* Flight direction relative to the witness.
* Aircraft altitude.
* Whether the aircraft was approaching head-on, which can create the illusion of a stationary bright light.

</div>

### Satellites and space objects

Bright satellites, newly launched satellite trains and occasional re-entry events regularly generate UFO reports worldwide. Satellite prediction services can reconstruct which spacecraft were visible from a specific location at a given time. Recent research has shown that even experienced airline crews have mistaken unusual satellite formations for unidentified aerial phenomena before reconstruction demonstrated they were newly launched satellites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08155" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Enhancing Space Situational Awareness to Mitigate Risk: A Single-Case Study in the Misidentification of a Recently-Launched Starlink...</span></span></span>

For Vanuatu, this is particularly relevant because many satellite passes occur over the Pacific and can be exceptionally bright under favourable illumination.

### Astronomy and weather

Bright planets near the horizon, meteors, atmospheric optics, lightning and unusual cloud illumination can all appear unfamiliar under tropical conditions.

Weather archives should be checked for:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Thunderstorms.
* High cloud layers.
* Temperature inversions.
* Lightning activity.
* Rain showers capable of producing unusual reflections.

</div>

Astronomical software can determine whether Venus, Jupiter, the Moon or a meteor shower was visible from the reported location at the time.

## Preserve evidence that can actually be analysed

Many online UFO videos cannot be evaluated because critical information has already been lost.

Whenever possible:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Keep the original image or video rather than edited copies.
* Do not crop the frame before investigators have examined it.
* Save multiple photographs instead of only the clearest-looking frame.
* Record surrounding landmarks to establish scale and viewing direction.
* Photograph the same location in daylight to aid reconstruction.

</div>

Modern smartphones normally embed metadata including capture time and approximate location. Removing this information unnecessarily makes later verification much harder.

If several witnesses are present, each person should write an independent account before discussing what they saw. Consistent independent observations are generally more informative than a single agreed narrative produced afterwards.

## Common social-media pitfalls

The limited public history of Vanuatu UFO reports shows how quickly unsupported stories can spread once copied across websites. Multiple webpages repeating the same claim do not create independent evidence if they all originate from a single unverified source.

Frequent problems include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Missing observation times.
* Unknown witness identities.
* Videos uploaded long after the event.
* Compressed or edited media.
* Screenshots instead of original files.
* Claims without identifiable locations.
* Headlines that exaggerate what the witness actually described.

</div>

The frequently cited Bunlap account demonstrates these limitations. Although widely reproduced online, the publicly available versions do not provide the detailed observational data, original local reporting or physical evidence required for a modern reconstruction. As a result, the event remains insufficiently documented rather than verified or disproved.

## How investigators would assess credibility

A practical way to classify a Vanuatu sighting is to separate the quality of the observation from the explanation.

A report becomes substantially more credible when it includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Multiple independent witnesses.
* Precise timing.
* Original photographs or video.
* Consistent witness descriptions.
* Identifiable viewing location.
* Successful elimination of known aircraft, satellites and astronomical objects.

</div>

Conversely, confidence decreases when:

* The event is reported weeks later.
* No original media survive.
* Witness accounts changed after discussion.
* The location or direction is vague.
* Only heavily edited social-media clips remain.

Importantly, an unexplained report is not the same as evidence for an extraordinary object. In countries such as Vanuatu, where historical documentation is sparse and few investigations have been conducted, many reports remain unresolved simply because essential observational information was never recorded in the first place.

## Why careful documentation matters in Vanuatu

Vanuatu's geography makes reconstruction both important and challenging. The nation consists of widely dispersed islands separated by large stretches of ocean, with comparatively limited public monitoring infrastructure and relatively few documented historical investigations. Once a witness forgets the precise time or original media are lost, later investigators often cannot determine whether the observation corresponded to a flight, satellite, meteorological event or something genuinely unidentified.

For that reason, the strongest contribution a witness can make is not to speculate about what was seen but to preserve enough reliable information that competing explanations can be tested objectively. In the context of Vanuatu's limited historical record, a well-documented observation is considerably more valuable than a dramatic but poorly recorded claim.
