## Introduction

The central problem is not that every Pakistani sighting has been conclusively explained. It is that most reports lack the radar records, original high-resolution imagery, precise viewing geometry and independent sensor data needed to reach a firm conclusion. “Unidentified” therefore usually means that the available information is insufficient, not that an extraordinary craft has been established. Pakistan’s record is best understood as a fragmented aviation and public-reporting history in which a few interesting cases remain unresolved at the documentary level, while several prominent claims have plausible conventional [explanations]({{ 'explanations-c6c2f0/' | relative_url }}).

## What Pakistan’s UFO record actually contains

There is no publicly visible Pakistani equivalent of the United States’ All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office or France’s civilian UFO investigation programme. Searches of accessible material from Pakistan’s aviation, defence and space institutions do not reveal a dedicated national archive, standard public reporting system or official series of UAP assessments. This does not prove that military or aviation authorities hold no relevant records; air-traffic, radar and security reports may be restricted or retained as ordinary operational material. It does mean that researchers cannot reconstruct a dependable national chronology from official releases alone.

## The incidents that shaped public attention

### The 2019 Karachi near-miss was probably a drone

On 18 January 2019, the crew of a PIA aircraft departing Karachi for Sukkur reported an object close to the aeroplane shortly after take-off. Contemporary reporting described it as hovering roughly 100 feet above the aircraft while the plane was at about 2,500 feet. The captain reportedly suspected a drone, and the sighting was passed to Karachi air-traffic control. Dawn presented the event as a dangerous drone encounter rather than an extraterrestrial mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dawn.com/news/1458420" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dawn.com">[Dawn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dawn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PIA plane escapes being hit by mysterious drone</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PIA plane escapes being hit by mysterious droneJanuary 19, 2019 — 19 Jan 2019 — A drone was sighted hovering over a Sukkur-bound comm...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 19, 2019</span></span></span>

This case matters because it is sometimes retrospectively added to lists of Pakistani “UFO encounters”. Technically, the object was unidentified at first, but the surrounding circumstances strongly favour an unauthorised unmanned aircraft: low altitude, proximity to an airport, a close observation by a flight crew and an immediate safety concern. Nothing publicly reported indicates exotic movement, unusual emissions or performance beyond known drone technology.

The incident also highlights a recurring confusion. “UFO” can describe any airborne object not yet identified, whereas popular culture treats the term as shorthand for an alien vehicle. In aviation, an unidentified drone near a flight path is both mundane in origin and potentially lethal in consequence.

### The 2021 Rahim Yar Khan object remains inadequately documented

Pakistan’s most widely publicised case occurred on 23 January 2021 during PIA flight PK-304 from Karachi to Lahore. The pilots recorded a bright, roughly circular object at about 4pm near Rahim Yar Khan and reported it to the control room. A PIA spokesperson confirmed the report but stressed that the airline could not say what the object was. Accounts also placed the observation broadly between Multan and Sahiwal, reflecting some inconsistency in how the flight’s location was reported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geo.tv/latest/332053-pia-pilots-see-ufo-on-domestic-flight" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geo.tv">[geo.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geo.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geo NewsUFO spotted by Pakistani pilots near Rahim Yar Khan, new...January 27, 2021 — 27 Jan 2021 — A PIA spokesperson said that the UFO...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 27, 2021</span></span></span>

The video became unusually influential because it came from an airline cockpit and appeared clearer than many casual UFO clips. Yet its evidential value remains limited. Publicly circulated footage does not establish the object’s distance, dimensions, altitude or speed. A small nearby object and a much larger distant object can occupy the same apparent area in an image. Brightness and camera zoom can also turn balloons, aircraft reflections and atmospheric effects into apparently solid discs.

Some commentary proposed a weather balloon or a reflective balloon-like object. That explanation is plausible, especially in daylight, but it has not been demonstrated through a publicly released reconstruction matching weather data, balloon launches, radar information and the aircraft’s exact position. The honest classification is therefore **unresolved because of insufficient data**, not confirmed anomalous technology.

The episode received later entertainment coverage, but television re-analysis does not substitute for original flight records. The decisive evidence would include uncompressed footage, cockpit timestamps, bearing changes, aircraft attitude, air-traffic radar returns and meteorological information. None has been released as a complete public case file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaQ6ZWp6QFY" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[YouTube]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on youtube.com.</span></span></span>

### The 2022 Islamabad “triangle” has a strong kite explanation

In January 2022, Islamabad resident Arslan Warraich filmed a dark object that reportedly remained visible for around two hours. International coverage described it as triangular or “bulging”, and the lengthy footage attracted attention because the object appeared nearly stationary over the capital.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.indiatoday.in/world/story/ufo-video-object-islamabad-skies-leave-internet-abuzz-watch-1916838" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: indiatoday.in">[indiatoday.in]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">indiatoday.in</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on indiatoday.in.</span></span></span>

The duration initially sounds impressive, but it weighs against some dramatic interpretations. A tethered object can remain in approximately the same area for hours, whereas an advanced vehicle hovering openly over Islamabad would be expected to generate independent observations, air-traffic information or security reporting. The video also lacks a dependable scale reference.

A detailed online discussion at Metabunk identified a large kite as a plausible explanation, noting the shape, motion and timing around the kite-flying season in neighbouring Rawalpindi. That assessment is not an official determination, but it fits the footage without requiring exceptional technology. The case is consequently better described as **probably a kite or other tethered object**, although definitive identification would require recovery of the object or independent imaging from another location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/islamabad-ufo-seen-for-over-2-hours.12293/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Islamabad UFO Seen for over 2 hours &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Islamabad UFO Seen for over 2 hours &#124; Metabunk</span></span></span>

## Why geography changes the likely explanations

Pakistan’s reports cluster in heavily populated or aviation-connected areas: Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad–Rawalpindi, southern Punjab and routes linking major cities. That distribution may reflect where unusual aerial activity occurs, but it more obviously reflects where people, cameras, airports and commercial flights are concentrated.

Different regions also produce different identification problems:

**Karachi and major airports.** Dense air traffic, drones, birds, balloons and coastal haze complicate visual judgement. A low-altitude unknown near an airport should first be assessed as an aviation-security problem.

**Punjab’s inter-city flight corridors.** Pilot sightings can involve reflections, distant aircraft, balloons or astronomical objects viewed through changing layers of haze. The flat landscape offers long sightlines but few visual cues for estimating distance.

**Islamabad and Rawalpindi.** Kites, tethered objects, security aircraft and drones provide several plausible sources of apparently hovering shapes. Hills and variable cloud layers can further distort apparent motion.

**Western border regions.** Military surveillance, drones and restricted operations make reports especially difficult to verify. Claims involving the Afghanistan–Pakistan frontier may also be based on foreign military sensors rather than Pakistani witnesses or records.

A thermal video released publicly in 2025 was said by journalist Jeremy Corbell to show a disc-shaped UAP filmed by the US military near the Afghanistan–Pakistan border on 23 November 2020. Reports repeated claims that the object changed direction and lacked an obvious heat signature. However, the full sensor data, chain of custody, range calculations and official analytical record were not made public alongside the clip. The location is also described only as a border region, so it cannot securely be classified as a Pakistani national incident. It belongs in the wider regional record but remains a contested, externally sourced claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lKYWrakDclw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[youtube.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on youtube.com.</span></span></span>

## Official records are the largest missing piece

The 2019 and 2021 PIA events reportedly reached air-traffic control, suggesting that operational records may once have existed. Yet no complete radar plots, controller transcripts, flight-data reconstructions or final investigative findings have been published. Media reports confirm that observations were reported; they do not show what authorities subsequently established.

Pakistan’s fragmented public record may arise from several factors. Aviation authorities focus on safety rather than public UFO cataloguing. Military radar information is sensitive. Older files may not be digitised or indexed under terms such as “UFO” or “UAP”. Reports suspected to involve drones may be handled as airspace violations, while lights judged insignificant may never progress beyond routine logs.

This absence produces two opposite errors. Enthusiasts may interpret missing files as evidence of concealment. Sceptics may treat the absence of published conclusions as proof that nothing unusual occurred. Neither follows logically. The correct conclusion is narrower: the publicly accessible record is too incomplete to determine how many reports were investigated, resolved or retained.

No credible public evidence currently shows that Pakistan’s government has confirmed an extraterrestrial craft, recovered non-human material or operated a formal alien-contact programme. Claims of that kind circulate online without supporting documents, identifiable officials or verifiable physical evidence.

## How the strongest claims should be graded

A useful evidence scale separates the reality of an observation from claims about its cause.

**Confirmed observation, probably conventional:** The 2019 Karachi case involved a real report by an identifiable commercial crew and an immediate aviation response. Available details favour a drone.

**Confirmed report, unresolved in public records:** The 2021 Rahim Yar Khan event was acknowledged by PIA and supported by video, but the object’s physical characteristics cannot be established from the released material.

**Recorded object, probably explained:** The Islamabad footage documents something in the sky, but its long duration and appearance are compatible with a kite or tethered object.

**Unverified witness material:** NUFORC and social-media accounts may be sincere, but most lack independent corroboration and cannot support strong conclusions.

**Contested regional military claim:** The 2020 border video may be authentic sensor footage, yet extraordinary descriptions of its size or manoeuvring depend on information that has not been independently released.

This distinction matters because unresolved cases are often treated as a single mysterious category. In reality, a case may remain open simply because nobody recorded the necessary measurements. NASA’s independent UAP study concluded that the limited number of high-quality observations prevents firm scientific conclusions and recommended standardised, multi-sensor data collection. US defence investigations similarly report that many resolved cases are balloons, birds, drones, satellites or aircraft, while a smaller group remains under review because the evidence is incomplete. These findings do not settle Pakistani cases, but they provide a sound standard for judging them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span></span></span>

## What credible investigation in Pakistan would require

Pakistan is well placed to improve the quality of its UFO record because it already has civil aviation systems, meteorological services, astronomical expertise and extensive airspace monitoring. The missing element is a transparent procedure for combining those sources without sensationalism.

A credible case file would record the exact time and coordinates; the witness’s bearing and elevation angle; the observer’s movement; weather and visibility; original, uncompressed imagery; known flights and drone activity; astronomical objects; balloon releases; and any radar or infrared detection. Two geographically separated observations would permit triangulation, turning an apparent shape into an estimate of distance, altitude and velocity.

Scientific UAP projects emphasise multiple simultaneous sensors because an isolated camera can produce misleading impressions through glare, focus, compression and parallax. Useful equipment can include wide- and narrow-field cameras, infrared instruments, radar receivers, radio-frequency monitors and environmental sensors. Corroboration across independent systems is more important than how striking a single image appears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

For Pakistan, the most practical improvement would be a civilian-facing reporting channel linked to aviation and scientific reviewers. Public summaries could protect sensitive radar capabilities while stating whether a case was attributed to a drone, balloon, aircraft, astronomical object, insufficient information or a genuine unresolved anomaly. That structure would help distinguish air-safety hazards from folklore and prevent the same viral clip from being repeatedly presented as a new incident.

## The strongest conclusion supported by Pakistan's UFO record
Pakistan has genuine reports of unidentified objects, including observations by professional pilots, but no publicly demonstrated case establishes technology beyond known human or natural causes. Its most famous events range from a likely drone and a probable kite to a cockpit video that remains unresolved because the necessary measurements were never released.

The enduring mystery is therefore institutional as much as aerial. Pakistan lacks an accessible, systematic archive that would allow researchers to compare incidents across time, regions and sensor types. Until original records are preserved and analysed transparently, the national UFO story will continue to be shaped by isolated news reports and compressed videos. Some objects will remain unidentified, but that status alone cannot bear the weight of claims about extraterrestrial visitors.
