## Introduction

On the night of **7 August 1962**, people across **Kampala** watched a brilliant greenish-blue object race across the sky before apparently breaking into pieces. The spectacle was dramatic enough that many observers initially described it as an unidentified flying object (UFO). However, unlike many historical UFO reports that remain unresolved, this incident was rapidly assessed by officials as a **large fireball meteor** entering Earth's atmosphere. As a result, it stands as the strongest documented Ugandan UFO case precisely because it also has the strongest conventional explanation. The event illustrates how an observation can legitimately begin as "unidentified" while ultimately receiving a well-supported scientific interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/American%20UFO%20Publications/Weekly%20Research%20Magazine%27s%20Look-See/Vol%205%20No%203.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE v S LOOK-S.EE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE vS LOOK-S.EESeptember 22, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 22, 2016</span></span></span>

## What witnesses reported over Kampala

Available contemporary accounts describe a striking nocturnal object moving rapidly across the Kampala sky. The most frequently cited eyewitness was **American Consul-General Hendrik Van Oss**, who reportedly described it as a **large object emitting a greenish-blue light**. He also noted that it appeared to **break apart** while travelling across the sky. These observations were subsequently summarised in *Look-See*, a 1962 UFO research periodical, which attributed its report to the *San Diego Union* of 9 August 1962.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/American%20UFO%20Publications/Weekly%20Research%20Magazine%27s%20Look-See/Vol%205%20No%203.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE v S LOOK-S.EE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE vS LOOK-S.EESeptember 22, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 22, 2016</span></span></span>

## Why fragmentation points to a fireball

The reported break-up is the strongest piece of evidence supporting a meteor explanation.

A **fireball** is an exceptionally bright meteor produced when a relatively large meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere at tremendous speed. As aerodynamic pressure and heating increase, the object often fractures into multiple glowing pieces. To observers on the ground, this can create the impression that a single luminous object suddenly splits apart or explodes.

Several aspects of the Kampala observation closely match this well-understood process:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* **Greenish-blue colour.** Bright meteors frequently display green or blue-green colours. The precise hue depends on factors including atmospheric ionisation and the chemical composition of the meteoroid.
* **High apparent speed.** Meteors travel far faster than conventional aircraft, often crossing large portions of the sky within seconds.
* **Visible fragmentation.** Breaking into multiple glowing fragments is one of the defining characteristics of larger fireballs as they disintegrate during atmospheric entry.
* **Single uninterrupted path.** The reports describe continuous travel rather than intelligent manoeuvring or sustained flight.

</div>

Because these features fit the expected behaviour of a large meteor so closely, meteorological and geological officials reportedly concluded that the object was **a gigantic meteorite or meteor**, rather than an unknown aircraft or extraordinary phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/American%20UFO%20Publications/Weekly%20Research%20Magazine%27s%20Look-See/Vol%205%20No%203.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE v S LOOK-S.EE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE vS LOOK-S.EESeptember 22, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 22, 2016</span></span></span>

## Why the event entered UFO catalogues

The Kampala fireball nevertheless appears in numerous UFO compilations. This can seem contradictory until the terminology is understood.

A **UFO** simply refers to an object that is **unidentified at the time of observation**. It does not imply extraterrestrial origin or that the object remained unexplained after investigation.

In the Kampala case, witnesses initially did not know what they had seen. Because of that initial uncertainty, UFO publications collected the report alongside other unexplained aerial sightings. Later summaries preserved the incident largely because it represented an interesting historical sighting rather than because it remained mysterious. The same archival issue of *Look-See* that reported the sighting also carried the official assessment identifying it as a meteor, illustrating that even UFO publications sometimes documented conventional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/American%20UFO%20Publications/Weekly%20Research%20Magazine%27s%20Look-See/Vol%205%20No%203.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE v S LOOK-S.EE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE vS LOOK-S.EESeptember 22, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 22, 2016</span></span></span>

This distinction is important when reading historical catalogues. Many databases record sightings based on the witness's initial perception rather than on the final investigative conclusion.

## How convincing is the meteor explanation?

Among [Uganda]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-ugandas-skies/' | relative_url }})'s historical UFO reports, the 1962 Kampala event enjoys unusually strong evidential support for a conventional explanation.

Several factors strengthen that assessment:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Contemporary reporting.** The surviving account was published only days after the event rather than being reconstructed decades later.
* **Named eyewitness.** Hendrik Van Oss was specifically identified, reducing the anonymity common in many catalogue entries.
* **Official evaluation.** Meteorological and geological authorities reportedly favoured a meteor explanation almost immediately.
* **Agreement between observation and physics.** The reported colour, speed and fragmentation all correspond closely with known fireball behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/American%20UFO%20Publications/Weekly%20Research%20Magazine%27s%20Look-See/Vol%205%20No%203.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE v S LOOK-S.EE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE vS LOOK-S.EESeptember 22, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 22, 2016</span></span></span>

</div>

The main limitation is that relatively little primary documentation survives. Researchers today generally rely on the *Look-See* summary and its citation to the *San Diego Union*, rather than extensive original witness interviews or official investigation files. Even so, the surviving evidence consistently points in the same direction instead of presenting conflicting accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/American%20UFO%20Publications/Weekly%20Research%20Magazine%27s%20Look-See/Vol%205%20No%203.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE v S LOOK-S.EE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE WEKLY RESEARCH MAGAZINE vS LOOK-S.EESeptember 22, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 22, 2016</span></span></span>

## What the case means within Uganda's UFO history

The Kampala fireball occupies a distinctive place in Uganda's small catalogue of reported UFO incidents.

Unlike later claims that survive only as brief catalogue entries with little supporting documentation, the 1962 sighting includes a contemporaneous description, a named witness and an explicit conventional interpretation.
