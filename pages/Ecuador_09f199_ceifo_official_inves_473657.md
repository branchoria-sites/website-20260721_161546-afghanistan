## Introduction

[Ecuador]({{ 'what-do-ecuadors-ufo-files-really-show/' | relative_url }})'s Commission for the Investigation of the UFO Phenomenon (CEIFO) represents one of the country's most unusual official initiatives. Between the mid-2000s and the end of that decade, the commission sought to gather military reports, pilot testimony and archived government material relating to unidentified aerial phenomena. Unlike later claims circulating online, the best-documented achievement of CEIFO was not proving extraordinary explanations but persuading the Ecuadorian government to search its own archives and permit at least partial access to records held by the armed forces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2007/06/27/0001/8/C8BF385F5B534648BCB3B691B421EF06.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El UniversoCorrea pide desclasificar informe ovni &#124; Política &#124; Noticias &#124; El UniversoJune 27, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2007</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Ecuador_09f199_ceifo_official_inves_473657-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="CEIFO illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Ecuador_09f199_ceifo_official_inves_473657-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Ecuador_09f199_ceifo_official_inves_473657-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The commission therefore occupies an important place in Ecuador's UFO history for institutional rather than evidential reasons. It marked the country's clearest attempt to move UFO reports from informal discussion into an official administrative process. At the same time, questions remain over how much access CEIFO actually received, how complete its archive ever became, and whether much of the material survives in a publicly verifiable form.

## Why CEIFO was created

The driving force behind CEIFO was Ecuadorian UFO researcher and broadcaster Jaime Rodríguez, who argued for years that military personnel possessed reports of unusual aerial events that deserved systematic investigation. In 2003 he formally presented the government with a proposal to establish an official commission dedicated to collecting and evaluating such reports. His proposal reflected the slogan he frequently used: investigators should neither accept nor dismiss UFO claims without examining the evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2003/02/21/0001/8/9995A29570CD4F0399D8753C1115741A.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eluniverso.com.</span></span></span>

The proposal was ambitious. Contemporary reporting indicates that Rodríguez wanted the commission to begin with around twenty prominent cases that ranged from military sightings to far more controversial reports involving alleged contact experiences. This illustrates an early challenge for CEIFO: the project mixed relatively well-documented military observations with much weaker claims, making it difficult to establish a consistently rigorous evidential standard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2003/02/21/0001/8/9995A29570CD4F0399D8753C1115741A.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eluniverso.com.</span></span></span>

By 2005, CEIFO had been established and was publicly described as operating in association with the Ecuadorian Air Force, although the precise administrative relationship has never been fully documented in publicly accessible government records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2007/06/27/0001/8/C8BF385F5B534648BCB3B691B421EF06.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El UniversoCorrea pide desclasificar informe ovni &#124; Política &#124; Noticias &#124; El UniversoJune 27, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2007</span></span></span>

## The significance of the 2007 presidential order

The commission's most important documented success came in 2007.

After lobbying President Rafael Correa and senior officials, Rodríguez secured an instruction authorising the Ministry of Defence and all three branches of the Ecuadorian armed forces to provide any archived UFO-related information they possessed. Contemporary reporting in *El Universo* stated that the Ministry of Defence confirmed the order had been transmitted to military commanders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2007/06/27/0001/8/C8BF385F5B534648BCB3B691B421EF06.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El UniversoCorrea pide desclasificar informe ovni &#124; Política &#124; Noticias &#124; El UniversoJune 27, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2007</span></span></span>

This presidential decision is significant because it establishes several facts that are supported by contemporary reporting:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the Ecuadorian government accepted that UFO-related military files could exist;
* it authorised officials to search those archives;
* it recognised CEIFO as the body requesting access to the material.

</div>

None of those actions, however, amounted to an official endorsement of extraordinary explanations. The order concerned the release of information, not the validation of alien spacecraft or other exceptional claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2007/06/27/0001/8/C8BF385F5B534648BCB3B691B421EF06.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El UniversoCorrea pide desclasificar informe ovni &#124; Política &#124; Noticias &#124; El UniversoJune 27, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2007</span></span></span>

This distinction is frequently lost in later retellings, some of which present the declassification order as evidence that Ecuador acknowledged extraterrestrial visitors. The documentary record supports a much narrower conclusion: the government authorised a search for archived reports of unidentified aerial phenomena.

## How much access did CEIFO actually receive?

This is the central unresolved question surrounding the commission.

Rodríguez repeatedly argued that military institutions possessed decades of reports involving pilots, radar operators, naval personnel and army units. At the same time, public comments from Ecuadorian Air Force representatives suggested that available archives were considerably more limited than enthusiasts expected. One Air Force spokesman stated that when Rodríguez requested information, intelligence personnel had little or nothing relevant in their files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2007/06/27/0001/8/C8BF385F5B534648BCB3B691B421EF06.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El UniversoCorrea pide desclasificar informe ovni &#124; Política &#124; Noticias &#124; El UniversoJune 27, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2007</span></span></span>

These apparently conflicting accounts need not be mutually exclusive.

Several explanations remain plausible:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* different military branches may have maintained separate archives;
* some reports may have existed only as witness testimony rather than formal files;
* certain documents may have remained classified or inaccessible;
* records could have been lost through routine archival practices rather than deliberate concealment.

</div>

No comprehensive government inventory has been published that resolves these uncertainties.

## The disputed archive of 44 cases and 412 records

Much of the modern discussion about CEIFO centres on two frequently repeated figures.

The first is that CEIFO examined approximately **44 cases** involving military or civilian witnesses.

The second is that a broader collection contained **412 photographs and videos**, from which only a small proportion received detailed analysis. These figures have appeared repeatedly in recent Ecuadorian media coverage and have been attributed largely to material preserved by Rodríguez rather than to a searchable government archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.expreso.ec/buenavida/ovnis-ecuador-registros-oficiales-testigos-militares-revelan-encuentros-235688.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: expreso.ec">[Diario Expreso]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">expreso.ec</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on expreso.ec.</span></span></span>

These numbers should therefore be interpreted carefully.

What appears reasonably supported is that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* CEIFO assembled or reviewed a limited body of cases.
* Military witnesses formed part of that collection.
* The commission never completed a comprehensive evaluation of every reported incident.

</div>

What remains uncertain is:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether the figure of 412 represents official Ministry of Defence records or Rodríguez's private collection;
* whether all of those materials were authenticated government documents;
* whether the complete archive still exists in a form that independent researchers can examine.

</div>

Because the underlying collection has not been published as a complete, indexed archive, neither supporters nor sceptics can independently verify every reported case.

## Why the investigation achieved only limited results

Several structural problems prevented CEIFO from becoming a lasting government investigation.

### Limited institutional authority

Although CEIFO was publicly associated with official institutions, available evidence does not indicate that it possessed strong statutory investigative powers. It relied heavily on cooperation from military organisations rather than possessing authority to compel disclosure or interviews.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2007/06/27/0001/8/C8BF385F5B534648BCB3B691B421EF06.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El UniversoCorrea pide desclasificar informe ovni &#124; Política &#124; Noticias &#124; El UniversoJune 27, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2007</span></span></span>

### Uneven documentation

Many reported incidents consisted primarily of witness testimony rather than radar records, technical measurements or preserved physical evidence. This limited the commission's ability to reach definitive conclusions even where witnesses appeared credible.

### Short operational life

Later reporting generally describes CEIFO as active mainly between 2005 and 2007. There is little evidence that it evolved into a permanent investigative office with sustained government funding or continuous publication of reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.expreso.ec/buenavida/ovnis-ecuador-registros-oficiales-testigos-militares-revelan-encuentros-235688.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: expreso.ec">[Diario Expreso]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">expreso.ec</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on expreso.ec.</span></span></span>

### No comprehensive public archive

Perhaps the commission's greatest long-term weakness is the absence of an accessible official archive. Unlike countries that eventually released searchable collections of government documents, Ecuador has never produced a complete public catalogue of CEIFO's work.

## What CEIFO demonstrated—and what it did not

The historical importance of CEIFO lies less in what it discovered than in what it showed about Ecuadorian government policy.

The commission demonstrated that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Ecuadorian officials were willing to acknowledge public interest in UFO reports.
* The government authorised a search of military archives.
* Military sightings were considered sufficiently noteworthy to justify administrative attention.
* At least some reports from armed forces personnel entered an organised review process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eluniverso.com/2007/06/27/0001/8/C8BF385F5B534648BCB3B691B421EF06.html/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eluniverso.com">[El Universo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eluniverso.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El UniversoCorrea pide desclasificar informe ovni &#124; Política &#124; Noticias &#124; El UniversoJune 27, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2007</span></span></span>

</div>

By contrast, CEIFO did **not** establish:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* verified extraterrestrial technology;
* recovered craft or materials;
* official confirmation of alien visitation;
* a continuous, scientifically documented investigation comparable to a permanent government research programme.

</div>

The distinction matters because later internet accounts often blur administrative recognition of unidentified reports with confirmation of extraordinary explanations.

## CEIFO's place in Ecuador's wider UFO history

Within Ecuador's broader history of UFO reports, CEIFO remains the country's closest approach to an official investigative body. It bridged civilian UFO research and government institutions at a time when several countries in Latin America were reconsidering military archives relating to unusual aerial observations.

Its legacy is therefore mixed. The commission succeeded in bringing official attention to a subject long regarded as marginal and prompted a documented presidential order to search defence archives. Yet it never produced a comprehensive public body of evidence capable of resolving Ecuador's most famous cases.

For historians and researchers, CEIFO is best understood as an important governance experiment rather than a source of definitive answers. It confirmed that Ecuador's military and political institutions were willing to collect and review reports of unidentified aerial phenomena, but it left behind an incomplete documentary record whose scope, accessibility and long-term preservation remain subjects of continuing uncertainty.
