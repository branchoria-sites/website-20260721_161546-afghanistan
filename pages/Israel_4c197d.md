## What the national record actually contains

Israels UFO history is not preserved in one authoritative public catalogue. Instead, it is scattered across newspaper archives, television footage, private researchers collections, police-call reports, personal testimony and a small amount of archival film. This fragmentation creates a basic reliability problem: dramatic cases are repeatedly retold, while original witness statements, exact observation positions, radar returns, photographs at full resolution and laboratory documentation are often unavailable.

A short film labelled as an unidentified object in Israeli skies, dated 16 May 1970, has circulated under the name of the Israel State Archives. It is historically interesting as evidence that unusual-sky material entered an official collection, but it is not an investigative finding and provides no usable basis for determining the objects distance, size or nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J7HA2hWRAII" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[YouTube]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Israeli Skies-Israel State Archives עב&quot;ם בשמי ישראל-גנזך...צולם בתאריך 16/05/1970 נקודת אור בלילה מקור הצילום והצלם אינם ידוע...</span></span></span>

The absence of a comprehensive public government file should not be mistaken for proof that the Israeli military has never logged unidentified radar tracks or pilot observations. A country with tightly controlled and heavily monitored airspace would necessarily assess unknown aircraft as a defence matter. ## The 1990s wave around Kadima

The most distinctive Israeli cluster is associated with Kadima, east of Netanya, and nearby parts of central Israel. Residents and UFO enthusiasts have described glowing red or orange objects, unusual impressions or circles on the ground and alleged encounters with very tall humanoid figures. The period most often identified as the peak runs from roughly 1993 to 1998, although later reports and local gatherings have kept Kadimas reputation alive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ynetnews.com/magazine/article/byerlvo1wl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ynetnews.com">[ynetglobal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ynetnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ynetglobalIsrael&#x27;s UFO hotspot: town draws believers chasing alien...November 7, 2025 — 7 Nov 2025 — “Kadima Forest is a center for UFO...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

Kadima is important because it represents more than a single light in the sky. The stories formed a regional narrative: recurring locations, alleged physical traces, repeated witnesses and private investigators who treated separate accounts as parts of one continuing wave. Israeli and foreign UFO literature subsequently amplified claims involving landing marks, unusual residues and giant beings.

The evidential weakness is that the most extraordinary details are largely preserved through believers accounts, retrospective journalism and material associated with private UFO researchers. Claims that substances or soil samples were scientifically analysed are frequently repeated without complete laboratory reports, an unbroken chain of custody, control samples or peer-reviewed publication. Some retellings also blend separate incidents and later interpretations, making it difficult to establish what witnesses said before the story became culturally established.

Barry Chamish became the figure most closely identified with Israeli UFO investigation during this period. His work helped preserve reports that might otherwise have disappeared, but he later became known for promoting numerous political conspiracy theories as well as paranormal claims. That does not automatically disprove every testimony he collected, but it raises the need for independent corroboration rather than reliance on his interpretation. A contemporary assessment described him as both a leading Israeli UFO researcher and a prominent conspiracy theorist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/39244" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.pub">[Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.pub</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Barry Chamish &#124; Encyclopedia MDPIDecember 26, 2022 — In 1975, Chamish emigrated to Israel, where he attended the Hebrew Unive...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 26, 2022</span></span></span>

Kadima therefore belongs in the contested category. There is good evidence that residents reported unusual experiences and that the area developed a genuine UFO subculture. There is not comparably strong evidence that anomalous vehicles landed there, that non-human beings were observed under controlled conditions or that the alleged traces could not have ordinary origins.

## Jerusalems viral UFO was almost certainly a hoax

On 28 January 2011, videos appeared to show a bright object descending over Jerusalems Old City, hovering near the Dome of the Rock, flashing and then accelerating vertically at extraordinary speed. Several purported camera angles made the episode appear unusually persuasive, and the religious and political significance of the location helped the footage spread internationally.

The apparent multiplication of viewpoints was initially treated as evidence of authenticity. Closer examination produced the opposite conclusion. Investigators noted inconsistencies between the clips, questioned whether the backgrounds were live scenes rather than still images, and pointed to suspicious sound timing and camera behaviour. One analysis proposed that a reflected light manipulated through glass could create the movement seen in at least one recording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/holy-land-hoax-jerusalem-ufo-not-kosher/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cbsnews.com">[CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cbsnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CBS News Holy Land Hoax: Jerusalem UFO Not Kosher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CBS NewsHoly Land Hoax: Jerusalem UFO Not KosherFebruary 9, 2011 — 9 Feb 2011 — Myriad Signs Point to Hoax Behind UFO Videos Depicted Hov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2011</span></span></span>

The witness context was also weak. A spectacular object supposedly hovering over one of the most photographed urban landscapes in the world should have generated numerous independent witnesses, security recordings and unrelated photographs. Instead, the evidence consisted mainly of a small, interconnected set of online videos whose origins and creators came under scrutiny. Contemporary reporting consequently described the affair as fabricated, while sceptical investigators judged the hoax explanation overwhelmingly more likely than a physical craft over the city.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jpost.com/jewish-world/jewish-news/article-207126" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jpost.com">[jpost.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">article 207126</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">article 207126</span></span></span>

The Jerusalem case is a useful warning against equating multiple videos with independent confirmation. Several clips can be coordinated, derived from common source material or manufactured to imitate triangulation. Genuine multi-angle evidence requires verifiable recording locations, original files, reliable timestamps and geometry that converges on the same physical position. None of those foundations was securely established here.

## The 2012 spiral that hundreds of people really saw

A far stronger mass sighting occurred on 7 June 2012, when a bright object and expanding spiral were seen from northern Israel and across parts of the wider Middle East. Hundreds of Israelis reportedly contacted the police, and witnesses in Lebanon, Jordan, Armenia, Iran and Syria described related phenomena. Unlike the Jerusalem videos, the event was unquestionably real and geographically widespread.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.haaretz.com/2012-06-08/ty-article/watch-israelis-report-seeing-unidentified-flying-object-glowing-in-the-sky/0000017f-f455-d044-adff-f7fdd4790000" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: haaretz.com">[Haaretz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">haaretz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on haaretz.com.</span></span></span>

Its origin, however, was not extraterrestrial. Russia announced a successful test of a Topol intercontinental ballistic missile launched from the Kapustin Yar range towards the Saryshagan test site in Kazakhstan. The reported launch timing, broad viewing area and spiral form matched an upper-atmosphere missile plume, probably distorted as the vehicle or a rocket stage rotated and released gases at high altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://phys.org/news/2012-06-spiral-middle-east-russian-missile.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phys.org">[Phys.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phys.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spiral seen over the Middle East likely Russian missile</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spiral seen over the Middle East likely Russian missile</span></span></span>

This episode illustrates why geographical comparison is essential. Seen only from Israel, the object might have appeared to be an unusual local incursion. Reports from multiple countries revealed that it was a high-altitude regional event. The wide footprint, rather than supporting a giant craft over Israel, helped investigators identify a distant missile test.

It also shows that an explained UFO can remain genuinely remarkable. The witnesses did not imagine the spiral, and the police calls were not frivolous. They observed a rare aerospace phenomenon without the contextual information needed to recognise it. The mistake arose only when unidentified was interpreted as alien.

## Satellites, rockets and wartime skies

Israel has become particularly vulnerable to misidentification because unusual lights are now embedded in an exceptionally complex aerial environment. Civil and military aircraft, drones, interceptors, rockets, missile tests, satellites and astronomical objects can all be visible within a relatively small field of sky. During periods of conflict, short videos are often uploaded without reliable location, direction or timing, making even ordinary objects difficult to reconstruct.

Starlink satellite trains have repeatedly prompted UFO speculation. In May 2020, Israelis reported fast-moving lights that were identified as a group of roughly 60 recently launched Starlink satellites. Similar lines of lights were reported again in later years; in July 2025, an Israeli space specialist explained that a widely seen formation was a low-orbit Starlink group rather than missiles or rockets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.timesofisrael.com/israelis-wonder-at-mysterious-fast-moving-lights-in-the-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofisrael.com">[The Times of Israel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofisrael.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Times of Israel Israelis wonder at mysterious, fast-moving lights in the sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Times of Israel Israelis wonder at mysterious, fast-moving lights in the sky</span></span></span>

Satellite misidentification is not always as simple as recognising a neat line. Reflected sunlight can make individual spacecraft brighten suddenly, fade, appear to pause or become visible only along part of an orbit. Technical research has shown that intense Starlink reflections have themselves been reported as UAP by experienced pilots.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13091" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Extreme Flaring of Starlink Satellites</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv Extreme Flaring of Starlink Satellites</span></span></span>

The July 2022 reports of mysterious lights over Israel and other parts of the eastern Mediterranean demonstrate the difficulty of immediate identification. Israeli media reported that the military was aware of the observations and that Starlink was among the possible explanations, while police had not received corresponding information. The case was initially unresolved in public reporting, but its regional distribution and resemblance to satellite formations made an orbital source more plausible than a localised craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jpost.com/breaking-news/article-712880" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jpost.com">[The Jerusalem Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on jpost.com.</span></span></span>

Security conditions add another complication: authorities may be unable or unwilling to discuss classified aircraft, surveillance systems or radar data. This can leave civilian sightings unresolved even when defence agencies possess a likely explanation. Yet secrecy alone is not evidence of an extraterrestrial cause. In Israel, it more commonly means that the public record cannot distinguish among military, intelligence and civilian possibilities.

## The Sinai border account

In 2022, three former United States Army personnel described an incident they said occurred in 2014 while they were stationed in Egypts Sinai Peninsula near the Israeli border. They reported observing several bright objects moving or hovering in ways they considered extraordinary, and said the episode was not properly investigated at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-veterans-ufo-israel-egypt-border-b2082469.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: independent.co.uk">[The Independent]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">independent.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Independent Three US Army veterans claim they saw UFOs along Israel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Independent Three US Army veterans claim they saw UFOs along Israel</span></span></span>

The account is notable because the witnesses had military experience and were operating in a sensitive surveillance environment. It is also geographically relevant to Israels southern airspace, even though the reported observation point was on the Egyptian side of the border.

Its limitations are substantial. The claims became public approximately eight years after the alleged event, and the available reporting does not provide radar data, original imagery, precise angular measurements or contemporaneous written records sufficient for reconstruction. Statements that objects moved at extreme speed cannot be converted into actual velocity without established distance and range. A nearby light crossing a large angle may appear faster than a distant object travelling at aircraft speed.

The case is best classified as unresolved testimony rather than confirmed anomalous technology. Multiple witnesses strengthen the claim that something was seen, but they do not by themselves identify what it was. Shared viewing conditions can produce shared misinterpretation, particularly at night when distance cues are weak.

## The former space officials alien claims

Israels most internationally famous UFO-related claim did not arise from a sighting. In 2020, Haim Eshed, a retired general and former head of the Defence Ministrys space directorate, asserted that extraterrestrials existed, that the United States and Israel had been in contact with them and that a Galactic Federation was waiting for humanity to become ready for disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jpost.com/omg/former-israeli-space-security-chief-says-aliens-exist-humanity-not-ready-651405" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jpost.com">[The Jerusalem Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on jpost.com.</span></span></span>

Esheds senior career gave the comments unusual visibility, but authority in military space programmes is not evidence for a specific extraordinary claim. He did not release documents, sensor records, physical samples or independently verifiable testimony demonstrating contact. His statements also extended far beyond his publicly documented professional experience into claims about secret interplanetary agreements and alien facilities.

Isaac Ben-Israel, another prominent Israeli space figure, responded that extraterrestrial life may well exist but rejected the suggestion that humans had already met it, saying Eshed had gone too far. That distinction remains the scientifically defensible one: the possibility of life elsewhere is an astronomical question, while claims of contact with governments require direct evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.timesofisrael.com/israeli-space-chief-says-aliens-may-well-exist-but-they-havent-met-humans/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofisrael.com">[The Times of Israel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofisrael.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on timesofisrael.com.</span></span></span>

Esheds comments therefore belong in the unsupported rather than unresolved sighting category. An unresolved observation begins with measured or witnessed data whose cause is unknown. A contact narrative presented without testable evidence is a claim awaiting substantiation, not an unexplained incident.

## How evidence quality divides the cases

Israels principal UFO episodes become much clearer when separated by what the evidence actually supports.

**Confirmed events with conventional explanations** include the June 2012 spiral, which was associated with a Russian missile test, and repeated formations of lights identified as Starlink satellites. These were real observations inaccurately interpreted during their first circulation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://phys.org/news/2012-06-spiral-middle-east-russian-missile.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phys.org">[Phys.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phys.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spiral seen over the Middle East likely Russian missile</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spiral seen over the Middle East likely Russian missile</span></span></span>

**Contested local cases** include the [Kadima wave]({{ 'kadima-wave/' | relative_url }}). The underlying witness culture and history are real, but claims of landing traces, giant beings and unusual materials lack sufficiently transparent documentation and independent scientific replication.

**Probable fabrications** include the 2011 Jerusalem videos. Their provenance, technical inconsistencies and absence of the expected independent witness record make a coordinated hoax much more credible than a physical object above the Old City.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/holy-land-hoax-jerusalem-ufo-not-kosher/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cbsnews.com">[CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cbsnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CBS News Holy Land Hoax: Jerusalem UFO Not Kosher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CBS NewsHoly Land Hoax: Jerusalem UFO Not KosherFebruary 9, 2011 — 9 Feb 2011 — Myriad Signs Point to Hoax Behind UFO Videos Depicted Hov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2011</span></span></span>

**Unresolved testimony** includes the 2014 Sinai border account. The witnesses may be sincere, but the delayed report and absence of sensor data prevent confident identification.

**Unsupported high-level allegations** include Haim Esheds claims of government contact with extraterrestrials. His former office makes the story newsworthy, but it does not replace documentary or physical evidence.

This framework avoids two opposite errors. The first is dismissing every witness as dishonest or foolish; many UFO reports begin with sincere observations of genuinely unfamiliar phenomena. The second is treating the inability to identify an object as positive evidence of non-human origin. In practice, weak data preserve mystery more often than they reveal extraordinary technology.

## Why reports vary by region

Israels geography helps shape the types of sightings reported.

**Jerusalem** produces highly symbolic cases because lights over its skyline are interpreted through religious, historical and political associations. That symbolism can accelerate global circulation before technical verification takes place.

**Central Israel and the Sharon plain**, including Kadima, are associated with the countrys strongest folklore-like wave: repeated local testimony, alleged ground traces and a community of investigators who linked cases into a continuing narrative.

**Northern Israel** has generated mass reports of high-altitude regional events, including the 2012 missile plume. Its view towards Lebanon and Syria also means that distant military or aerospace activity may appear within the Israeli sky.

**The Negev and Sinai border region** offers dark horizons and long sightlines, which improve visibility but make distance hard to judge. It is also dense with military activity, restricted airspace and surveillance, increasing both the number of unusual observations and the difficulty of obtaining public explanations.

**Urban coastal Israel** is especially exposed to aircraft, satellites, drones and optical effects recorded on mobile phones. Videos taken between buildings or without visible stars and horizon references are often impossible to analyse reliably.

Regional clustering can therefore reflect observation conditions, local storytelling and reporting behaviour as much as the distribution of unusual objects.

## What would count as a strong Israeli case?

A persuasive future case would need more than eyewitness credibility or a striking video. It would require independently preserved evidence capable of ruling out ordinary explanations.

The strongest record would combine:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* original, uncompressed photographs or video with complete metadata;
* exact observation locations, directions and times;
* simultaneous recordings from separated positions;
* radar, infrared or other sensor confirmation;
* checks against aircraft, drone, missile and satellite activity;
* weather and astronomical data;
* documented handling and independent testing of any alleged physical material;
* public findings that distinguish unresolved data from speculation.

</div>

Israel is technically well placed to produce such evidence because its airspace is intensely monitored. The problem is public accessibility. Military sensors are classified, civilian videos are often incomplete, and private UFO archives may not meet forensic standards. Until a case crosses that gap, Israels UFO record remains culturally rich but scientifically inconclusive.

## What the record supports about Israel's UFO record
Israel has experienced genuine mass sightings, enduring local UFO traditions and several internationally famous claims. The record nevertheless provides no verified evidence of extraterrestrial visitation. Its clearest incidents show how missiles and satellites can produce spectacular observations, while its most famous video case demonstrates how apparent multi-camera confirmation can be manufactured.

A smaller group of reports remains unresolved, chiefly because the surviving data are too poor for identification. Unresolved should be preserved as an honest category: it neither proves an ordinary explanation nor licenses an extraordinary one. The enduring value of Israels UFO history lies less in evidence of alien craft than in the way military secrecy, regional aerospace activity, viral media, religious geography and local folklore combine to turn unfamiliar lights into powerful national stories.

      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })

  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');

    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {

        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });

      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');

      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();

        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');

        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });

        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
