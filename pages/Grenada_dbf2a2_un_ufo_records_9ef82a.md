## Introduction

[Grenada]({{ 'grenadas-ufo-mystery-beyond-the-myths/' | relative_url }})’s most durable connection to UFO history is not a verified sighting record but a set of United Nations documents showing that the country formally attempted to create an international system for UFO research. Between 1977 and 1978, the Grenadian government placed the subject before the UN General Assembly, proposing an agency or department that would collect information, coordinate research, and distribute findings on unidentified flying objects and related phenomena. The surviving records provide clear evidence of a diplomatic initiative, although they do not establish the existence of extraterrestrial craft or confirm any particular UFO claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/660849" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemRequest for the inclusion of an additional item in the agenda of the 32nd session:July 20, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 20, 1977</span></span></span>

## The 1977 UN submission

### A proposal for an international UFO research body

Grenada’s first major UN intervention came during the 32nd session of the General Assembly in 1977. A letter from Grenada’s Prime Minister and Minister of External Affairs, Sir Eric Gairy, requested that the agenda include the “establishment of an agency or a department of the United Nations” to research, coordinate, and disseminate information about UFOs and related phenomena. The document was formally registered as A/32/142 and included an explanatory memorandum outlining the proposed initiative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/660849" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemRequest for the inclusion of an additional item in the agenda of the 32nd session:July 20, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 20, 1977</span></span></span>

The proposal did not ask the UN to accept a specific explanation for UFO reports. Instead, it framed the issue as a problem of information gathering: governments and researchers were believed to hold scattered data that should be shared internationally. The underlying argument was that unidentified aerial observations represented a global question that exceeded the capacity of any single country.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/660849" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemRequest for the inclusion of an additional item in the agenda of the 32nd session:July 20, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 20, 1977</span></span></span>

The 1977 archival record also shows that Grenada attempted to build momentum around the idea beyond a single resolution. A later communication to the UN in November 1977 announced the submission of a study of UFO phenomena, including material connected with the wider debate over UFO research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/679874" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemLetter dated 10 November 1977 from the Permanent Representative of Grenada to the United Nations add...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1977</span></span></span>

### What the documents reveal about Grenada’s approach


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The surviving paperwork shows that Grenada’s initiative was primarily diplomatic rather than investigative. The government was seeking an international framework rather than presenting a scientific discovery or a confirmed event. The records mention research, coordination, and data exchange, but they do not contain evidence of recovered objects, verified extraterrestrial activity, or a UN assessment that UFOs were alien spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/660849" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemRequest for the inclusion of an additional item in the agenda of the 32nd session:July 20, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 20, 1977</span></span></span>

</div>

This distinction is important because later discussions often describe Grenada as having “taken UFOs to the UN” in a broad sense. The archival record is more specific: Grenada used UN procedures to argue for an organised study programme. The documents preserve the political effort, not proof of the phenomena being studied.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/660849" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemRequest for the inclusion of an additional item in the agenda of the 32nd session:July 20, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 20, 1977</span></span></span>

## The 1978 draft resolution

### A more detailed international proposal

Grenada returned to the issue in 1978 with a formal draft resolution before the General Assembly’s Special Political Committee. The document, A/SPC/33/L.20, was titled “Unidentified flying objects: draft resolution / Grenada” and again focused on creating a UN role in coordinating research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/678474?ln=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System Unidentified flying objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemUnidentified flying objects:November 27, 1978...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1978</span></span></span>

The draft resolution proposed that the United Nations, in consultation with appropriate specialised agencies, initiate and coordinate research into the nature and origin of unidentified flying objects and related phenomena. It also called for information and proposals from member states, specialised agencies, and non-governmental organisations, with the aim of supporting a possible international study.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/678474/files/A_SPC_33_L-20-E.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Distr. The proposal also suggested the creation of a small expert group under the framework of the Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space to help define guidelines for such a study. This shows that Grenada attempted to connect UFO research with existing UN structures concerned with space-related issues rather than creating a completely separate system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/678474/files/A_SPC_33_L-20-E.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Distr. ### The UN debate and archived proceedings

The 1978 discussion generated formal meeting records. The UN summary record for the Special Political Committee’s 35th meeting documents consideration of agenda item 126, the establishment of a UN agency or department for UFO research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/673551?ln=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemSummary record of the 35th meeting...</span></span></span>

Archival material from the period shows that the proposal was presented as an international information-sharing effort. Contemporary diplomatic records describe Grenada’s argument for a UN clearinghouse-style role, where countries could exchange data and coordinate research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wikileaks.org/plusd/cables/1978USUNN05425_d.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikileaks.org">[wikileaks.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikileaks.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1978USUNN05425 d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cable: 1978USUNN05425_dNovember 28, 1978...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 28, 1978</span></span></span>

However, the existence of debate should not be confused with adoption of a UFO programme. The records show discussion, proposals, and consideration by UN bodies; they do not show that a permanent UN UFO agency was established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/678474?ln=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System Unidentified flying objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemUnidentified flying objects:November 27, 1978...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1978</span></span></span>

## Why the agency was never created

### Political and scientific limits

Grenada’s proposal faced a basic challenge: the UN system was designed to coordinate international action where member states broadly agreed on a need and purpose. UFO research lacked a defined scientific mandate, established methodology, or consensus among governments about what an international body should actually investigate. The proposal therefore remained controversial rather than becoming an operational programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/678474/files/A_SPC_33_L-20-E.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Distr. ### The legacy of the documents

The importance of Grenada’s UN records lies in what they demonstrate historically. They provide one of the clearest examples of a national government formally attempting to place UFO research on an international diplomatic agenda. The documents preserve a chain of events: the 1977 agenda request, supporting submissions, the 1978 draft resolution, and the committee discussions that followed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/660849" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemRequest for the inclusion of an additional item in the agenda of the 32nd session:July 20, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 20, 1977</span></span></span>

For researchers examining Grenada’s UFO history, these records are stronger evidence than later retellings of individual [sightings]({{ 'sightings-d7d63d/' | relative_url }}) or claims.
