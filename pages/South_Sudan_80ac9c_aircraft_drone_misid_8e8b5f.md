## Introduction

In [South Sudan]({{ 'what-really-explains-south-sudans-ufo/' | relative_url }}), reports of unusual objects in the sky are more likely to involve misidentified aircraft or drones than genuinely unexplained aerial phenomena. The country's vast, sparsely populated landscape, limited civilian aviation infrastructure, ongoing security operations and dependence on humanitarian air transport create conditions in which ordinary aircraft can appear unfamiliar or threatening. At the same time, weak communications, patchy air-traffic awareness and the absence of a well-developed civilian reporting network mean that eyewitness accounts are often difficult to verify.

Available evidence does not indicate a pattern of credible UFO incidents involving unknown craft. Instead, the best-documented cases show how helicopters, transport aircraft and, increasingly, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) can be mistaken for mysterious objects or be incorrectly identified during periods of tension. Understanding these mechanisms is essential for interpreting South Sudan's limited UFO record.

## Remote Flights and Limited Air-Traffic Coverage

South Sudan presents an unusually difficult environment for identifying aircraft from the ground. Much of the country has few permanent roads, limited electrical infrastructure and large areas where civilian observers rarely see conventional air traffic except during humanitarian missions or military operations.

Several factors increase the likelihood of misidentification:

* **Irregular flight patterns.** United Nations, humanitarian and charter aircraft frequently operate between remote settlements rather than along familiar commercial routes.
* **Low-altitude operations.** Helicopters delivering aid or evacuating casualties often fly lower than scheduled passenger aircraft, making them appear unusually close or slow.
* **Limited public awareness of flight schedules.** Unlike major aviation markets, there is little publicly visible information about local air movements, leaving witnesses without an obvious explanation for unexpected aircraft.
* **Variable surveillance coverage.** Air-traffic monitoring across remote regions has historically been less comprehensive than in more developed aviation environments, while pilots operating in South Sudan have relied on procedures adapted to limited communications and surveillance coverage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scaa-aim.gov.sd/report/html/eAIP/EN-ENR-1.1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scaa-aim.gov.sd">[AIM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scaa-aim.gov.sd</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EN ENR 1.1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AIMAIP for SUDAN (section ENR-1.1) valid from 24 MAR 2022March 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2022</span></span></span>

These conditions make ordinary aircraft easier to interpret as unknown objects, particularly during dawn, dusk or poor weather when judging distance, speed and altitude becomes more difficult.

The country's long-running conflict also changes how people interpret what they see. In peaceful settings, an unfamiliar aircraft may simply attract curiosity. In areas affected by armed violence, however, witnesses may immediately associate an unidentified object with surveillance, military activity or hostile forces.

## The Likuangole Helicopter Misidentification

The clearest documented example of mistaken aerial identification in South Sudan is not a civilian UFO report but a fatal military misidentification.

On 21 December 2012, a United Nations Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) Mi-8 helicopter conducting a reconnaissance flight near Likuangole in Jonglei State was shot down, killing all four crew members. The South Sudanese military later acknowledged that its forces had mistaken the clearly marked UN helicopter for an enemy aircraft. According to official UN statements, the helicopter had been operating on an authorised mission, and communication after the incident confirmed that government forces had engaged it believing it to be hostile.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.un.org/sg/en/node/202531" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: un.org">[un.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited NationsNoon briefing of 21 December 2012 &#124; Secretary-GeneralDecember 21, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 2012</span></span></span>

Although this was not a UFO incident, it illustrates several mechanisms that also influence civilian reports of unexplained aerial objects:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* aircraft can be identified primarily by expectation rather than appearance;
* high-stress security environments encourage rapid assumptions;
* observers often have only a brief visual view before forming conclusions;
* incomplete situational awareness increases the chance of serious error.

</div>

The incident demonstrates that even trained military personnel equipped with weapons can misidentify known aircraft under operational pressure. Civilian witnesses, possessing far less information, are naturally even more susceptible to incorrect interpretations.

## How Drones Mimic Extraordinary Flight

Small unmanned aerial vehicles introduce another source of confusion, although publicly documented South Sudanese civilian drone sightings remain sparse.

Several characteristics make drones particularly easy to mistake for something unusual:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Stationary hovering.** Multirotor drones can remain almost motionless, unlike conventional aircraft.
* **Rapid changes of direction.** Short bursts of acceleration can appear impossible when viewed without depth perception.
* **Bright navigation lights.** At night, observers may see only isolated coloured lights rather than the drone itself.
* **Quiet operation.** Small drones can be difficult to hear at moderate distances, especially in windy conditions.

</div>

In conflict environments, drones are increasingly used for reconnaissance, observation and, in neighbouring conflicts, military operations. Even when a drone itself is not present, public awareness of drone warfare can influence how people interpret unfamiliar lights or aircraft overhead.

Importantly, no well-substantiated South Sudan UFO case has been shown to involve a mysterious craft later confirmed as a drone. Instead, the available evidence suggests that drones should be viewed as a plausible mechanism for some future reports rather than as an explanation already demonstrated by documented national cases.

## Why Misidentifications Are More Likely Than Extraordinary Explanations

Several characteristics of South Sudan favour ordinary explanations over exotic ones.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.unmissions.org/en/unmas/where-we-work/south-sudan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unmissions.org">[unmissions.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unmissions.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">south sudan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNMAS facilitates access to water points for communities in South Sudan. UNMAS / Genevieve Imbali ABOUT...</span></span></span>

First, humanitarian aviation is unusually prominent. UNMISS, the United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) and numerous relief organisations operate aircraft into locations inaccessible by road. For communities that rarely encounter aviation, these flights may appear unusual simply because they are infrequent.

Second, conflict affects perception. Aircraft associated with surveillance, troop movement or aid delivery may generate rumours that spread faster than verified information.

Third, documentation remains limited. South Sudan has no widely recognised civilian UFO reporting organisation, and many observations are never independently investigated or preserved. This makes it difficult to distinguish isolated eyewitness accounts from verifiable incidents.

Finally, no publicly available South Sudanese case combines multiple independent witnesses, radar data, imagery and official investigation in a way that would justify classifying an aircraft-related sighting as genuinely unexplained.

## What the Evidence Shows

Within South Sudan's limited public UFO record, aircraft misidentification provides a stronger explanation than extraordinary hypotheses.

The evidence supports several conclusions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* Conventional helicopters and aircraft can be difficult to identify correctly in remote regions.
* The 2012 Likuangole tragedy demonstrates that even experienced military personnel can mistake known aircraft for hostile targets under operational stress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.un.org/sg/en/node/202531" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: un.org">[United Nations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited NationsNoon briefing of 21 December 2012 &#124; Secretary-GeneralDecember 21, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 2012</span></span></span>
* Expanding drone use adds another category of unfamiliar aerial object that may generate future reports, especially at night or during security incidents.
* No well-documented South Sudan UFO investigation has produced evidence that an aircraft or drone initially believed to be unexplained was later shown to involve anything beyond conventional aviation.

</div>

Taken together, these factors suggest that aircraft and drone misidentification should be considered one of the principal mechanisms for understanding unusual aerial reports in South Sudan, alongside other conventional explanations such as meteors, astronomical objects and atmospheric effects.

    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
