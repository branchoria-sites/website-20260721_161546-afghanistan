## Introduction

The best-known modern [Afghanistan]({{ 'what-does-afghanistans-ufo-record/' | relative_url }})-linked unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP) record is a short infrared (IR) military video reportedly captured on 23 November 2020. Although the footage has been widely circulated as evidence of an unexplained object, the video itself does not establish what the object was. The publicly available material is a short, edited extract with missing telemetry, no original sensor file, and an incomplete chain of custody. Those limitations mean neither extraordinary claims nor ordinary explanations can be confirmed from the released clip alone. The case remains noteworthy because it illustrates both the value and the limits of military sensor evidence: it documents that an object was considered worth recording, but it does not preserve enough technical information to determine its identity or flight characteristics with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007713/dow-uap-pr055-spherical-uap-over-afg-and-out-clouds-23-nov-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Video - DOW-UAP-PR055, &quot;Spherical UAP over AFG in and out of clouds 23 Nov 2020&quot;May 22, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2026</span></span></span>

## What the released footage visibly contains

The publicly circulated clip is presented as infrared imagery from a United States military platform operating within the US Central Command area of responsibility during November 2020. The object appears as a compact thermal or contrast feature moving across the sensor's field of view while partially associated with cloud cover. The publicly released version lasts only seconds and contains several editing stages, including zoomed, slowed and contrast-enhanced segments before returning to the original sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007713/dow-uap-pr055-spherical-uap-over-afg-and-out-clouds-23-nov-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Video - DOW-UAP-PR055, &quot;Spherical UAP over AFG in and out of clouds 23 Nov 2020&quot;May 22, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2026</span></span></span>

The video is often described online as showing a "disc-shaped" or "spherical" object. These descriptions should be treated carefully. Infrared sensors record heat contrast rather than conventional visible-light images, meaning apparent shape is strongly influenced by sensor resolution, zoom level, image processing and viewing angle. At the available resolution, the clip cannot reliably establish the object's true physical geometry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007713/dow-uap-pr055-spherical-uap-over-afg-and-out-clouds-23-nov-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Video - DOW-UAP-PR055, &quot;Spherical UAP over AFG in and out of clouds 23 Nov 2020&quot;May 22, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2026</span></span></span>

Some commentators have argued that the object appears to change direction while moving through clouds or without an obvious thermal exhaust. Others note that apparent manoeuvres in infrared footage can result from camera tracking, aircraft motion, changing viewing geometry or image processing. Because the released clip lacks the underlying navigation and sensor data, these competing interpretations cannot be decisively tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007713/dow-uap-pr055-spherical-uap-over-afg-and-out-clouds-23-nov-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Video - DOW-UAP-PR055, &quot;Spherical UAP over AFG in and out of clouds 23 Nov 2020&quot;May 22, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why the editing matters

The greatest weakness of the publicly available evidence is not necessarily what appears in the video but how the surviving copy reached investigators.

The officially released description states that the media had already been digitally altered before it was uploaded to a classified network and that it is presented exactly as received. The published version therefore is not the original sensor recording. Instead, it contains edited sequences labelled with descriptions such as "zoomed in" and "sharpened, zoomed motion tracked contrast enhanced". These alterations prevent analysts from treating the clip as pristine evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007713/dow-uap-pr055-spherical-uap-over-afg-and-out-clouds-23-nov-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Video - DOW-UAP-PR055, &quot;Spherical UAP over AFG in and out of clouds 23 Nov 2020&quot;May 22, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2026</span></span></span>

Digital enhancement can improve visibility for human viewers, but it also changes the evidential value of imagery. Sharpening, contrast enhancement, motion tracking and interpolation may exaggerate apparent edges, alter perceived motion or introduce artefacts that were absent from the original recording. Modern media-forensics research consistently stresses that once a video has undergone undocumented processing, independent reconstruction of the original scene becomes substantially more difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06564" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Media Forensics and Deep Fakes: an overview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Media Forensics and DeepFakes: an overviewJanuary 18, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 18, 2020</span></span></span>

Equally important is the documented chain-of-custody problem. The official release notes that many videos in the recovered collection lack a substantiated chain of custody. That does not prove the footage is fabricated, but it does mean investigators cannot fully document every stage from original capture through later handling and editing. This uncertainty lowers confidence in technical conclusions drawn solely from the public copy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007713/dow-uap-pr055-spherical-uap-over-afg-and-out-clouds-23-nov-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Video - DOW-UAP-PR055, &quot;Spherical UAP over AFG in and out of clouds 23 Nov 2020&quot;May 22, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2026</span></span></span>

## What cannot be calculated without the missing metadata

The most important information absent from the released clip is the sensor metadata normally recorded alongside military infrared imagery.

Without those data, analysts cannot independently determine:

* The aircraft or sensor platform that recorded the footage.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Exact sensor mode, focal length and field of view.
* Range to the target.
* Altitude of both platform and object.
* Camera pointing angles and stabilisation behaviour.
* Aircraft speed and heading.
* Wind conditions.
* Precise frame timing.
* Whether additional radar, electro-optical or electronic sensor tracks existed.

</div>

These omissions have major consequences. Apparently unusual motion in two-dimensional video cannot automatically be converted into real-world speed or acceleration. A small nearby object, a distant aircraft, atmospheric effects and camera movement can produce very different physical scenarios while generating similar image motion on screen.

Likewise, the absence of a visible thermal plume cannot by itself demonstrate unconventional propulsion. Infrared appearance depends on viewing angle, detector settings, wavelength band, atmospheric absorption, distance and sensor sensitivity. An object may produce little obvious thermal signature in one configuration while being entirely conventional. Multi-sensor measurements are generally required before meaningful performance estimates can be made.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Scientific Investigation of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) Using Multimodal Ground-Based ObservatoriesMay 29, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2023</span></span></span>

## What official sources actually say

Official wording surrounding the case is considerably more cautious than many online discussions.

The published description does not identify the object as extraterrestrial, non-human or technologically extraordinary. Instead, it states only that the footage is likely derived from an infrared sensor aboard a US military platform operating in the Central Command area during November 2020. It also explicitly notes that the accompanying description is informational and should not be interpreted as an investigative conclusion regarding the object's validity, nature or significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007713/dow-uap-pr055-spherical-uap-over-afg-and-out-clouds-23-nov-2020" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dvidshub.net">[DVIDS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dvidshub.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Video - DOW-UAP-PR055, &quot;Spherical UAP over AFG in and out of clouds 23 Nov 2020&quot;May 22, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2026</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. A video can document an unidentified object without implying that the object exhibited impossible performance or represented unknown technology.

## Competing interpretations

Because the available evidence is incomplete, several interpretations remain plausible.

Those favouring an anomalous interpretation argue that the object appears difficult to identify, lacks an obvious exhaust signature and seems to move in a manner inconsistent with straightforward aircraft flight.
