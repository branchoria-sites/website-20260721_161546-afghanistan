## Introduction

[North Macedonia]({{ 'north-macedonia/' | relative_url }}) does not maintain a dedicated public archive of unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) or UFO reports. Instead, any unusual event observed in the country's airspace enters official systems only if it affects aviation safety, air traffic operations or accident investigation. This distinction is important because it explains why the country's documented UFO record is sparse: the absence of a searchable national UFO archive should not be confused with evidence that no unusual sightings have ever been reported. Rather, it reflects how aviation governance is organised and what authorities are legally required to record.

For anyone examining UFO claims in North Macedonia, the central challenge is therefore an evidence gap rather than a hidden archive. Most publicly discussed sightings survive only as media reports, personal testimony or online database entries, while the technical information needed to assess them—such as radar tracks, air traffic communications, flight data or structured witness interviews—is generally unavailable.

## What do North Macedonia's aviation authorities actually record?

North Macedonia operates a modern civil aviation safety reporting framework aligned with European aviation regulations. The Civil Aviation Agency (CAA) requires mandatory reporting of aviation occurrences and also provides a voluntary reporting system for safety-related events. These systems exist to improve aviation safety rather than to investigate unexplained phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.caa.gov.mk/en/safety/reporting-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: caa.gov.mk">[www.caa.gov.mk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">caa.gov.mk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">www.caa.gov.mk Occurrence reporting – www.caa.gov.mk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">reporting – www.caa.gov.mk...</span></span></span>

## Why there is no national UFO archive

Unlike a handful of countries that have periodically released military or government UFO files, North Macedonia has never established a public office dedicated to collecting civilian UFO reports or publishing unexplained aerial cases.

Several practical reasons explain this situation.

First, the country has a relatively small civil aviation sector. Aviation authorities prioritise operational safety, regulatory compliance and accident prevention over cataloguing unusual visual observations.

Second, the legal reporting system is hazard-based. Officials record events that create or could create aviation risk. A witness who merely reports "strange lights" without an associated safety concern may never enter the formal occurrence database.

Third, even when unusual observations are reported through aviation channels, they are generally classified according to aviation safety categories rather than under a dedicated "UFO" or "UAP" designation. This means there is no publicly searchable collection of unexplained aerial observations comparable to the national UFO archives once released by some other governments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.caa.gov.mk/en/safety/reporting-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: caa.gov.mk">[www.caa.gov.mk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">caa.gov.mk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">www.caa.gov.mk Occurrence reporting – www.caa.gov.mk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">reporting – www.caa.gov.mk...</span></span></span>

## What public records leave out

The lack of a dedicated archive creates several important evidence gaps.

Most publicly discussed North Macedonian sightings lack:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* primary witness statements preserved in official files;
* precise observation coordinates;
* radar data;
* air traffic control recordings;
* weather reconstructions prepared during investigation;
* photographic metadata;
* chain-of-custody documentation for images or video;
* documented expert conclusions.

</div>

This absence of technical information makes many reported sightings effectively impossible to evaluate years later. Cases often remain "unexplained" not because investigators ruled out ordinary causes, but because insufficient evidence survives to conduct a meaningful investigation.

Media reports also tend to preserve only the most dramatic parts of an incident while omitting details that investigators would consider essential, such as viewing angle, duration, atmospheric conditions or the observer's exact location.

## The consequences for evaluating reported sightings

This evidence gap affects both sceptical and extraordinary interpretations.

Supporters of unusual explanations cannot demonstrate that a reported object displayed capabilities beyond known aircraft or natural phenomena because the necessary measurements are missing.

Likewise, sceptics often cannot conclusively identify ordinary explanations because the original observational data no longer exist.

As a result, many North Macedonian reports fall into a third category: **insufficient evidence**.

This distinction is significant. An unresolved case is not automatically evidence of an extraordinary phenomenon, just as the absence of an explanation does not prove that investigators ignored the event. In many instances, there simply is not enough verifiable information to reach a reliable conclusion.

## How a credible national reporting system could improve the evidence

If North Macedonia wished to improve the quality of future aerial-anomaly investigations without creating a specialised UFO agency, relatively modest changes to existing aviation reporting structures could substantially improve the evidence available.

A stronger national system would ideally include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a standard reporting form for unusual aerial observations;
* precise time and geographic coordinates;
* preservation of original photographs and videos with metadata;
* automatic checks against aircraft, satellite and astronomical databases;
* documentation of weather conditions;
* procedures for collecting independent witness accounts;
* transparent publication of findings once investigations are complete.

</div>

Because the Civil Aviation Agency already operates mandatory and voluntary occurrence reporting mechanisms, such additions could build upon existing safety infrastructure rather than requiring an entirely separate bureaucracy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.caa.gov.mk/en/safety/reporting-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: caa.gov.mk">[www.caa.gov.mk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">caa.gov.mk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">www.caa.gov.mk Occurrence reporting – www.caa.gov.mk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">reporting – www.caa.gov.mk...</span></span></span>

## What this means for the wider North Macedonia UFO record

The country's broader UFO history should therefore be interpreted with caution. Publicly discussed incidents—including reports around Skopje and other locations—exist largely outside official archival systems and are usually preserved through journalism, private databases or personal recollections rather than government documentation.

For readers exploring other aspects of North Macedonia's reported sightings, this institutional context explains why so few cases can be examined using the kinds of technical evidence available in well-documented aviation investigations. The main limitation is not necessarily the number of reported sightings but the lack of systematic official records capable of distinguishing ordinary aerial events from genuinely unresolved ones.

The result is a persistent evidence gap: North Macedonia possesses functioning aviation safety institutions and legally defined occurrence-reporting procedures, yet no dedicated public repository for unexplained aerial sightings. Consequently, most alleged UFO incidents remain historically interesting but evidentially weak, with too little preserved official documentation to support either confident debunking or extraordinary claims.
