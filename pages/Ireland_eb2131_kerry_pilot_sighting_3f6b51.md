## Introduction

The November 2018 pilot sightings off [Ireland]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-irelands-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s west coast remain one of the country's most discussed unexplained aviation incidents because they combined several features rarely found together: multiple independent airline crews, real-time radio communications with air traffic control, experienced observers and an official aviation occurrence report. Yet the case is equally notable for what is missing. No publicly released radar tracks, cockpit images, flight-data evidence or physical material have emerged despite years of interest. As a result, the incident remains unresolved in the narrow sense that the reported lights were never definitively identified, but it does not constitute evidence of an extraordinary craft. Instead, it illustrates the limits of eyewitness testimony when instrumental evidence is absent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>

## What the Pilots and Controllers Reported

Shortly before 06:47 UTC on 9 November 2018, the crew of British Airways flight BA94 from Montreal to London Heathrow contacted Shannon Air Traffic Control while west of County Kerry. The captain asked whether military exercises were taking place because the crew had observed a very bright object moving rapidly past the aircraft before apparently turning north. The pilots emphasised that they did not believe there was an immediate collision risk but wanted to know what they had seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>

Shannon controllers replied that there were no military operations in the area and that nothing appeared on either primary or secondary radar. That exchange quickly became the defining feature of the case because it established that controllers were not tracking an obvious aircraft corresponding to the reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>

The incident became more significant when additional crews joined the conversation.

A Virgin Atlantic pilot described what appeared to be multiple bright objects following a common trajectory and suggested they resembled either a meteor or an object re-entering the atmosphere. ## Why Multiple Witnesses Matter – and Why They Are Not Enough

One reason the Kerry case stands above many Irish UFO reports is that it does not depend on a single observer.

Several factors strengthen the testimony:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Multiple commercial airline crews reported unusual lights within the same time window.
* The reports were made immediately over operational radio channels rather than after the event.
* The witnesses were trained aviation professionals accustomed to observing aircraft, navigation lights and atmospheric conditions.
* Air traffic controllers documented the reports as an aviation occurrence requiring follow-up.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[theguardian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>

</div>

These features substantially reduce the likelihood of simple fabrication.

However, they do not establish what was actually observed.

All witnesses viewed the phenomenon from different positions, distances and viewing angles without a known reference point. Human observers are generally poor at estimating the distance, size and speed of bright lights seen against a dark sky. If an object's true distance is unknown, estimates of its velocity or manoeuvring can become highly misleading. Apparent changes in direction may also arise from perspective effects as aircraft themselves move through three-dimensional space.

Consequently, multiple agreeing witnesses increase confidence that an unusual visual event occurred, but they do not independently determine the nature of that event.

## The Missing Evidence

The most important feature of the Kerry incident is not what exists but what does not.

### No publicly released radar confirmation

Perhaps the most frequently quoted part of the exchange is Shannon ATC's statement that nothing appeared on primary or secondary radar.

This observation is often misunderstood.

Secondary surveillance radar depends on aircraft transponders. Any object without a transponder would naturally be absent. Primary radar can detect non-cooperative targets, but detection depends on numerous variables including object size, radar cross-section, altitude, range and radar coverage. Therefore, "nothing on radar" should not be interpreted as proof that no physical object existed. Equally, it cannot be treated as evidence for advanced technology. It simply means no correlating radar target was available to controllers at that time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>

### No cockpit imagery

Despite several aircraft being present, no authenticated cockpit photographs or videos have entered the public record.

Modern airliners are not routinely equipped to record forward-looking video from the flight deck, and pilots engaged in active flight are neither expected nor encouraged to photograph unexpected phenomena during critical operational phases.

### No flight-data correlation

No publicly released flight-data recorder information demonstrates evasive manoeuvres, abnormal aircraft behaviour or unusual sensor readings associated with the event.

The aircraft continued safely, and none reported a collision hazard requiring emergency action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>

### No physical evidence

No debris, recovered object or measurable physical trace has ever been associated with the sightings.

This sharply limits the kinds of scientific testing that can be performed.

## Radar Gaps and Competing Explanations

The absence of corroborating physical evidence leaves several explanations technically possible, although none has been conclusively demonstrated.

The explanation discussed most often by aviation and astronomy commentators is that the pilots observed an exceptionally bright meteor or fragments from atmospheric re-entry. This interpretation fits several aspects of the reports, including intense brightness, multiple luminous objects and descriptions resembling objects following similar trajectories. One of the reporting pilots independently suggested this possibility during the radio conversation itself rather than after media coverage began.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[theguardian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>

Another possibility involves perspective effects created by distant astronomical or atmospheric phenomena viewed from high altitude. Bright celestial bodies near the horizon, reflections or unusual atmospheric lighting can occasionally appear to move dramatically relative to an aircraft's own motion, although these explanations fit some witness descriptions better than others.

Conventional aircraft are generally regarded as a weaker explanation because controllers reported no corresponding radar contacts and there were no military exercises known to Shannon ATC. Nevertheless, the absence of radar alone does not completely eliminate every conventional aerial object, since not all targets are equally detectable under operational conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>

Importantly, none of these explanations accounts perfectly for every reported detail. That is precisely why the case remains classified as unidentified rather than solved.

## Why the Case Remains Unresolved

The Kerry pilot sightings continue to attract attention because they combine unusually credible witnesses with unusually limited physical evidence.

Unlike many classic UFO stories, the event is anchored by recorded air traffic communications, multiple commercial flight crews and documented aviation reporting procedures. Unlike well-documented aviation incidents, however, it lacks the independent instrumental evidence that would normally allow investigators to reconstruct events confidently.

The result is a case that remains unresolved in a strictly evidential sense. The observations cannot be confidently reduced to a single known cause, but neither do they provide convincing proof of advanced aircraft, extraterrestrial technology or any other extraordinary explanation. Within Ireland's broader history of unexplained aerial reports, the Kerry incident is best understood as a reminder that reliable witnesses can establish that something unusual was seen, while still leaving the central question—what was actually observed—unanswered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/nov/13/irish-authorities-investigate-ufo-sightings-by-commercial-pilots" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[theguardian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianIrish authorities investigate UFO sightings by commercial pilots &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianNovember 12, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 2018</span></span></span>
