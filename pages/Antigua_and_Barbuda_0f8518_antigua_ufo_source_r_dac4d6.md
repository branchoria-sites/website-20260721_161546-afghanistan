## Introduction

The short answer is that [Antigua and Barbuda]({{ 'antigua-and-barbuda/' | relative_url }})'s UFO sources should be treated with caution rather than dismissed outright. The country's documented UFO record is exceptionally small, which means individual reports receive disproportionate attention. Some local news stories accurately preserve eyewitness testimony, but many later claims are recycled through commercial UFO databases, social media, or unsourced websites that rarely add independent evidence. The strongest approach is therefore not to ask whether a source "believes in UFOs", but whether it provides enough verifiable information for the claim to be independently checked.

By contrast, anonymous internet posts, inflated sighting totals and repeated retellings of old stories deserve substantially less confidence.

## Why commercial UFO databases require careful reading

Several international databases include Antigua and Barbuda reports. They can be valuable finding aids, but they should not be mistaken for independently verified investigations.

The National UFO Reporting Center (NUFORC), for example, openly explains that reports are largely published as submitted by witnesses and that the organisation does not claim every report is valid. It now grades reports by credibility where possible, but eyewitness testimony remains the foundation of the database rather than confirmed evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/databank/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC - Latest UFO SightingsSeptember 6, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 6, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why social media often exaggerates Antigua's UFO record

Social media introduces several problems that are especially significant in countries with few documented sightings.

A single video may be reposted:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* without its original upload date,
* with the location changed,
* under sensational headlines,
* or attached to unrelated stories from other countries.

</div>

As a result, an observer may mistakenly believe numerous independent Antigua sightings exist when the same footage is simply circulating repeatedly.

Another common problem is the appearance of unsupported statistics, such as claims that Antigua has experienced "dozens" or "hundreds" of unexplained UFO encounters. These figures often originate from aggregated databases containing duplicate reports, anonymous submissions or entries copied from secondary sources rather than official investigations.

Without transparent methodology, such totals have little analytical value.

## How to verify an Antigua sighting yourself

Most recent Caribbean "UFO" reports can be tested against publicly available information before reaching extraordinary conclusions.

A sensible verification process includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Confirm the exact local time and location.
2. Check whether a rocket launch occurred within the previous hour.
3. Look for scheduled Starlink deployments or satellite trains.
4. Review aircraft tracking data where available.
5. Compare the viewing direction with the positions of bright planets or the Moon.
6. Examine local weather conditions, including high cloud and atmospheric optics.
7. Search for reports from neighbouring Caribbean islands recorded at the same time.

</div>

The December 2019 Antigua event illustrates why this process matters. Contemporary witness reports were later compared with regional observations and launch information, producing a conventional explanation that fit the available evidence better than an unidentified craft hypothesis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://antiguanewsroom.com/residents-concerned-over-strange-lighted-object-in-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguanewsroom.com">[Antigua News Room]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguanewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Antigua News Room Residents Concerned Over Strange Lighted Object In Sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Antigua News RoomResidents Concerned Over Strange Lighted Object In Sky - Antigua News RoomDecember 18, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2019</span></span></span>

## Warning signs that a claim is weak

Readers should be cautious when a source:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* provides no date or location;
* cites unnamed "government insiders";
* presents cropped or low-resolution images without originals;
* repeats stories from other websites without attribution;
* treats every unidentified object as extraterrestrial;
* ignores obvious aviation or launch activity;
* or claims certainty while offering no supporting evidence.

</div>

These warning signs are particularly important in Antigua because the limited number of genuine local reports makes recycled stories relatively easy to mistake for new evidence.

## What remains genuinely unknown?

It is important to distinguish between an unexplained observation and proof of an extraordinary phenomenon.

Some Antigua sightings remain unidentified simply because too little information was recorded. Missing timestamps, absent photographs, uncertain witness locations and incomplete documentation prevent investigators from reaching a confident conclusion.

That is different from demonstrating that an object was extraordinary.

Likewise, the absence of a conventional explanation today does not automatically support an extraterrestrial explanation. It more often reflects insufficient evidence.

## The balanced reading of Source Check
