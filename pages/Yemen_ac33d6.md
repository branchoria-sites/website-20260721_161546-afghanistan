## The 2024 object off Yemen

The central Yemeni UAP case entered the public record on 9 September 2025, when Representative Eric Burlison showed an infrared video during a House Oversight hearing on UAP transparency and whistleblower protection. He said it had been recorded on 30 October 2024 by an MQ-9 Reaper monitoring an orb-like object off Yemen, while another MQ-9 launched an AGM-114 Hellfire missile towards it. Journalistic descriptions of the hearing reported that the object appeared to continue moving after a flash and apparent interaction with the missile.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/restoring-public-trust-through-uap-transparency-and-whistleblower-protection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oversight.house.gov">[house.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oversight.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">House Oversight CommitteeRestoring Public Trust Through UAP Transparency and Whistleblower Protection - United States House Committee on...</span></span></span>

The date and location matter. In October 2024, the waters and airspace around Yemen were an active combat zone involving Houthi missiles and drones, international naval forces and American surveillance and strike operations. Reported activity near the end of that month included attacks on shipping, drones launched from Yemen and continuing coalition operations around the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden. The presence of an MQ-9 and an attempted interception is therefore compatible with an ordinary military-security incident, even though the target itself has not been publicly identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Red_Sea_crisis_%282024%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Timeline of the Red Sea crisis (2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Timeline of the Red Sea crisis (2024</span></span></span>

### What the video actually establishes

The publicly shown footage supports several limited conclusions. A sensor platform tracked a small object against a relatively featureless background. A fast object approached it, followed by a bright event and material apparently separating or falling away. The tracked object, or something close to its apparent position, remained visible afterwards.

That is not enough to demonstrate that a Hellfire made direct contact and “bounced off”. Infrared video compresses a three-dimensional scene into two dimensions. Without range, viewing angle, lens data and the flight paths of both aircraft, apparent overlap does not prove physical contact. Parallax—the apparent movement caused by the observer’s own motion—can make separate objects look aligned, while automatic tracking may briefly lose and reacquire a target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/uap-hearing-new-video-yemen-orb.14427/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Hearing New Video</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Hearing New Video - Yemen Orb9 Sept 2025 — Dated Oct 20th, 2024, in Yemen It appears to show a missile interception viewed fr...</span></span></span>

## How the competing claims measure up

The Yemen video has attracted three broad interpretations, but they do not carry equal evidential weight.

**Extraordinary-technology interpretation.** At the congressional hearing and in subsequent media coverage, the event was presented as an object surviving a direct Hellfire strike. This interpretation treats the apparent continuation of the object as evidence of exceptional durability or unknown technology. Its weakness is that the crucial premise—a confirmed direct hit on the same object—cannot be established from the compressed public clip.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nypost.com/2025/09/09/us-news/shocking-radar-footage-shows-hellfire-missile-fired-by-us-military-directly-hit-ufo-over-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nypost.com">[New York Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nypost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Further testimony came from Air Force veterans Jeffrey Nuccetelli and Dylan Borland, who recounted dramatic UFO encounters at military si...</span></span></span>

**Conventional-target interpretation.** Analysts have proposed a balloon, drone, decoy or other lightweight object. A balloon can appear circular in infrared imagery, may show little obvious structure at long range and could remain airborne after a nearby explosion, particularly if the visible post-detonation object is a fragment or if the track was reacquired incorrectly. The Mutual UFO Network has published differing internal commentary, including a specialist analysis of the video and a later article describing a foreign surveillance balloon as the most likely broad explanation. These are civilian opinions rather than verified identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mufon.com/2025/10/08/the-hellfire-uap-strike-video-a-mufon-photo-team-analysis/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mufon.com">[MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on mufon.com.</span></span></span>

**Unresolved military incident.** The most defensible current label is simply an unidentified object encountered during active military operations. That classification leaves open mundane possibilities without claiming they have been proved. It also reflects the distinction between “unidentified” and “inexplicable”: an object may remain unidentified because essential evidence is withheld, missing or never collected.

The balance of publicly available evidence therefore supports **contested and unresolved**, not **confirmed extraordinary craft**. Claims that the object possessed alien technology, an impenetrable surface or the ability to violate known physics go beyond what the footage can establish.

## Official records and the transparency problem

The congressional hearing was an official proceeding, but showing a video in Congress did not authenticate every interpretation attached to it. The House committee framed the hearing principally around government transparency, reporting procedures and whistleblower protection. Its summary records calls for better disclosure but does not provide a completed technical finding that the Yemen object was anomalous or extraterrestrial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oversight.house.gov/release/luna-announces-hearing-on-transparency-relating-to-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oversight.house.gov">[House Oversight Committee]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oversight.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on house.gov.</span></span></span>

The Pentagon’s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, known as AARO, has repeatedly said that most cases it resolves turn out to involve balloons, birds, drones, satellites, aircraft or sensor effects. In November 2024, AARO’s director said the office had received more than 1,600 reports and had resolved hundreds as commonplace objects. Only a small proportion appeared potentially anomalous, often because resolving them required additional data and analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dod examining unidentified anomalous phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod examining unidentified anomalous phenomena</span></span></span>

No publicly available AARO case resolution specifically identifying the Yemen object was located. Nor is there a public release of the full sensor recording, platform positions, targeting logs, weapon settings or battle-damage assessment. This missing material is decisive. Proper analysis would require synchronised observations, known camera parameters, radar-derived range, accurate timing and an account of what military personnel believed they were engaging. Scientific UAP research similarly emphasises multimodal measurement—combining optical, infrared, radar, acoustic and environmental data—to distinguish unusual objects from sensor artefacts and ordinary aerial traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

## Regional variation inside Yemen

The available evidence is too weak to construct a meaningful province-by-province UFO map. There is no dependable public series showing that sightings cluster around Sana’a, Aden, Al Hudaydah, the highlands or the eastern desert. Apparent regional differences would largely reflect where cameras, military sensors, journalists and internet access happen to be concentrated.

Nevertheless, different settings create different identification problems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Red Sea and Gulf of Aden:** Shipping, naval helicopters, surveillance aircraft, combat drones, missiles and countermeasures produce a crowded and security-sensitive environment.
* **Western coastal areas:** Military operations around Al Hudaydah and maritime routes increase the chance that unusual lights or distant objects have a conflict-related origin.
* **Sana’a and other cities:** Air strikes, interceptors, flares, explosions and debris can generate brief luminous events that are difficult to interpret from phone footage.
* **Remote highlands and deserts:** Dark skies improve visibility of meteors, planets and satellites, but limited reference points make distance and speed especially hard to judge.

</div>

These are explanatory conditions, not documented UFO “hotspots”. Any future national chronology should separate civilian visual reports from military-sensor encounters and record the surrounding aviation and conflict activity at the exact time.

## Historical sky reports are not UFO cases

Yemen possesses an important historical record of unusual celestial observation, but it should not be folded indiscriminately into UFO lore. Medieval Yemeni chronicles describe the brilliant supernova of AD 1006. Modern researchers have argued that the Yemeni account may record an observation around 17 April, earlier than many other surviving reports, and that its descriptions of position, brightness and repeated visibility are astronomically consistent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02915" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

This is a useful cautionary example. A spectacular light can be mysterious to observers at the time yet later become securely identified through dates, celestial position and comparison with independent records. The SN 1006 account is valuable astronomical evidence, not evidence of an unidentified craft. Treating every historic “strange light” as a UFO strips away the very details that allow a reliable explanation.

**Relatively secure facts:** American congressional records confirm that the September 2025 hearing occurred and that footage associated with an incident off Yemen was publicly presented. ## The most credible conclusion

Yemen’s UFO record is not a rich national archive of repeated, corroborated encounters. It is a thin body of scattered claims dominated by one militarily significant but technically under-documented video. The 2024 object off Yemen deserves careful examination because an armed drone apparently attempted to engage something in active airspace. Its importance lies primarily in air defence, identification procedures, classification and public accountability—not in demonstrated evidence of alien visitation.

Until the original recording and associated sensor and targeting data are released, the case should remain open but narrowly described: an unidentified airborne object, observed during conflict, subjected to an apparent near-interception or interception attempt, with insufficient public evidence to determine its nature.

    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
