## Introduction

The Boianai encounter of 26–27 June 1959 remains one of the most discussed UFO cases in [Papua New Guinea]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-papua-new/' | relative_url }}) because it combined unusually prolonged observations, numerous witnesses and detailed contemporary notes. At the Anglican mission at Boianai in what is now Milne Bay Province, the Reverend William Booth Gill and mission staff reported watching luminous aerial objects over two consecutive evenings. They also claimed to see human-like figures on or near the main object and believed some of those figures responded to hand and torch signals from the ground. Despite the extraordinary nature of these claims, no photographs, radar records, physical traces or independently verified instrumental evidence were obtained. As a result, the case occupies an unusual position: it is widely regarded as one of the strongest multiple-witness UFO reports, yet it remains unproven as evidence of an extraterrestrial craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A703_554-1-30_Part%201_637518.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A703 554 1 30 Part 1 637518</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsREPORT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSBoianai, Territory of Papua and New Guinea, 1959By W.B. ## The events of 26 and 27 June

William Gill was an Australian Anglican missionary stationed at Boianai. Before the famous observations, he had already become aware of reports of unusual lights in the surrounding region and had himself seen unexplained lights earlier in 1959. Importantly, his correspondence before the main encounter shows a cautious attitude. He expressed interest in the reports but also argued that natural [explanations]({{ 'explanations-9f18eb/' | relative_url }}) should be considered before extraordinary conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/father-gill-1959-papua-new-guinea-ufo-sighting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">father gill 1959 papua new guinea ufo sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Father Gill &amp; the 1959 Papua New Guinea UFO Sighting - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

On the evening of 26 June, Gill noticed a bright object above the sea shortly before sunset. He began making notes as events unfolded rather than relying entirely on memory afterwards. As additional mission residents gathered, witnesses described a large luminous object apparently hovering at a relatively fixed position while smaller lights manoeuvred nearby.

The observation continued for several hours. Cloud periodically obscured the object, but the witnesses reported seeing it repeatedly reappear. Gill estimated that what looked like figures became visible on the upper part of the object. According to his notes, these figures appeared to move about as though engaged in purposeful activity rather than remaining stationary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A703_554-1-30_Part%201_637518.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A703 554 1 30 Part 1 637518</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsREPORT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSBoianai, Territory of Papua and New Guinea, 1959By W.B. The following evening, 27 June, similar activity was reported. During this second observation, Gill and others claimed they deliberately attempted visual communication. Several witnesses waved towards the object. Gill later wrote that one or more apparent figures seemed to wave back. At another point, a torch was flashed towards the object, after which witnesses believed the object responded by rocking or altering its apparent position. These signalling episodes became the most famous element of the entire case because they suggested interaction rather than simple observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A703_554-1-30_Part%201_637518.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A703 554 1 30 Part 1 637518</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsREPORT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSBoianai, Territory of Papua and New Guinea, 1959By W.B. ## What Gill and the other witnesses reported

The Boianai reports contain several distinct categories of claims, not all of which carry the same evidential weight.

**The luminous object.** Nearly all versions of the account agree that a bright, unusually luminous object remained visible for an extended period. Witnesses consistently described it as brighter than ordinary stars and apparently stationary for long intervals before making movements that they considered unusual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A703_554-1-30_Part%201_637518.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A703 554 1 30 Part 1 637518</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsREPORT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSBoianai, Territory of Papua and New Guinea, 1959By W.B. **Smaller accompanying objects.** Gill recorded additional smaller lights approaching and departing from the principal object. These were sometimes described as moving rapidly before apparently merging with or separating from the larger object. Because these observations occurred at night without distance references, their actual size and motion cannot be independently determined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A703_554-1-30_Part%201_637518.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A703 554 1 30 Part 1 637518</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsREPORT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSBoianai, Territory of Papua and New Guinea, 1959By W.B. **Human-like figures.** The most controversial claim concerns the apparent occupants. Gill described seeing several dark, human-shaped forms on what appeared to be the top of the main object. He believed they were moving naturally rather than remaining fixed silhouettes. Other witnesses broadly agreed that figures were visible, although surviving individual witness statements vary in descriptive detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A703_554-1-30_Part%201_637518.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A703 554 1 30 Part 1 637518</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsREPORT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSBoianai, Territory of Papua and New Guinea, 1959By W.B. **Signalling behaviour.** The reported exchange of waves and torch signals has become central to later discussions of the case. Gill interpreted the apparent responses as deliberate acknowledgement from the figures. However, because the observations occurred at considerable distance under changing lighting conditions, alternative explanations—including coincidental movement or perceptual interpretation—cannot be excluded from the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A703_554-1-30_Part%201_637518.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A703 554 1 30 Part 1 637518</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsREPORT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSBoianai, Territory of Papua and New Guinea, 1959By W.B. ## How many witnesses were there?

The case is frequently described as involving either 25, 37 or 38 witnesses. These differing figures do not necessarily represent contradictory evidence.

People arrived and departed during the observations on both evenings, meaning the group was not static. Gill's formal report includes a signature list from 27 witnesses, while later publications often refer to approximately 37 mission residents who observed some portion of the events over the two evenings. Some modern summaries round this to 38. These variations largely reflect different counting methods rather than evidence that the number was deliberately inflated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A703_554-1-30_Part%201_637518.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A703 554 1 30 Part 1 637518</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsREPORT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTSBoianai, Territory of Papua and New Guinea, 1959By W.B. The witness group also differed in background and experience. It included Gill himself, mission teachers, assistants and local residents associated with the Anglican mission. While this diversity reduces the likelihood of a single individual inventing the account, it does not eliminate the possibility of shared misinterpretation during a prolonged group observation.

## Which claims remain independently unverified?

The Boianai encounter has survived decades of discussion because several elements appear unusually well documented for a UFO report. Nevertheless, its strongest claims remain independently unverified.

## Why investigators reached different conclusions

Supporters of the Boianai case point to several unusual strengths.

Within Papua New Guinea's UFO history, the Boianai encounter stands out because of the combination of prolonged observation, multiple contemporaneous witnesses and detailed written documentation. Those features make it considerably stronger than many isolated anecdotal reports.
