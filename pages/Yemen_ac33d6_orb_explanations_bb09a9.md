## Introduction

The publicly available footage of the alleged October 2024 [Yemen]({{ 'what-do-we-really-know-about-yemens-ufos/' | relative_url }}) "orb" does not demonstrate that an extraordinary craft was present. Instead, the available evidence supports a more cautious conclusion: several conventional explanations remain plausible, but none has been conclusively verified. Because the released clip lacks the original sensor data, complete telemetry, engagement logs, radar information and an official technical assessment, it is impossible to determine the object's identity with confidence. That uncertainty cuts both ways. It prevents confirmation of exotic claims while also preventing a definitive ruling in favour of any single mundane explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/uap-hearing-new-video-yemen-orb.14427/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Hearing New Video</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Hearing New Video - Yemen Orb &#124; MetabunkSeptember 9, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2025</span></span></span>

Among the competing hypotheses, lightweight balloons, small drones, decoys and missile-related debris fit important aspects of the footage without requiring unknown technology. Their strengths and weaknesses depend largely on how the infrared imagery is interpreted and on whether the apparent motion reflects the object itself or the geometry of the observing aircraft.

## Why balloons and lightweight targets remain plausible

A balloon is often dismissed because viewers assume the object appears too stable or survives what looks like a missile impact. However, that conclusion depends on interpreting a short infrared clip as a literal record of physical motion rather than a compressed view through a moving sensor.

Several factors make a balloon or similarly lightweight airborne object a reasonable candidate:

* **The operating environment:** Waters off Yemen were an active military theatre in late 2024, with surveillance aircraft, drones, decoys and airborne objects of various types present in the region.
* **Small radar or infrared signature:** Weather balloons, commercial balloons or lightweight surveillance devices can produce simple bright infrared returns depending on temperature contrast and sensor settings.
* **Limited manoeuvring evidence:** The released video does not clearly show controlled acceleration, abrupt directional changes or other movements that would require advanced propulsion.
* **Apparent persistence after impact:** If the missile misses, passes behind, detonates nearby or strikes only part of a lightweight target, surviving fragments may continue drifting while appearing to remain associated with the original object because of viewing geometry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/uap-hearing-new-video-yemen-orb.14427/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Hearing New Video</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Hearing New Video - Yemen Orb &#124; MetabunkSeptember 9, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2025</span></span></span>

## How parallax and tracking errors alter appearances

The strongest ordinary explanation focuses less on the object itself than on how airborne infrared systems record moving targets.

Parallax occurs when the observing platform moves while tracking a distant object. An MQ-9 Reaper is itself flying at considerable speed. If the camera continually slews to keep a target centred, distant water, clouds and the target can appear to move relative to one another in ways that do not directly represent the target's true flight path.

In practical terms, this can create several misleading impressions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a nearly stationary object can appear to travel rapidly;
* debris from an explosion can seem to remain attached to the original target;
* the apparent speed of an object may increase or decrease without any corresponding change in its real velocity;
* a missile can appear to "bounce" if the viewing angle changes rapidly immediately after the engagement.

</div>

Video analysts examining the available footage have argued that much of the dramatic appearance can be reproduced through camera geometry and changing relative motion between the drone, missile and target. They point in particular to changes in apparent angular velocity after the interaction, consistent with parallax rather than an indestructible object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/uap-hearing-new-video-yemen-orb.14427/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Hearing New Video</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Hearing New Video - Yemen Orb &#124; MetabunkSeptember 9, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2025</span></span></span>

Infrared imagery also compresses three-dimensional events into a two-dimensional image. Without calibrated range information, complete telemetry and the original sensor files, viewers cannot accurately determine true distance, size or speed from the public clip alone. Professional thermal imaging systems are powerful tools, but interpreting their imagery requires the associated metadata that has not been released publicly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flir.com/en-gb/instruments/science/high-speed-ir/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flir.com">[FLIR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">High Speed IR &#124; Flir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">High Speed IR &#124; Flir...</span></span></span>

## Could it have been a drone?

A small drone represents another conventional possibility, although it has its own limitations.

Military operations around Yemen routinely involve unmanned aerial vehicles of multiple sizes and capabilities. Small reconnaissance drones, loitering munitions and improvised airborne systems have all been documented in the wider regional conflict. An unidentified drone therefore cannot be excluded simply because the object appeared unusual.

However, the public footage does not clearly demonstrate features normally associated with powered drones, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* identifiable propulsion;
* sustained manoeuvres against the wind;
* repeated controlled course changes;
* recognisable airframe structure.

</div>

If the object were a very small drone viewed at long range through infrared optics, many structural details could disappear into a single bright point. Conversely, the absence of visible drone characteristics means the video alone cannot establish that explanation either. The available imagery is simply too limited for positive identification.

## Could the apparent "orb" actually be debris?

Another possibility is that the object seen after the apparent engagement is not the original target at all.

Several scenarios have been proposed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* fragments from the missile continuing on a ballistic path;
* pieces of a lightweight airborne object surviving partial damage;
* optical confusion caused by overlapping debris fields viewed from a moving platform;
* thermal blooming, where intense heat temporarily alters the appearance of nearby objects in infrared imagery.

</div>

These explanations are difficult to distinguish because the publicly released clip is short and lacks frame-by-frame calibrated data. Some analysts argue that the object visible after the flash follows a trajectory more consistent with drifting debris than with an intact craft executing controlled flight. Others disagree over exactly which fragment is being tracked. The disagreement itself illustrates how much interpretation depends on missing technical information rather than on clear visual evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/uap-hearing-new-video-yemen-orb.14427/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Hearing New Video</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Hearing New Video - Yemen Orb &#124; MetabunkSeptember 9, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2025</span></span></span>

## Which observations still resist confident identification?

Although conventional explanations remain viable, they are not proven.

Several unresolved questions prevent a definitive conclusion:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* The original sensor recording has not been released in full.
* Public viewers do not have complete range, altitude or target-tracking data.
* No official forensic reconstruction of the engagement has been published.
* Independent analysts must estimate geometry from partially visible on-screen information rather than complete telemetry.
* No confirmed recovery of wreckage or physical evidence has entered the public record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/3d-analysis-of-the-yemen-orb.14446/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">3D Analysis of the Yemen Orb &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">3D Analysis of the Yemen Orb &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

</div>

These gaps mean that neither sceptical nor extraordinary interpretations can claim decisive support. Claims that the missile physically "bounced off" an unknown craft are stronger than the evidence presently allows, while equally confident assertions that the object was definitely a balloon also go beyond what has been publicly demonstrated.

## The most evidence-supported assessment

Based on the material currently available, the balance of evidence favours caution rather than certainty. Balloon, lightweight drone, decoy and debris scenarios all remain physically plausible because they require fewer assumptions than the existence of technology displaying confirmed physics-defying behaviour. At the same time, none of these explanations has been publicly verified through official sensor data or recovered material.

Accordingly, the Yemen incident remains best described as an unresolved identification problem rather than evidence of extraordinary technology.
