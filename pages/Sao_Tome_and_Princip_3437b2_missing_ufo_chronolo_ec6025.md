## Introduction

Why is São Tomé and Príncipe's national UFO timeline almost empty? The simplest answer is that no publicly documented sequence of UFO incidents currently meets the basic standards required for a credible historical chronology. Unlike countries with extensive military files, long-running civilian reporting organisations or well-preserved newspaper archives, São Tomé and Príncipe has no established chain of dated, documented and independently verifiable UFO cases in the accessible public record. Searches across major reporting databases, declassified-record collections and international UFO archives produce either no country-specific cases or only fragmentary references that cannot be traced back to original evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[The National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Why the national timeline remains largely empty

The absence of a recognised chronology should not be interpreted as evidence that unusual lights have never been seen over the islands. Instead, several practical factors help explain why no verified sequence has emerged.

First, the country has a very small population spread across two islands. Even if unexplained observations occur occasionally, the absolute number of potential witnesses is much lower than in larger countries.

Second, there is no publicly visible long-running national UFO organisation that systematically collected reports over decades. In countries with established chronologies, civilian research groups often preserved witness statements that governments ignored. No comparable archive has become publicly available for São Tomé and Príncipe.

Third, no accessible collection of military or civil aviation records has surfaced showing sustained official interest in unidentified aerial sightings within the country's airspace.

Finally, local newspaper archives and historical reporting are comparatively limited and less likely to have been digitised or incorporated into international databases, making retrospective research significantly more difficult.

Taken together, these factors produce a situation where isolated claims may exist but fail to accumulate into a documented national chronology.

## The fragmentary sightings and database traces

Although a complete chronology is absent, a handful of scattered references appear during searches. None currently satisfies the evidential standard required for inclusion as a nationally significant historical event.

### Country entries without documented cases

Some worldwide UFO directories include São Tomé and Príncipe simply because they aim to list every country. However, inclusion in an index is not the same as documenting an investigated incident.

Several international catalogues contain a country page while providing no underlying witness statements, photographs, official documents or investigative conclusions for São Tomé and Príncipe itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/databank/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[National UFO Reporting Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National UFO Reporting Center Data Bank &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National UFO Reporting CenterData Bank &#124; NUFORC - Latest UFO Sightings...</span></span></span>

### Isolated database records

Secondary datasets derived from public UFO reporting collections occasionally appear to contain a single country-coded entry for São Tomé and Príncipe.

By themselves, these records have limited historical value because they often omit the original report, reporting chain, precise coordinates, witness identity and source provenance. Without those elements, researchers cannot determine whether the observation was accurately located, duplicated from another dataset or even correctly assigned to the country.

Such entries may serve as leads for future investigation, but they do not establish a verified national chronology.

### No identifiable sequence

Perhaps the most significant observation is what cannot be found.

There is no publicly documented progression from an early landmark sighting to later investigations, no recognised wave of reports in a particular decade, and no officially investigated incident that became part of the country's historical record. This distinguishes São Tomé and Príncipe from nations where researchers can reconstruct decades of documented cases from preserved archives.

## Why unverified claims should stay off the timeline

Sparse evidence creates a temptation to lower historical standards. Doing so would produce an unreliable chronology rather than a useful one.

Several recurring problems deserve particular caution.

**Geographical confusion.** Internet searches frequently return material relating to São Thomé das Letras in Brazil, a town famous in UFO folklore. Despite the similarity in spelling, these reports concern Brazil rather than the African island state and cannot legitimately be incorporated into São Tomé and Príncipe's history.

**Undated stories.** Claims that cannot be tied to a year, location or identifiable witness cannot be independently evaluated.

**Repeated secondary citations.** Some internet lists recycle the same brief entry without adding any primary documentation. Multiple repetitions of an unsupported claim do not increase its historical reliability.

**Missing investigative record.** Without evidence that police, aviation authorities, journalists or other contemporary observers documented an event, later retellings remain difficult to verify.

For these reasons, the most responsible approach is to classify unsupported reports as unconfirmed rather than allowing them to become accepted milestones in a national chronology.

Evidence capable of expanding São Tomé and Príncipe's documented UFO history could include:

## The most accurate chronology available today

Based on the accessible documentary record, the national chronology is remarkably short:
