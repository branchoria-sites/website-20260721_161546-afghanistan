## Introduction

The reported “floating city” over Dulali village in Bauchi State remains [Nigeria]({{ 'what-really-lies-behind-nigerias-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s best-known UFO-related case because it combines numerous eyewitness accounts with an almost complete absence of evidence that can now be independently tested. According to accounts first published by Nigerian journalists in 2012, villagers described a huge, slow-moving, cloud-like object that appeared to contain buildings, roads and vehicles, accompanied by a mechanical sound. Some witnesses also claimed the phenomenon returned roughly two weeks later. Despite the striking descriptions, no verified photographs, videos, meteorological [records]({{ 'records-ec9535/' | relative_url }}) or official investigative files have surfaced. As a result, the case remains unresolved—not because it provides strong evidence for an extraordinary craft, but because the surviving evidence consists almost entirely of human testimony that cannot now be corroborated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufointernationalproject.com/latest-news/floating-interdimensional-city-witnessed-by-hundreds-of-villagers-in-africa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufointernationalproject.com">[UFO International Project]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufointernationalproject.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO International ProjectFloating &#x27;Interdimensional City&#x27; Witnessed by Hundreds of Villagers in AfricaApril 20, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 20, 2015</span></span></span>

## What did witnesses report?

The reported event is generally dated to March 2011 in Dulali, a village in Lanzai South Ward of Darazo Local Government Area, Bauchi State. The incident was said to have occurred shortly after morning prayers, when many villagers were already outdoors, giving rise to claims that dozens or even hundreds of people observed the same phenomenon. The earliest widely cited published account appeared about a year later in Nigerian media before being repeated by international UFO websites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufointernationalproject.com/latest-news/floating-interdimensional-city-witnessed-by-hundreds-of-villagers-in-africa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufointernationalproject.com">[UFO International Project]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufointernationalproject.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO International ProjectFloating &#x27;Interdimensional City&#x27; Witnessed by Hundreds of Villagers in AfricaApril 20, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 20, 2015</span></span></span>

### The reported second appearance

One aspect that attracts particular interest is the claim that the object returned approximately two weeks after the first sighting.

If accurate, a repeat appearance would distinguish the Dulali incident from many one-off atmospheric observations. However, this claim rests entirely on later witness recollections. No independent reporting from the time of the alleged second event has been located, and there are no known photographs, weather observations or official reports documenting it separately. Consequently, the reported return remains part of the oral narrative rather than an independently verified event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufointernationalproject.com/latest-news/floating-interdimensional-city-witnessed-by-hundreds-of-villagers-in-africa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufointernationalproject.com">[UFO International Project]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufointernationalproject.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO International ProjectFloating &#x27;Interdimensional City&#x27; Witnessed by Hundreds of Villagers in AfricaApril 20, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 20, 2015</span></span></span>

## Why is the evidence so limited?

The Dulali case illustrates the difference between a widely witnessed event and a well-documented one.

Several factors limit what investigators can conclude.

**No contemporary imagery.** No authenticated photographs or video recordings from the sighting have entered the public record. Later illustrations circulating online are artistic impressions rather than documentary evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufointernationalproject.com/latest-news/floating-interdimensional-city-witnessed-by-hundreds-of-villagers-in-africa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufointernationalproject.com">[UFO International Project]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufointernationalproject.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO International ProjectFloating &#x27;Interdimensional City&#x27; Witnessed by Hundreds of Villagers in AfricaApril 20, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 20, 2015</span></span></span>

**No instrumental observations.** There are no publicly available radar records, [aviation]({{ 'aviation/' | relative_url }}) reports, astronomical observations or meteorological analyses tied directly to the reported time and location. Without such information, it is impossible to estimate altitude, size or speed objectively.

**No preserved official investigation.** Although local officials were reportedly aware of the event, there is no publicly available investigative report explaining what inquiries, if any, were undertaken or what conclusions were reached. Accounts suggesting that competing security concerns limited any investigation have never been supplemented by documentary records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufointernationalproject.com/latest-news/floating-interdimensional-city-witnessed-by-hundreds-of-villagers-in-africa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufointernationalproject.com">[UFO International Project]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufointernationalproject.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO International ProjectFloating &#x27;Interdimensional City&#x27; Witnessed by Hundreds of Villagers in AfricaApril 20, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 20, 2015</span></span></span>

**Dependence on a single reporting chain.** Much of the international coverage ultimately traces back to one Nigerian newspaper feature written after the event rather than multiple independent contemporaneous news reports. As stories were republished by UFO websites, many added speculation while introducing little genuinely new evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufointernationalproject.com/latest-news/floating-interdimensional-city-witnessed-by-hundreds-of-villagers-in-africa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufointernationalproject.com">[UFO International Project]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufointernationalproject.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO International ProjectFloating &#x27;Interdimensional City&#x27; Witnessed by Hundreds of Villagers in AfricaApril 20, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 20, 2015</span></span></span>

These limitations mean the case cannot be tested in the way that modern investigations analyse synchronized videos, radar tracks, satellite data or multiple independent sensor records.

## Could a mirage explain the reports?

The most frequently proposed conventional explanation is that witnesses experienced an unusual atmospheric optical phenomenon.

A superior mirage, sometimes called a **Fata Morgana**, occurs when layers of air at different temperatures bend light in unusual ways. Under the right conditions, distant landscapes or structures can appear elevated, distorted or apparently suspended above the horizon. Atmospheric scientists have used this mechanism to explain several famous "floating city" reports elsewhere in the world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/151020-city-sky-china-mirage-fata-morgana-weather-atmosphere?loggedin=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicChina&#x27;s Floating City and The Science of Mirages &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2015</span></span></span>

However, applying that explanation to Dulali is difficult because the necessary observational data are missing.

Several questions cannot now be answered:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Were suitable temperature inversions present that morning?
* What distant landscape could have produced the apparent image?
* Was the object actually near the horizon, or nearly overhead as some witnesses implied?
* Were all observers viewing the same optical geometry?

</div>

Without meteorological measurements or contemporary photographs, these questions remain unresolved.

It is also important to note that while "Fata Morgana" has become a popular label for floating-city stories, experts caution that not every report involving apparent structures in the sky necessarily fits that specific optical mechanism. Determining whether a superior mirage occurred requires detailed information about viewing angle, weather conditions and the surrounding landscape—information unavailable for Dulali.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/151020-city-sky-china-mirage-fata-morgana-weather-atmosphere?loggedin=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[nationalgeographic.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicChina&#x27;s Floating City and The Science of Mirages &#124; National GeographicOctober 20, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2015</span></span></span>

## Other possible explanations

Because the evidence is limited, several hypotheses remain conceivable, although none can be demonstrated.

**Cloud interpretation.** Humans naturally identify familiar patterns within complex cloud formations. Under unusual lighting conditions, layered clouds may resemble buildings or city skylines, especially when observers compare impressions with one another.

**Atmospheric optics beyond classic mirages.** Refraction, unusual illumination and layered cloud structures can occasionally generate striking visual effects that appear unfamiliar to observers.

**Misperception of distance and scale.** Without fixed reference points in the sky, observers often overestimate the size and underestimate the distance of unusual aerial phenomena.

**Extraordinary craft hypothesis.** Some UFO researchers interpret the testimony literally, arguing that witnesses described a genuine structured object unlike known aircraft. ## What the Dulali case demonstrates

The significance of the Dulali incident lies less in proving any particular explanation than in illustrating the strengths and weaknesses of eyewitness evidence.

The reported consistency among numerous witnesses suggests that many villagers sincerely believed they observed something highly unusual. Multiple observers reduce the likelihood that the event resulted from a single individual's mistake or fabrication. At the same time, large groups can still collectively misinterpret unfamiliar atmospheric phenomena, particularly when everyone observes the same ambiguous stimulus under similar viewing conditions.

Because no independent records survive, the case cannot move beyond that point. The available evidence neither confirms an extraordinary aerial craft nor identifies a conventional atmospheric phenomenon with confidence.

Within Nigeria's wider UFO record, Dulali therefore occupies an unusual position. It is arguably the country's most vivid mass sighting, yet it also demonstrates how quickly potentially important evidence can disappear when no photographs, sensor data or formal investigation are preserved.
