## Introduction

The 2022 Kyiv camera observations became one of the most widely discussed Ukrainian UFO cases because they appeared to come from professional astronomers rather than eyewitness testimony. Researchers from the Main Astronomical Observatory of the National Academy of Sciences of [Ukraine]({{ 'what-really-lies-behind-ukraines-ufo/' | relative_url }}) reported that specially configured meteor-monitoring cameras near Kyiv and Vinarivka had recorded numerous fast-moving unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP), including bright "Cosmics" and dark "Phantoms". Their calculations suggested that some objects were travelling at extraordinary speeds while remaining optically dark.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11215" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Unidentified aerial phenomena I. The controversy, however, centres less on whether unusual objects appeared in the camera footage than on how their distance, size and speed were inferred. Critics argue that relatively small errors in estimating range could transform apparently extraordinary objects into nearby insects, birds, debris or military projectiles. As a result, the Kyiv camera papers have become an important case study in how sensitive UAP analysis is to assumptions about distance rather than a confirmed demonstration of extraordinary technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01972" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv&quot;Down to Earth&quot; Limits on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in Ukraine (Comment on arXiv:2208.11215)October 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 5, 2022</span></span></span>

## What the observatory cameras recorded

The observations were made using meteor-detection stations installed in Kyiv and the village of Vinarivka south of the capital. Unlike ordinary surveillance cameras, these systems were configured to detect extremely fast-moving objects in daylight by using short exposures, high frame rates and specialised processing intended for meteor research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11215" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Unidentified aerial phenomena I. The researchers divided detections into two categories:

* **Cosmics** – bright objects appearing more luminous than the daytime sky.
* **Phantoms** – dark objects that appeared to absorb rather than reflect light, producing high contrast against the bright sky.

According to the first paper, the team observed solitary objects as well as groups or "squadrons", with apparent angular speeds between roughly 3 and 15 degrees per second. Using their colour-based analysis, they estimated that many Phantom objects were between 3 and 12 metres across, at distances of approximately 10–12 km, with velocities reaching around 15 km/s (about 54,000 km/h).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11215" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Unidentified aerial phenomena I. ## How distance and speed were calculated

The debate hinges almost entirely on geometry.

The Ukrainian team estimated distance primarily through colourimetric analysis. Their argument was that the apparent contrast between a dark object and the blue sky, produced through Rayleigh scattering in the atmosphere, could be used to estimate how far away the object was. Once a distance estimate was obtained, apparent angular motion recorded by the cameras could be converted into physical speed.

This approach means that several quantities depend on a single assumption:

* estimated distance;
* estimated physical size;
* calculated velocity.

If the distance estimate is substantially wrong, the inferred size and speed change proportionally. An object believed to be 10 km away would appear ten times smaller and ten times slower if it were actually only 1 km distant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11215" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Unidentified aerial phenomena I. Observations of events</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified aerial phenomena I. The first paper relied heavily on these inferred distances rather than direct geometric ranging through simultaneous observation from separated cameras. Although the project involved two meteor stations, critics argued that many of the published measurements were not derived from robust triangulation of the same target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.livescience.com/ukraine-uap-report-debunked" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livescience.com">[Live Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livescience.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Live Science Are there really UFOs flying over Ukraine? &#124; Live Science</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Live Science Are there really UFOs flying over Ukraine? &#124; Live Science</span></span></span>

## Why critics questioned the conclusions

The principal criticism came from two independent directions.

First, Abraham Loeb of Harvard University argued that objects travelling through the lower atmosphere at the reported speeds of up to 15 km/s should generate enormous aerodynamic heating. Such objects would not remain optically black; they should produce brilliant fireballs comparable to meteors or hypersonic re-entry events. He therefore suggested that the inferred distances were likely too large. Reducing those distances by roughly an order of magnitude produced estimated sizes and velocities much more consistent with artillery shells.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01972" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv&quot;Down to Earth&quot; Limits on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in Ukraine (Comment on arXiv:2208.11215)October 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 5, 2022</span></span></span>

Second, the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine itself criticised the work. A review concluded that the analysis contained significant methodological errors in determining object distances and that the interpretation failed to meet the expected scientific standard for publication. The Academy stated that the work should not be presented as an official scientific result of the institution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.livescience.com/ukraine-uap-report-debunked" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livescience.com">[Live Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livescience.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Live Science Are there really UFOs flying over Ukraine? &#124; Live Science</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Live Science Are there really UFOs flying over Ukraine? &#124; Live Science</span></span></span>

These criticisms did not claim that every recorded object had been identified. Instead, they argued that the extraordinary numerical estimates for speed and size were not sufficiently supported by the measurement method.

## Insects, projectiles and the near-camera problem

One of the most influential alternative explanations is known informally as the **near-camera problem**.

High-speed imaging systems frequently capture nearby objects crossing the field of view:

* insects;
* seeds;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* birds;
* airborne debris;
* raindrops;
* military fragments or projectiles.

</div>

Without an independent measurement of distance, a small nearby object can produce the same apparent angular motion as a much larger distant object.

For example, a fly passing close to the lens may cross the image extremely quickly while remaining sharply silhouetted against the sky. If software assumes that object is kilometres away rather than metres away, its inferred dimensions and velocity become vastly inflated. Similar reasoning applies to fragments of debris or artillery moving through wartime airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01972" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arxiv.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv&quot;Down to Earth&quot; Limits on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in Ukraine (Comment on arXiv:2208.11215)October 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 5, 2022</span></span></span>

This explanation also addresses another apparent paradox in the original report. Objects allegedly travelling at hypersonic speeds within the atmosphere would be expected to produce conspicuous heating, shock effects or luminous plasma. Nearby slow-moving objects require no such extraordinary behaviour because the extreme velocity disappears once the distance estimate changes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01972" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv&quot;Down to Earth&quot; Limits on Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in Ukraine (Comment on arXiv:2208.11215)October 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 5, 2022</span></span></span>

## What this case means within Ukraine's UAP record

Within Ukraine's broader catalogue of UFO and UAP reports, the Kyiv camera observations stand out because they rely on instrumental measurements rather than eyewitness testimony. That gives them scientific value even if many interpretations remain disputed.

The case also illustrates several broader lessons relevant to evaluating Ukrainian UAP claims:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Instrumental observations are generally stronger evidence than anecdotal sightings, but only if their calibration and geometry are robust.
* Extraordinary velocity estimates are highly sensitive to distance assumptions.
* Wartime airspace contains numerous conventional objects—including drones, artillery, missiles and debris—that complicate interpretation.
* Remaining "unidentified" does not imply an extraterrestrial explanation; it often reflects incomplete information or insufficient measurement precision.

</div>

For these reasons, the Kyiv camera papers are best understood as an unresolved observational dataset whose most extraordinary quantitative claims have faced substantial methodological criticism.
